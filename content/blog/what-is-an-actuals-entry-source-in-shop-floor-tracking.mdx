**An actuals entry source records where a logged actual value came from, so a planner can tell a figure a person typed from one the system estimated.** The three sources are User, entered by a planner or operator; system auto-filled from plan, back-filled by the engine when a downstream step is closed before an upstream one; and ERP import, pushed from an external system. It is the provenance stamp on every actual. It answers who produced the number, which is a different question from the entry mode, which answers whether hours or pieces were the primary input.

For the related but distinct format question, see [what is an actual entry mode](/blog/what-is-an-actual-entry-mode-in-shop-floor-tracking).

## How an Actuals Entry Source Works

Every logged actual, a day's hours or pieces on a step, carries a source alongside the value. The source is not the number itself; it is a label describing how the number came to exist.

The common case is User: a planner or operator typed the figure, for example seven and a half hours in the Log Actuals dialog. That is a measured value, entered by someone who was there.

The interesting case is system auto-filled from plan. Actuals are supposed to be logged in order, but the floor does not always oblige, and a later step sometimes gets closed before an earlier one has any actuals. To keep the sequence coherent, the engine can back-fill the earlier step's actuals from the plan. Those values are estimates, so they are stamped as system auto-filled rather than left to masquerade as typed figures.

The third source is ERP import: actuals pushed in from an external system. Labeling these separately lets a planner see that a figure arrived from a feed rather than being keyed at a kiosk.

## A Concrete Example

An operator finishes step three and closes it, but step two was never clocked in the system, the operator ran it earlier and forgot to log it. Step three's actuals now exist with nothing behind them for step two, and the sequence looks broken.

The engine back-fills step two's hours from the plan so the job's history is coherent again, and it stamps those hours with the system auto-filled source. In the Log Actuals grid, step two's days now show an auto-filled badge, the timesheet row marked estimated by the system, not typed by a person.

The planner reviews the badged rows against what really happened. When they correct step two's Tuesday figure by typing the real hours, the badge disappears on the spot, because the source flips to User the instant a person overwrites the estimate. The provenance stamp did its job: it flagged the estimate, prompted a review, and cleared itself once a human took ownership.

## How EDGEBIC Uses It

The actuals entry source is a field on each logged actual, and it drives how the record is presented and trusted:

- **User actuals** are values a planner or operator typed, the measured baseline of the record.
- **System auto-filled actuals** are back-filled from the plan when a downstream step is closed before an upstream one, and they surface as an auto-filled badge in the Log Actuals grid so planners know to review them. The badge clears live when the value is edited.
- **ERP import actuals** are values pushed from an external system, labeled so their external origin is always visible.

The source is provenance; the [actual entry mode](/blog/what-is-an-actual-entry-mode-in-shop-floor-tracking) is format, whether hours or pieces lead. Read them together for the full shape of a logged actual, and see [how to backfill actuals for earlier steps](/blog/how-to-backfill-actuals-for-earlier-steps-in-edgebic) for the workflow that produces the auto-filled source.
