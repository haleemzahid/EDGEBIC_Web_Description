**To reschedule only the jobs that changed in EDGEBIC, tick just those rows on the Drive Schedule tab and press Schedule + Re-Schedule, or press the Re-Schedule button after saving Gantt edits. EDGEBIC by User Solutions scopes the run to your selection, so every job you did not touch keeps its plan and its reserved capacity.** There is no mode to choose. The rows you tick, and the confirm dialog you approve, are the entire scope control.

This is the targeted-reschedule procedure. For the full mechanism, read [EDGEBIC rescheduling explained](/blog/edgebic-rescheduling-explained); for the everyday workflow, [how to run the scheduler in EDGEBIC](/blog/how-to-run-the-scheduler-in-edgebic) covers the basic pass. It is one of the task guides in the [EDGEBIC how-to hub](/blog/edgebic-how-to-hub).

## Before You Start

| Prerequisite | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| The jobs you changed are already scheduled | Targeted rescheduling replans existing jobs; a brand-new job is a first-time schedule instead. |
| Actuals are logged and current | Remaining steps resume from where the floor actually is, so log before you replan. |
| You know exactly which jobs changed | The scope is your selection. Tick the wrong rows and you replan the wrong work. |

## Reschedule Changed Jobs From the Drive Schedule Tab

1. Open the **Drive Schedule** tab. Every job sits on the grid with its **Status**, **Start Date**, **Item Start**, **Job End** and **Days Late**.
2. **Tick the Select checkbox** on each job you changed. Leave every other row unticked.
3. Press **Schedule + Re-Schedule**.
4. Read the **Confirm Scheduling** dialog. It states the split, for example "You are about to schedule 3 job(s): 0 new, 3 existing." That count is your last cheap check that the scope is right.
5. Answer **Yes** and watch the progress overlay. The grid is locked while the pass runs and unlocks when it finishes.

Do not answer **Yes** to the Schedule All Orders prompt unless you mean it. That prompt only appears when no rows are ticked, and answering yes pulls every order into the run.

## Reschedule Changed Jobs After a Gantt Edit

When the change was a manual drag rather than a data edit, use the dedicated button:

1. On the **Schedule View** or **Job View** tab, drag the bars you want to move and press **Save Changes** to record them.
2. Press **Re-Schedule**. It collects only the jobs you modified since the last run and replans exactly those.
3. Confirm the prompt, which warns that the selected jobs' schedules will be cleared and regenerated.

The Job View tab's Re-Schedule button does the same thing for the selected job's context. Both leave every other job, and its reserved capacity, exactly where it was.

## What Changes When You Run

A targeted pass touches a deliberately narrow surface:

| Surface | Effect |
|---|---|
| Selected jobs | Their not-started operations get new planned start and end times against current capacity. |
| Completed operations | Preserved verbatim. Recorded work is never moved, shrunk, or recomputed. |
| In-progress operations | The logged hours stay on their real days; only the remaining hours are replanned on the same work center. |
| Unselected jobs | Untouched. Their bars stay, and their reserved hours are respected by everything replanned around them. |
| Audit trail | A new reschedule event is added to each replanned job, with old dates next to new dates. |

The point of a targeted run is stability. On a loaded plant, the shop floor trusts a plan that does not churn, so replanning ten jobs when only three changed is a habit worth breaking.

## How to Check It Worked

Spend thirty seconds confirming the scope held:

1. Pick one job you did **not** select and compare its **Start Date** and **Job End** before and after. They must be identical.
2. Open a replanned job in **Job View** and read the hours header. **Actual Hours** must be unchanged; only planned timing should have moved.
3. Press **Job Audit** on a replanned job (or right-click a bar on Schedule View and choose **View Audit Trail**). The newest entry is your reschedule, with old and new dates side by side. Untouched jobs carry no new entry.

If a job you expected to move stayed nailed down, it is carrying a planned-start pin, a saved override, or an actual start. Those all beat a fresh reschedule by design.

## Common Mistakes and Gotchas

- **Answering yes to Schedule All Orders.** That prompt appears only when nothing is ticked. Answer no, tick your specific rows, and run again. The confirm dialog's job count is the safety net that catches this before anything writes.
- **Pressing Re-Schedule without saving Gantt drags first.** The button collects only jobs modified since the last run. Unsaved drags exist on your screen only, so it reports no modified jobs. Save, then Re-Schedule.
- **Rescheduling against stale actuals.** Remaining steps resume from the last logged position. If the floor has moved on but nobody logged it, the replan resumes from fiction. Log first, replan second.
- **Expecting a master-data edit to ripple on its own.** Editing a shift, holiday, routing, or capacity does not move any plan by itself. Make the change, tick the affected jobs, and run the targeted pass.
