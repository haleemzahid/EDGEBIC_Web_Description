**An operator pin hard-assigns a routing step to one specific named operator, narrowing the qualified pool for that step to exactly that person, with no silent substitution allowed.** Where requiring a skill says any certified person may do the work, a pin says only this person. If the pinned operator is unavailable, the step slides forward to the next window where that individual has free hours rather than being quietly handed to a colleague. A pin requires the step to also have a required skill, and it is the strongest labor control EDGEBIC offers.

## How an Operator Pin Works

Normal skilled scheduling widens a step to a pool: everyone who holds the required skill, is rostered, and is not on leave. A pin collapses that pool to a single named person. The engine still enforces every other rule, machine capacity, roster, time off, but the only operator it will consider for the step is the pinned one.

The defining behavior is what happens when that person is unavailable. Ordinary skilled steps degrade gracefully by picking another qualified operator. A pinned step refuses to. The rest of the pool is never a fallback, so the step cannot run until the pinned operator has hours again. It slides through their leave visibly instead of substituting someone else in the background.

A pin is durable. It is written to both the master routing step and the job's snapshot, so a reschedule re-applies the same pin rather than losing it. It is cleared only when the underlying required skill is removed from the step, which is the one action that dissolves the labor constraint a pin depends on.

## A Concrete Example

Only Joe touches the Inconel welds. The finishing standard on that product line is unforgiving, and the plant has decided, as a policy, that Joe personally runs those steps.

The planner sets Joe as the required operator on the Inconel welding steps, which already require the TIG-Inconel skill. The engine now staffs those steps with Joe and no one else. As long as Joe has hours, the plan looks ordinary. But if Joe is out Monday, the pinned step does not quietly move to another certified welder; it moves to Thursday, the next day Joe is back with free hours. The one-day-out becomes a visible slide on the board.

That visible slide is the whole value. The plant chose to depend on one person, and the schedule shows exactly what that dependency costs on the weeks it bites, rather than papering over it with a substitution nobody signed off on.

## How EDGEBIC Uses It

An operator pin is a field on the routing step, and it layers on top of the step's required skill. Its behavior threads through the engine and the plan:

- **The qualified pool** for a pinned step is exactly one person. The engine narrows to the pinned operator before it does any window search, so no substitution can slip in.
- **Availability still applies.** The pin does not bypass the roster, the operator's hours, or time off. It simply removes everyone but the pinned person from consideration, then schedules that person under the normal rules.
- **The pin persists across reschedules** because it is stored on both the global routing and the job snapshot, and it is cleared only when the required skill is removed from the step.

A pin is the narrow end of a spectrum whose wide end is [requiring a skill](/blog/what-is-a-skill-in-manufacturing-scheduling), where any qualified operator will do.
