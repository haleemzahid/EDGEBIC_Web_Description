**To schedule a work center in pieces instead of hours in EDGEBIC by User Solutions, give it a pieces capacity type and an output rate in pieces per hour, then set the piece requirement on the routing step that runs there.** The scheduler divides the pieces required by the effective rate to get the job's time, so an output-limited line is never promised a shorter duration than its physical rate allows.

Most work centers schedule fine by time. Pieces mode is for the machines whose real constraint is throughput, not clock hours: bottling lines, molders, packaging cells. This page is the setup for that. For when to pick pieces over hours in the first place, read [pieces-based vs hours-based capacity](/blog/pieces-based-vs-hours-based-capacity-edgebic).

## Before You Start

- **The work center already exists** ([how to add a work center](/blog/how-to-add-a-work-center-in-edgebic)).
- **You know the line's real output rate** in pieces per hour, and how close it runs to its rated rate.
- **You know the piece requirement per job,** which lives on the routing step.

## Step 1: Set the Capacity Type to Pieces

In the work center's configuration, set its **Capacity Type** to **Pieces**. This tells the scheduler to measure the machine by output rather than time. A third option, **Both**, is covered below and is often the safer choice.

## Step 2: Enter the Output Rate

Enter the base **Pieces Per Hour** for the line. This is the machine's throughput at full stride.

## Step 3: Set the Efficiency Factor

Enter an **Efficiency Factor** to reflect the real, sustained rate. Effective pieces per hour equals the base rate times this factor, and the factor is clamped between 0.1 and 2.0. A line rated at 120 that holds 108 has a factor of 0.9. Leave it at 1.0 for a line running at its rated rate.

Optionally set **Min Batch Size** and **Max Batch Size** if the line has batching constraints.

## Step 4: Set the Piece Requirement on the Routing Step

The pieces a job must make live on the routing step, not the work center. On the step that runs on this line, set its requirement to pieces and enter the pieces required. The scheduler then combines the two: pieces required from the step, divided by effective pieces per hour from the work center, gives the hours to book.

## Step 5: Save and Choose Between Pieces and Both

Save the work center. If the routing step also carries a fixed time, consider the **Both** capacity type instead of pure **Pieces**. Both computes the time from the routing hours and from the piece rate, then keeps whichever is longer, so an optimistic routing time can never under-promise a slow line.

## What Changes When You Save

| Surface | Effect |
|---|---|
| Job duration on this work center | Derived from pieces divided by effective rate, not a fixed time |
| **Both** model, if chosen | Uses the longer of the hours estimate and the piece-limited estimate |
| Existing schedules | Unchanged until you rerun |
| Next **Drive Schedule** run | Books the piece-limited hours for jobs on this line |

A worked case: a bottling line with a base rate of 120 and an efficiency factor of 0.9 has an effective rate of 108 bottles an hour. A 500-bottle job needs 500 divided by 108, about 4.63 hours. If the routing step claimed only 2.5 hours, the Both model keeps the longer 4.63 hours, and the line is scheduled honestly.

## How to Check It Worked

1. Place a test job with a known piece count and run **Drive Schedule**.
2. On the Gantt, check the job's duration matches pieces divided by effective rate, not the routing's fixed time.
3. For a Both work center, confirm the scheduler used the longer of the two estimates.
4. Record actual pieces as the job runs to compare planned against real output. See [how to record a piece count](/blog/how-to-record-a-piece-count-in-edgebic).

## Common Mistakes

**Leaving the output rate at zero.** A pieces work center with a zero rate cannot compute a finite time and schedules with unbounded duration. Always set a non-zero pieces-per-hour rate.

**Confusing the efficiency factor with lost time.** The factor rescales output rate. It models a slower line, not an idle one. For time lost to breaks or changeovers, use shift hours or a downtime event instead, covered in [how to schedule a downtime event for a machine](/blog/how-to-schedule-a-downtime-event-for-a-machine-in-edgebic).

**Forgetting the piece requirement on the step.** The work center holds the rate; the routing step holds the pieces to make. A pieces work center with no piece requirement on the step has nothing to schedule against.

**Using pure Pieces when the routing time is the real limit.** If some jobs are time-limited and some are output-limited, the Both model protects you either way by keeping the longer estimate.
