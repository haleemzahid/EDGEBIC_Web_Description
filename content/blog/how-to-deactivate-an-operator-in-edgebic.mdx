**To deactivate an operator in EDGEBIC by User Solutions, open the Operators tab under Daily Hours, select the person, uncheck Active, and save.** An inactive operator drops out of the scheduling engine's feed, so no new work is staffed to them on the next run, while their history, certifications, roster, and time off all stay intact. Deactivation is the reversible, history-preserving way to retire a person from scheduling.

Deactivating is not deleting. Deletion throws the record away and is guarded when a routing step is pinned to that person; deactivation keeps the audit trail and lets the operator come back. This page covers the clean retirement and the one cleanup step that prevents a scheduling problem afterward. To create an operator in the first place, see [how to add an operator](/blog/how-to-add-an-operator-in-edgebic).

## Before You Start

- **You know which steps are pinned to the operator.** A step hard-assigned to one named person has no fallback, so it needs a plan before the person leaves the feed.
- **You have a reassignment plan for pinned steps.** Another qualified operator, or removal of the pin, for any step that names this person specifically.
- **You want to keep the history.** If so, deactivate rather than delete, so past jobs the person was planned into stay readable.

## Step 1: Open the Operator

Open the **Daily Hours** tab and select the **Operators** inner tab. This is the operators manager, with the operator list on the left and detail sub-tabs for skills, roster, and time off on the right. Select the person you want to retire.

## Step 2: Uncheck Active

Uncheck **Active** on the operator. This marks the person out of service for scheduling. Their skills, roster rows, and time off are not deleted; they simply stop counting as available capacity.

## Step 3: Save

Save the operator. The person is now inactive: no longer part of the engine feed, so the next scheduling run staffs no new work to them, while every historical booking that named them stays intact and readable.

## Step 4: Clear Any Pinned Steps

Deactivating removes the person from the feed but does not rewrite routings. A step pinned to one named operator never substitutes anyone else, so with that operator inactive the step's qualified pool is empty and it will slide or fail loudly. Find any steps pinned to the person, reassign them to another qualified operator or remove the pin, then rerun **Drive Schedule**. Steps that only required a skill rather than a specific person are covered by the remaining certified, rostered operators, though work can slide if losing one person tightens capacity.

## What Changes When You Save

| Surface | Effect |
|---|---|
| The scheduling engine | Excludes the operator from the qualified pool on the next run |
| New work | No steps are staffed to the person going forward |
| History and certifications | Preserved and readable; past bookings still name the person |
| A step pinned to the operator | Has an empty pool until reassigned, so it slides or fails loudly |

Deactivating an operator moves no bars on its own. Work only re-staffs on the next scheduling run, which is when the person's absence from the pool takes effect.

## Deactivate Versus Delete

| Action | When to use | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| **Deactivate** (uncheck Active) | Someone who has left or is on indefinite leave | Reversible; history preserved; out of the feed |
| **Delete** | A record created in error and never used | Guarded while any routing step is pinned to the person; throws away history |

The delete guard is deliberate: it refuses to remove someone a pinned step still depends on, so you cannot orphan a routing by accident. For anyone who has done real work, deactivation is the correct path.

## Reactivating an Operator

Deactivation is reversible, which is much of why it beats deletion. If the person returns, open them, recheck **Active**, and save. Because their certifications, roster, and time off were never thrown away, they rejoin the qualified pool immediately and are schedulable again at the next run, with their past and new bookings on one continuous record. That makes deactivation the right tool for a seasonal layoff, a long leave, or a contractor between assignments, where a delete would force you to rebuild the whole record and lose the trail.

## Common Mistakes

- **Deactivating without clearing pinned steps.** A step hard-assigned to the person has no fallback and will slide or fail. Reassign or unpin those steps, then rerun.
- **Deleting instead of deactivating.** Deletion loses history and is guarded while the person is pinned to any step. For someone who ran real jobs, deactivate.
- **Confusing deactivation with time off.** For a vacation or training week, use time off so the person returns automatically. Deactivation is for an open-ended exit.
- **Expecting bars to vanish on save.** Deactivation stops new staffing; it does not move work already planned. Rerun to rebuild the schedule without the person.
