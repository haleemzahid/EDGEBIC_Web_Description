**SQDC is the lean mnemonic for a daily standup board, ordering the day's review by Safety, Quality, Delivery, and Cost, in that deliberate priority.** In EDGEBIC by User Solutions, the Standup dashboard builds four SQDC tiles, one per category, so a shift huddle reads the plant's state in the order lean intends: safety before quality, quality before delivery, delivery before cost. Where EDGEBIC cannot compute a tile honestly, it marks that tile Offline rather than showing a fabricated number.

This entry is part of the [EDGEBIC](/edgebic) glossary series. For the broader vocabulary, see the [manufacturing glossary](/blog/manufacturing-glossary). SQDC tiles carry a [data quality chip](/blog/what-is-a-data-quality-chip) that tells you whether each number is live, planned, or offline.

## How SQDC Works

The four letters are an order of precedence, not just a checklist. Safety comes first because no amount of output justifies harming a person. Quality comes second because shipping defects helps no customer. Delivery comes third. Cost comes last, not because it is unimportant, but because chasing cost ahead of the other three tends to break them. A standup board built to SQDC keeps the conversation in that sequence every day, so the team talks about people and product before it talks about money.

EDGEBIC maps each letter to a tile on the Standup dashboard:

| Category | What the tile shows | Chip |
|---|---|---|
| Safety | a dash, because no incident log exists yet | Offline |
| Quality | a dash, because no defect or scrap log exists yet | Offline |
| Delivery | all-time on-time-in-full from order end versus due date | Plan |
| Cost | cost variance: actual minus estimated over estimated | Live |

The honesty of the Offline marks is deliberate. The Safety and Quality tiles have no source entity to compute from, so they show a dash instead of a green number that would imply everything is fine. The design note here is pointed: an older dashboard once carried a tile labeled first pass yield that actually computed delivery, and the SQDC rebuild made every tile say plainly what it is, including admitting when it cannot measure yet.

## A Concrete Example

Take the Cost tile over a 30-day window, computed from completed orders by their completion day.

| Date | Estimated cost | Actual cost | Variance |
|---|---|---|---|
| Jun 1 | $10,000 | $9,800 | minus 2.0% |
| Jun 5 | $8,000 | $8,500 | plus 6.3% |
| Jun 8 | $12,000 | $11,900 | minus 0.8% |
| Jun 11 | $5,000 | $7,000 | plus 40% |
| Jun 13 | $6,000 | $6,000 | 0% |

The tile's headline value is the total variance:

- (43,200 actual minus 41,000 estimated) divided by 41,000 times 100 = **plus 5.4 percent**

So the month's completed jobs ran 5.4 percent over their estimated cost, with the pain concentrated on June 5 and June 11. The tile carries a Live chip because the figure comes from real completed-order costs, and its sparkline sits near zero most days with two visible spikes. That single number, read in its place after Safety, Quality, and Delivery, tells the standup where the cost leaks are without pretending the plant has no bigger priorities.
