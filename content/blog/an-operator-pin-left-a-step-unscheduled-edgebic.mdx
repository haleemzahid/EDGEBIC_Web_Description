**An operator pin narrows a step's qualified pool to exactly one named person and offers no fallback, so if that operator is not certified, rostered, and free during the step's window, the step has no one to run it and is left unscheduled.** [EDGEBIC by User Solutions](/edgebic) treats a pin as a hard assignment: the rest of the qualified pool is never a substitute for the pinned operator.

This post sits in the [EDGEBIC troubleshooting guide](/blog/edgebic-troubleshooting-guide) and is the companion to [how the operator pin pins a person to a job](/blog/how-the-operator-pin-pins-a-person-to-a-job) and the broader [job shop scheduling challenges](/blog/job-shop-scheduling-challenges).

## What You Are Seeing

You pinned a routing step to a specific operator, usually because only that person is trusted or certified for the work. After the run, the step did not schedule: the job stalls at that operation, slides far out, or fails to place at all, even though the work center itself has open capacity. The machine is free, but the step will not sit on it.

## Why It Happens

A pin changes who can staff the step, and it changes it completely.

- **The pool shrinks to one.** Normally the scheduler staffs a step from the qualified pool: every operator who is certified for the required skill, rostered on the shift for that day, and not on time-off, each contributing their unbooked hours. A pin replaces that whole pool with the single pinned operator.
- **There is no fallback.** The other qualified operators are not a backup for a pinned step. If the pinned operator cannot cover the step at the needed time, the step waits for them rather than borrowing anyone else.
- **Availability has three gates.** The pinned operator must be certified for the step's required skill (a pin requires a skill), rostered on a shift the work center runs on that day of week, and not on time-off across the window. Miss any one and the pool for that step is empty.

Because there is no substitute, an empty pool means the step has no one, so it does not place. The machine being free is irrelevant: capacity is not the constraint, the person is.

## How to Fix It

Work through the three availability gates for the pinned operator, or clear the pin.

1. **Check the certification.** Confirm the pinned operator holds an unexpired certification for the step's required skill. A pin needs a skill, and a lapsed certification empties the pool.
2. **Check the roster.** Rosters are keyed by shift and day of week. Make sure the operator is rostered on a shift the work center actually runs on the days the step needs, including nights or weekends if that is where the step lands.
3. **Check time-off.** Confirm the operator is not on time-off across the step's window. If they are out, the step will wait for their return.
4. **Or clear the pin.** If the person genuinely cannot cover the step, set the step back to Auto so the scheduler staffs it from the full qualified pool ([how to set an operator pin](/blog/how-to-set-an-operator-pin-in-edgebic) covers both directions).

## How to Prevent It

- **Pin only what truly needs one person.** A pin is right when the work genuinely requires a named, certified individual. For work several people can do, leave the step on Auto so the pool can cover it.
- **Roster the pinned operator for the shifts their steps run.** If you pin someone to work that lands on night shift, make sure they are rostered on night shift, because the roster is what makes them available there.
- **Keep certifications current.** An expired certification silently empties a pinned step's pool. Review certifications on the operators you pin most.
- **Clear pins that no longer apply.** When the reason for a pin ends (a person leaves, a qualification spreads to others), clear it so the step is not locked to someone who may become unavailable.

If the problem is not a pin but an operator with the right skill who still was not chosen, that is a different symptom covered in [an operator with the right skill was not used](/blog/an-operator-with-the-right-skill-was-not-used-edgebic).
