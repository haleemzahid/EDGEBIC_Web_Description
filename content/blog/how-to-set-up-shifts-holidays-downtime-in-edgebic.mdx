**Set up the plant calendar before anything else, because a work center with no working hours has zero capacity and will never receive a job.** The order that works is always the same: shifts first, holidays second, work centers third, and one-off exceptions handled as capacity overrides rather than calendar edits.

[EDGEBIC by User Solutions](/edgebic) keeps shifts and plant holidays together on one screen, which makes the whole plant clock a five-minute setup for a typical two-shift operation. This guide walks each step and flags the two entries that behave differently from how people expect. For the concepts behind the layers, read [shifts and calendars explained](/blog/edgebic-shifts-and-calendars-explained) first.

## Before You Start

| Prerequisite | Why |
|---|---|
| You can see the **Daily Hours** tab | Shifts and plant holidays both live there |
| You know the plant's real working pattern | The scheduler believes the calendar literally |
| At least one work center exists, or will soon | A shift only affects scheduling once a work center works under it |

## Step 1: Create a Shift

1. Open the **Daily Hours** tab, then the inner **Shift** tab. The **Shift Management** page lists existing shifts with their daily working hours and weekly total.
2. Click **Add Shift**. The **Shift Configuration** dialog opens.
3. Enter the **Shift Name**, for example `Day Shift`. Name it by when it runs, not by who works it: the name appears on every capacity screen and every allocation.
4. In the **Daily Working Hours** section, tick the checkbox on each day the shift runs, then set **Start:** and **End:** for that day. Each row shows the computed duration as you type.
5. Click **Save Shift**. The shift appears in the grid with its weekly **Total Hours**.

| Field | Required | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| **Shift Name** | Yes | Display name used everywhere the shift appears |
| Working-day checkbox (one per weekday) | n/a | Toggles whether the shift runs that day. Unchecked days contribute zero hours |
| **Start:** | Per working day | Time work begins that day |
| **End:** | Per working day | Time work ends. An end at or before the start is treated as running past midnight |
| **Reset All Days** | n/a | Clears every day to 00:00 so you can start over |

Two notes worth having in mind while you type.

**Overnight shifts need no special handling.** Enter `16:00` to `00:00` or `22:00` to `06:00` as they are. The scheduler handles the wrap and attributes the hours to the calendar day the shift starts on.

**Breaks are not a field here.** Either define the shift hours net of the break, or model a plant-wide break as a recurring partial holiday (step 3). If no job may span the break, define two shifts (`Morning` 08:00 to 12:00 and `Afternoon` 13:00 to 16:00) rather than one.

## Step 2: Attach Shifts to Work Centers

Shifts and work centers meet on the work center editor. For most of the plant there is nothing to do:

1. Open the **Workcenter** tab and click **Edit** on a work center row.
2. Select the **Associated Shifts** tab.
3. If **Use Global Shifts** is checked, the work center already inherits every plant-wide shift, including the one you just created. Nothing further is needed.
4. To give one machine its own calendar, untick **Use Global Shifts**, then use **Add Existing Shift** to copy a pattern locally, or **Create New Shift** for a machine-specific pattern.
5. Click **Save**.

The work center grid's **Shift Calendar** column shows which mode each machine is in: a green **Global** badge, or an amber **Custom** one. Prefer Global. Every custom calendar is another place to update when the plant pattern changes, and global shift edits do not reach it. The full work center side is in [how to set up work centers](/blog/how-to-set-up-work-centers-in-edgebic).

## Step 3: Add Plant Holidays

1. Open the **Daily Hours** tab, then the inner **Holiday** tab. The **Plant Holidays** page lists closures affecting all work centers and shifts.
2. Click **Add Holiday**.
3. Fill in the dialog:

| Field | Required | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| **Name** | Yes | Shown in the grid and used for audit |
| **Date** | Yes | The calendar day. For recurring holidays only the month and day matter |
| **Recurring each year** | No | Matches the same month and day every year |
| **Closes the whole day** | No | When checked, the whole day is blocked for every work center |
| **Start Time** / **End Time** | Partial only | The blocked window when the whole-day box is unchecked |
| **Description** | No | Free-text note |
| **Active** | No | Untick to keep the record but stop the scheduler honoring it |

4. Click **Save**. The grid shows **Scope** = **Whole Day**.

Use **Recurring each year** for anything on the public calendar. Entering one row per year works, but the year nobody remembers to add is the year the scheduler books straight through the holiday.

## Step 4: Add a Partial Holiday

A partial holiday blocks part of a day: an early release, a union assembly, a quarterly all-hands.

1. On the **Plant Holidays** page, click **Add Holiday**.
2. Enter a **Name** (for example `Quarterly All-Hands`) and the **Date**.
3. Untick **Closes the whole day**. The **Start Time** and **End Time** fields appear.
4. Enter the blocked window, for example `10:00` to `10:45`.
5. Click **Save**. The grid shows **Scope** = **Partial** and the window in the **Window** column.

The scheduler charges each shift only its own overlap:

```
blocked hours = max(0, min(shift end, holiday end) − max(shift start, holiday start))
```

| Shift | Window | Overlap | Result |
|---|---|---|---|
| Day | 08:00 to 16:00 | 0.75 h | 8 h becomes 7.25 h per machine |
| Night | 16:00 to 00:00 | none | unchanged |

On a two-instance work center that day shift offers 14.5 hours instead of 16, because the reduction applies before the instance multiplier. One record covers the whole plant and repeats automatically if you tick recurring.

## Step 5: Handle One-Off Days With Overrides

Approved Saturday overtime, a half-day PM window, a temporarily de-rated machine: none of these belong in the shift.

1. On the work center's row, click the **per-day capacity** button. You can also double-click a Total Capacity cell in **Schedule > Resource Calendar**.
2. Select the shift and adjust the date range.
3. Find the day in the grid and type the hours in the **Capacity (h)** column.
4. Add a **Reason**, for example `Overtime approved, rush order`.
5. The change saves as you edit and the **Overridden** column ticks. Click **Reset** on the row to undo it.

Three rules make overrides behave:

- **Enter the day total, not per-machine hours.** Three instances at four hours each is a **Capacity (h)** of 12. The engine uses the number exactly as typed.
- **Overrides are per shift.** A work center running day and night needs both overridden to change the whole day.
- **A per-day override beats a date-range override** on any date both cover.

For a longer period, a date-range override takes a total hours budget and spreads it across the available days and shifts in the window: 60 machine-hours across a retooling week is one record instead of five edits.

## Step 6: Model the Awkward Cases

Three patterns come up in almost every implementation and none of them has an obvious field.

**A lunch break.** Either define the shift hours net of the break (08:00 to 16:00 with a working lunch), or create a recurring partial holiday for the break window so the scheduler subtracts it from every overlapping shift. If no job may span the break, define two shifts instead: `Morning` 08:00 to 12:00 and `Afternoon` 13:00 to 16:00. The two-shift version is the only one that hard-splits an operation.

**A machine down for one day.** Use a per-day capacity override set to zero on the affected work center and shift, with a reason. That blacks out the cell, keeps the plant calendar untouched, and documents itself.

**Retiring a shift.** Untick the shift's active flag rather than deleting it. Schedules already generated keep their dates either way, but the next run will have no capacity from that shift, so the plan will move. Check which work centers use it before you retire it, and clean up their shift lists afterwards: a work center still referencing a retired shift keeps the association and loses the hours, which reads as a mysterious capacity drop.

## Step 7: Run the Schedule

Nothing you have done so far has moved a single job. Saving a shift or a holiday changes only the capacity calendar. Existing schedules keep their dates, including allocations that may now sit outside working hours.

Run **Drive Schedule** and review the result before communicating dates. Completed work is never moved by a reschedule, so rerunning is safe on jobs already in progress. The run itself is described in [the scheduling engine guide](/blog/edgebic-scheduling-engine-guide).

Here is what the rerun is worth. A 20-hour milling job on a single-instance work center, day shift only, starting Monday morning:

| Shift length | Monday | Tuesday | Wednesday | Finishes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 8 h/day | 8 | 8 | 4 | Wednesday 12:00 |
| 10 h/day | 10 | 10 | 0 | Tuesday 18:00 |

A full day earlier, with nothing changed but the calendar.

## A Worked Setup, Start to Finish

A two-shift plant setting up from scratch:

1. **Day Shift**: Monday to Friday, 08:00 to 16:00. Total Hours 40.
2. **Night Shift**: Monday to Friday, 16:00 to 00:00. Total Hours 40.
3. Every work center keeps **Use Global Shifts** checked, so all six show the **Global** badge and work 16 hours a day per instance.
4. **Independence Day**: whole day, recurring each year.
5. **Quarterly All-Hands**: partial, 10:00 to 10:45.
6. Run Drive Schedule. The engine skips the holiday entirely, charges 7.25 hours instead of 8 on the all-hands day, and the Gantt shows jobs flowing around both with no manual intervention.
