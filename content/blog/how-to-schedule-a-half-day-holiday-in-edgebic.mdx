**To schedule a half-day holiday in EDGEBIC, add a plant holiday and untick Closes the whole day.** **Start Time** and **End Time** fields appear, you enter the blocked window, and each shift loses only the hours that overlap it. The grid marks the record **Scope** = **Partial** and shows the window.

This is the right tool for an early release, a plant-wide meeting, a union assembly, or a shortened day before a public holiday. It is also the cleanest way to document a recurring plant break. For the whole-day case, read [how to add a plant holiday](/blog/how-to-add-a-plant-holiday-in-edgebic).

## Before You Start

- **You know the exact window**, not just "the afternoon". The scheduler works in times.
- **You know which shifts the closure genuinely affects.** Only overlapping hours are removed.
- **The closure applies to the whole plant.** A single machine's half-day is a per-day capacity override instead.
- **You can rerun the schedule afterward.**

## Step 1: Open the Plant Holidays Page

Open the **Daily Hours** tab, then select the inner **Holiday** tab. The **Plant Holidays** page opens.

## Step 2: Click Add Holiday and Name It

Click **Add Holiday**. In the **Holiday** dialog, enter a **Name** that says what happened: `Quarterly All-Hands`, `Christmas Eve early release`, `Safety stand-down`. Enter the **Date**.

## Step 3: Untick Closes the Whole Day

Untick **Closes the whole day**. The **Start Time** and **End Time** fields appear.

## Step 4: Enter the Blocked Window

Type the window the plant is genuinely not producing. For a 45-minute all-hands: **Start Time** `10:00`, **End Time** `10:45`.

Be literal. If the meeting runs 10:00 to 10:45 but the line does not restart until 11:00, enter 10:00 to 11:00. The scheduler charges exactly what you enter.

## Step 5: Save

Click **Save**. The grid shows **Scope** = **Partial** and the **Window** column displays the time range.

## How the Scheduler Counts It

The subtracted amount is the overlap between the holiday window and each shift's window, computed per shift and per work center. In plain terms: take the later of the two start times, take the earlier of the two end times, subtract, and floor the result at zero. No overlap means no reduction.

Worked through with **Day Shift** 08:00 to 16:00 (8 hours) and an all-hands at 10:00 to 10:45:

| Shift | Shift window | Overlap | Hours available per instance |
|---|---|---|---|
| Day Shift | 08:00–16:00 | 10:45 − 10:00 = **0.75 h** | 8 − 0.75 = **7.25 h** |
| Night Shift | 16:00–00:00 | **0 h** | 8 h, unchanged |

A work center with 2 instances on Day Shift therefore offers 7.25 × 2 = **14.5 hours** on that date instead of 16.

Two things follow from this. First, the reduction is applied **before** the instance and utilization multipliers, so a partial holiday scales with the size of the work center, the same way scheduled downtime does. Second, a night crew that never attends the morning meeting is never charged for it. That is why partial holidays are configured once, plant-wide, rather than machine by machine. The full priority chain is in [how EDGEBIC calculates work center capacity](/blog/how-edgebic-calculates-work-center-capacity).

## What Changes When You Save

| Surface | Effect |
|---|---|
| **Plant Holidays** grid | Shows **Scope** = **Partial** with the **Window** immediately |
| Overlapping shifts on that date | Lose exactly their overlap, per instance |
| Non-overlapping shifts | Unaffected |
| Resource Calendar and dashboards | Show the reduced day when refreshed |
| Existing schedules | **Unchanged** until **Drive Schedule** runs |

## How to Check It Worked

1. Confirm the row on the **Plant Holidays** grid reads **Scope** = **Partial** with the correct **Window**.
2. Open the Resource Calendar and read a work center's total capacity on that date. Do the arithmetic yourself first: shift hours minus overlap, times instances. If the screen and your arithmetic agree, the record is right.
3. Run **Drive Schedule**.
4. Check the Gantt. The affected date should show a shorter total of allocated hours, and downstream steps should have shifted by the difference.

## Common Mistakes

**Using a partial holiday to shave routine losses.** A partial holiday is a dated event with a name. If the plant reliably loses time every day to changeover drift or cleanup, that belongs in honest shift hours, not in a holiday record you have to recreate every week. Better to enter the shift net of the loss.

**Expecting a partial holiday to hit every shift.** It hits only what it overlaps, and that is by design. If a closure genuinely applies to both crews, enter two records, one per window.

**Forgetting the reduction happens before the multipliers.** On a four-instance work center, a one-hour partial holiday removes four hours of capacity, not one. That is usually correct (every operator is in the room), but it surprises planners the first time.

**Adding it and not rerunning.** Same as every other calendar change. The plan on screen keeps the old numbers until Drive Schedule runs, which is a recurring theme in our [shift and calendar mistakes](/blog/shift-and-calendar-mistakes-edgebic) list.
