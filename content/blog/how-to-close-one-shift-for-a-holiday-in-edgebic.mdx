**To close one shift for a holiday in EDGEBIC by User Solutions, open the Shifts tab, select the shift, and add a holiday on its Holidays tab.** Enter the date, a name, and check Is Active. That closes only that shift on that date, so a night crew can take a day off while the day shift keeps running. This is different from a plant holiday, which closes every work center on every shift.

EDGEBIC has three layers of closure for exactly this reason: the plant, the shift, and the individual machine. Choosing the right layer is what lets you send one crew home without stopping the whole factory.

## Before You Start

- **You know which shift is off and on which date.**
- **You know this is not a plant holiday.** A plant holiday would close every shift.
- **You can rerun the scheduler** so existing plans reflect the closure.

## Step 1: Open the Shift

Open the **Shifts** tab and select the shift you want to close, for example the night shift.

## Step 2: Go to the Shift's Holidays Tab

Click **Edit** and open the shift's **Holidays** tab. This holiday list belongs to the shift, which is what makes the closure shift-specific rather than plant-wide.

## Step 3: Add the Holiday

Click **Add Holiday**. Enter the **Date**, a **Name** that explains the closure for the audit trail such as `Festival day, night crew off`, and check **Is Active**. Save.

The shift now contributes zero capacity on that date. Every other shift is untouched.

## Step 4: Reschedule

Run **Schedule / Re-Schedule** so any work that was planned for that shift on that date moves to available capacity. Adding the holiday changes the calendar but does not move existing bars on its own.

## What Changes When You Save

| Surface | Effect |
|---|---|
| The chosen shift on that date | Zero capacity |
| Other shifts | **Unchanged**; they keep running |
| Existing schedules | Not moved until you rerun |
| Capacity resolution | The shift closure is one of three layers checked together: plant, shift, machine |

## How to Check It Worked

1. Reopen the shift's Holidays tab and confirm the holiday is listed and active for the right date.
2. Confirm the other shifts show normal capacity on that date.
3. Rerun the scheduler and confirm night work that was planned for that date has moved off the closed shift.

## Common Mistakes

**Using a plant holiday by accident.** A plant holiday closes every shift and every work center. If you only mean one crew, use a shift holiday on the shift instead.

**Forgetting to rerun.** The calendar shows the shift closed, but existing bars stay put until a reschedule. Rerun to move the work.

**Confusing shift holiday with machine holiday.** A shift holiday closes a crew's shift; a machine holiday takes one work center out. Pick the layer that matches what is actually off.
