**There is no downtime events screen on a work center in the current release of EDGEBIC by User Solutions.** Downtime is part of how the engine computes a day's capacity, but the way you actually take hours out of a machine's day is a per-day capacity override, or a partial plant holiday when the loss hits the whole site.

If you came looking for a downtime tab, this page saves you the search and covers the two routes that work today, including the honest cost of modeling a standing weekly window.

## Before You Start

- **The work center and its shift exist** ([how to add a work center](/blog/how-to-add-a-work-center-in-edgebic)).
- **You know the window:** which dates, which shift, and how many hours are genuinely lost.
- **You know how wide the loss is.** One machine and the whole plant take different routes.

## Route A: One Machine Loses Part of a Day

This is the common case and the per-day capacity override is the tool.

1. Open the **Workcenter** tab and select the machine.
2. Open its per-day capacity dialog.
3. Select the **shift** the loss falls on and find the **date**.
4. Enter the **capacity** as the hours the machine can genuinely offer: an eight-hour shift losing two hours becomes `6`, and a machine out all day becomes `0`.
5. Add a **reason**, such as "weekly line clean" or "bearing replacement".
6. Repeat for every affected date, and for every shift if the machine runs more than one.

The step-by-step version with screenshots is [how to block a machine for a maintenance day](/blog/how-to-block-a-machine-for-a-maintenance-day-in-edgebic), which covers the full-day case; a partial day is the same dialog with a number other than zero.

## Route B: The Whole Site Loses the Same Hours

When the window hits everything, a partial plant holiday does it in one entry. You define the window once and every shift is charged only its own overlap with it, so a 10:00 to 10:45 all-hands meeting costs the day shift 45 minutes and costs an evening shift that never touches those hours nothing at all.

That makes it right for site-wide events: a utility outage, a safety stand-down, a company meeting. It is wrong for one machine, because it reduces every work center in the plant. See [how to add a plant holiday](/blog/how-to-add-a-plant-holiday-in-edgebic).

## What About a Recurring Window?

Recurring windows and closures scoped to a single shift are supported by the scheduling engine but have no configuration screen today. A standing weekly slot is therefore entered as per-day overrides on the affected dates rather than as one repeating rule.

It is better to know that than to hunt for a menu that is not there. Two practical notes. Enter them in batches, a quarter at a time, rather than week to week. And do not reach for the tempting shortcut of shortening the shift: that moves the start or end time and tells the scheduler the machine is closed at a time it is actually running, which distorts every other calculation that reads the shift.

## What Changes When You Save

| Surface | Effect |
|---|---|
| The machine's hours on that date | Replaced by your number, ahead of the shift formula |
| Capacity and calendar views | Show the reduced hours on refresh |
| Existing schedules | Unchanged until you rerun |
| The next scheduling run | Books only the remaining hours; work that no longer fits slides |
| Work already started | Untouched; logged actuals are never moved by a reschedule |

A worked case: a milling cell on an eight-hour day shift with a two-hour Monday maintenance window is entered as `6` for that Monday. If a seven-hour job was landing there, the next run places six hours and spills the last hour into Tuesday.

## How to Check It Worked

1. Reopen the per-day capacity dialog and confirm the date carries your number and its reason.
2. Read the machine's available hours for that day in the capacity view: a one-instance eight-hour shift with two hours removed should read 6.
3. Run the schedule and confirm no bars sit inside the protected hours.

## Common Mistakes

- **Looking for a downtime tab.** There is none in the current release. The override dialog is where the hours come out.
- **Entering the loss on one shift only.** A machine running days and nights needs the entry on both, or half the day stays bookable.
- **Using a plant holiday for one machine.** It closes every work center on every shift, which is a large and very visible mistake.
- **Shortening the shift instead.** It misrepresents when the machine is open and affects everything else that reads the calendar.
- **Forgetting to rerun.** Capacity is read at planning time, so bars already on the plan sit through the window until a run happens.
- **Leaving the entry in place afterward.** A stale override quietly starves a machine that is back in service.
