**A KPI tile is the unit a dashboard is built from: a single metric rendered together with everything a reader needs to interpret it honestly.** In EDGEBIC by User Solutions one tile carries a formatted value, a unit, a target and its plain-language summary, a 30 day sparkline, a short delta note, a data quality chip declaring where the number came from, and an explanation holding the full formula. The design principle is that a number should never appear without the context that makes it readable.

This entry is part of the EDGEBIC glossary series introduced on [the platform overview](/edgebic); the wider vocabulary index sits in the [manufacturing glossary](/blog/manufacturing-glossary). The provenance badge on every tile has its own entry, the [data quality chip](/blog/what-is-a-data-quality-chip).

## How a KPI Tile Works

A car dashboard is the honest comparison. A speedometer is not just a needle: it is a needle, a scale, a redline, and the shared understanding that the reading comes from the wheels rather than from a guess. Strip any of those away and the needle becomes decorative. A metric on a factory dashboard has exactly the same problem, and a KPI tile is the answer to it.

Seven parts do the work.

- **The value**, pre-formatted and shown large, for example "94.2%". It is the thing you see from across the room.
- **The unit**, a short label such as percent, jobs, or units per week, so the scale is never ambiguous.
- **The target**, a numeric threshold used to color the tile, plus a **target summary** stating it in words, for example "Target 95 percent or better".
- **The sparkline**, thirty daily values oldest first, drawn as a small line chart beneath the value.
- **The delta note**, a brief change summary such as "35 ops moved off plan", which turns a fallen number into a starting point for investigation.
- **The data quality chip**, one of Live, Plan, Estimate or Offline, declaring the provenance of the value.
- **The explanation**, the full formula and source description, reachable from the question mark on the tile.

The chip and the explanation are the two that separate a dashboard from a poster. Live means a persisted real event, such as a count of open orders. Plan means a projection from the schedule that firms up as actuals arrive. Estimate means a heuristic. Offline means the formula is correct but the source data does not exist yet, and in that case the tile shows a dash. That last behavior is a deliberate choice: a plausible zero would be read as a real zero and quietly poison every decision downstream, while a dash is unmistakably a statement about the data.

## A Concrete Example

Take the schedule adherence tile on the planner view during a rough fortnight.

Over the 14 day window, 120 operations were scheduled to start. Eighty-five started within an hour of plan and 35 have no logged actual start at all. Adherence is 85 divided by 120, or 70.8 percent.

The tile renders as:

| Part | Content |
|---|---|
| Value | 70.8% |
| Unit | percent |
| Target | 90, colored red because the value is below it |
| Target summary | "Target 90 percent or better" |
| Sparkline | 30 daily adherence values, oldest first |
| Delta note | "35 ops moved off plan" |
| Chip | Live, because an actual start is a captured event |
| Explanation | the formula and the source of the operations counted |

Everything a planner needs to act sits in one square. The value says there is a problem. The target says how big. The sparkline says whether it arrived this morning or has been building. The delta note names the 35 operations to look at first. The chip warns that a missing kiosk entry and a genuinely late start are indistinguishable in the arithmetic, so the first move is to confirm the actuals exist. None of that requires opening a second screen.

## How EDGEBIC Uses KPI Tiles

Tiles are the building block of the dashboard cockpit, which arranges them into audience-specific tabs: an executive view, a planner view, and a standup board sized for a wall-mounted screen. Each tab loads a snapshot rather than streaming, so after a schedule run you refresh to see the new picture.

Two behaviors are worth knowing:

- **Tiles can be shown or hidden.** A board displayed to the floor every morning is usually better without permanently dashed Offline tiles, because a standing dash trains people to skip the whole row. A team actively working toward capturing that data may prefer to keep the gap visible as a reminder. What no setting will do is turn a dash into a zero.
- **Each tile owns its threshold.** Coloring compares a tile's raw value to its own target, so the same percentage can read red on one tile and green on another. The target summary is the line that resolves it.

The step by step for choosing which tiles appear and in what order is in [how to customize the dashboard KPI tiles](/blog/how-to-customize-the-dashboard-kpi-tiles-in-edgebic), and the whole cockpit is walked through in the [EDGEBIC dashboards guide](/blog/edgebic-dashboards-explained).
