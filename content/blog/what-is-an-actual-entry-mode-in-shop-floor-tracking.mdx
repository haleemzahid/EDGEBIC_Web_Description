**An actual entry mode sets whether the shop-floor kiosk captures hours or pieces as the primary input when an operator records production, deriving the other value from the work center's rate.** It has three settings. Hours Primary shows elapsed time and calculates the piece count from the rate. Pieces Primary shows a tap-to-count piece counter and calculates the hours from the rate. Both accepts hours and pieces as independent measurements, which lets the system compute a performance variance between the measured output and the rate-predicted output. The mode fits the way each operation is naturally measured.

## How it works

Every operation that gets tracked produces two related numbers: the time it took and the pieces it made. The two are linked by the work center's rate, the pieces per hour it is expected to run. The actual entry mode decides which number the operator actually enters and which the system calculates from the rate.

In Hours Primary, time is the measured quantity. The operator clocks elapsed hours at the kiosk, and the system multiplies by the rate to record a piece count. This suits operations where time is the honest measure, such as a machining cell, and where the exact piece count is less important than the hours consumed.

In Pieces Primary, output is the measured quantity. The operator taps a counter as each good unit is finished, and the system divides by the rate to record the hours. This suits operations where counting pieces is the natural gesture, such as an assembly line, and where knowing exactly how many good units exist matters.

In Both, neither number is derived. The kiosk captures real elapsed time and a real piece count separately. Because the two are measured independently, the system can compare them: the pieces actually produced per hour against the pieces the rate predicted. That gap is a performance variance, a useful signal that a machine ran fast, slow, or off-standard.

## A concrete example

Take three operations. A CNC machining cell runs in Hours Primary: the operator clocks two hours, and at a rate of forty pieces an hour the system records eighty pieces. Time was measured; pieces were derived.

An assembly station runs in Pieces Primary: the operator taps a count as each unit is finished, reaching seventy-five pieces, and at the station's rate the system derives the hours. Output was measured; time was derived.

A paint line runs in Both: the operator logs the real cycle time and, separately, a real piece count. The system now has two independent numbers and can compute the performance variance between them, revealing whether the line ran above or below its standard rate. Each operation is measured in the way that is honest for the work it does.

## How EDGEBIC uses it

EDGEBIC applies the actual entry mode at the kiosk, where operators record production against a scheduled operation. Hours Primary presents an elapsed-time entry and derives pieces from the work center rate; Pieces Primary presents a tap-to-count entry and derives hours; Both captures each independently and enables the performance-variance comparison. Different work centers can use different modes, so each station records actuals the way its work is naturally measured.

Whichever mode produced them, the resulting hours and pieces flow into the same daily hour breakdown and job actuals, so the schedule, the Log Actuals view, and the reports read them uniformly. A step's recorded actuals then become immutable to the rescheduler, so once captured they anchor the plan while the remaining work is replanned around them.

Because the mode only governs which number the operator enters, it changes how actuals are captured, not what the schedule does with them. That keeps data entry fast and honest on the floor while the planning side stays consistent.
