**To schedule around a planned shutdown week in EDGEBIC, add a whole-day plant holiday for each closed day of the week, mark them recurring if the shutdown repeats annually, then reschedule the affected jobs. EDGEBIC by User Solutions removes those dates from every work center's capacity, so a rescheduled job flows its work before and after the closure.** A holiday record covers one date, so a five-day shutdown is five records.

This is the shutdown procedure. For a single closure, see [how to add a plant holiday in EDGEBIC](/blog/how-to-add-a-plant-holiday-in-edgebic); for the calendar mechanics, read [EDGEBIC shifts and calendars explained](/blog/edgebic-shifts-and-calendars-explained). It is one of the task guides in the [EDGEBIC how-to hub](/blog/edgebic-how-to-hub).

## Before You Start

| Prerequisite | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| You know the exact shutdown dates | Each closed working day becomes its own whole-day holiday. |
| You know which jobs overlap the week | Those are the jobs you reschedule so their work flows around the closure. |
| You know whether the shutdown recurs | An annual shutdown is entered once with Recurring each year. |

## Step One: Add a Whole-Day Holiday Per Closed Day

1. Open the **Daily Hours** tab, then the inner **Holiday** tab. The **Plant Holidays** page opens.
2. Click **Add Holiday**.
3. Enter a **Name**, for example "Summer Shutdown Mon", and the **Date** for the first closed day.
4. Leave **Closes the whole day** checked so the entire day is blocked for every work center and shift.
5. If the shutdown repeats every year, tick **Recurring each year**. Recurring holidays match on month and day, so only those matter.
6. Confirm **Active** is checked, then **Save**. The grid shows **Scope** as **Whole Day**.
7. Repeat for each remaining working day of the shutdown week. Weekends the plant already does not work need no holiday.

## Step Two: Reschedule the Affected Jobs

Saving holidays changes the calendar but moves nothing on its own.

1. On the **Drive Schedule** tab, tick the jobs whose work overlaps or runs near the shutdown week.
2. Press **Schedule + Re-Schedule**.
3. Confirm the dialog and let the pass complete.

The engine now treats the shutdown days as unavailable and replans the affected jobs so their operations land before or after the closed week.

## What Changes When You Save

| Surface | Effect |
|---|---|
| Plant Holidays grid | Each closed day appears immediately as Whole Day. |
| Work center capacity on those dates | Removed entirely for every work center on the global calendar. |
| Existing schedules | Unchanged until you reschedule, even if they now sit inside the closed week. |
| Rescheduled jobs | Flow their work before and after the shutdown, skipping the closed days. |
| Completed work | Never moved by the reschedule. |

## How to Check It Worked

1. On the **Plant Holidays** grid, confirm each shutdown day is listed with **Scope** as **Whole Day** and, for an annual shutdown, **Recurring** ticked.
2. Open an affected job in **Job View** and confirm no operation lands on any shutdown day.
3. Confirm the job's finish moved out by the length of the closure, which is expected.
4. Spot-check a work center's capacity view for the shutdown week and confirm it shows zero available hours.

## A Worked Shutdown

The plant closes the first full week of August for annual maintenance, Monday through Friday. The plant already does not run weekends, so those two days need nothing.

On the Plant Holidays page you add five whole-day holidays, one per working day: Monday through Friday of that week, each with Closes the whole day checked and Recurring each year ticked because the shutdown repeats every summer. The grid now lists five rows, each with Scope reading Whole Day and Recurring marked.

Then you tick the jobs whose work runs anywhere near that week and press Schedule + Re-Schedule. A job that would have milled Tuesday now finds no capacity there, so the engine flows that work to the Friday before or the Monday after, and its finish moves out by the length of the closure. A job that could finish entirely before the shutdown is untouched by the closure, because its operations never needed those days.

Because the holidays are recurring, next August the closure is already in the calendar. You do not re-enter anything; you simply reschedule as the dates approach and the plant closure is honored automatically. Confirm the result by scanning a work center's capacity view for the shutdown week, where every day should read zero available hours.

## Common Mistakes and Gotchas

- **Expecting one record to cover the week.** A holiday covers a single date. Enter one whole-day holiday per closed working day.
- **Saving holidays but not rescheduling.** The plan computed against the old calendar still stands. Reschedule the affected jobs after adding the holidays.
- **A work center on a custom calendar.** Global plant holidays cover work centers on the global calendar. A machine with its own calendar follows its own rules; handle it with a per-day capacity override or its own closures.
- **Forgetting Recurring for an annual shutdown.** Without it, you re-enter the holidays every year and risk a gap. Tick Recurring each year so the closure repeats automatically.
- **Leaving a holiday Inactive.** An unticked Active box keeps the record but the scheduler ignores it, so the plant does not actually close.
