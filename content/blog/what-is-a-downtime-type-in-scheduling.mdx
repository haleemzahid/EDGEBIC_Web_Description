**A downtime type classifies why a work center is unavailable, from Scheduled planned maintenance that is already out of capacity to an Unscheduled breakdown that triggers a review, so lost time carries a labeled cause rather than being a blank gap.** It tells a planner whether a machine being down was expected or a surprise.

## How it works

Downtime on a work center is recorded as an event covering a period, and a downtime type is stamped on it to say why. At the base level the type is Scheduled or Unscheduled. Scheduled means planned maintenance, PM, or changeover: known in advance and already removed from available capacity before the plan is built. Unscheduled means a breakdown or unplanned stoppage: entered after the fact, so it was not in the plan and it triggers a review of the affected work.

A richer classification expands this into planned, unplanned, maintenance, break, meeting, and training, so a shop that wants to analyze lost time can tag each event with a specific cause.

The distinction drives behavior. Scheduled downtime is a normal capacity input, so the engine simply plans around it. Unscheduled downtime is a disruption to reality, so it prompts the planner to review and reschedule the steps it interrupted. Same lost hours, very different meaning, which is exactly what the type captures.

## A concrete example

Think of a delivery van being off the road. A booked service appointment is planned: the dispatcher already routed around it, so no drama. A flat tire on the highway is unplanned: it was not in the plan, it stranded a load, and someone has to re-route the remaining stops. The van is unavailable either way, but the two events mean completely different things, and the label is what tells them apart.

Take a work center with a Saturday PM (Scheduled) and a Tuesday spindle failure (Unscheduled). The Saturday PM was subtracted from capacity when the week was planned, so no job was ever placed there and nothing needs attention. The Tuesday failure was entered after it happened, so the jobs that were running get flagged for review and the remaining steps are rescheduled from where the shop actually stands. The type decided which of the two got a second look.

## How EDGEBIC uses it

In EDGEBIC, a downtime type is the classification on a downtime event, and it shapes how the schedule reacts. Scheduled downtime is already out of the capacity picture, so the plan flows around it cleanly. Unscheduled downtime, being an after-the-fact entry, triggers the actuals review that leads to rescheduling only what the disruption touched, while completed work stays put.

The richer types add analysis value. Tagging events as maintenance, break, meeting, or training lets a planner total lost time by cause and target the biggest one, rather than treating all downtime as a single undifferentiated loss. That is the difference between knowing the shop loses hours and knowing precisely where.
