**To see a job's step-by-step schedule in EDGEBIC by User Solutions, open the Schedule Job BOR view and pick the job number.** It lists one row per operation with the work center it was assigned to, its planned start and end, its actual start and end if any, and the utilization used. This is the operation-level view that the job's overall start and end dates on the Orders grid cannot give you.

Two questions look the same but are not. "When does this job start and finish?" is answered on the Orders grid. "Which machine is each operation on, and when?" needs the step view. This page covers the second.

## Before You Start

- **The job has been scheduled.** An unscheduled order has no operation rows to show.
- **You have the job number** or can find it in the dropdown.
- **You understand this reads the saved plan,** not unsaved Gantt edits.

## Step 1: Open the Schedule Job BOR View

Under the **Schedule** area, open the **Schedule Job BOR** sub-tab. This view is built specifically to show operation-level detail for one job at a time.

## Step 2: Select the Job

Choose the job number from the dropdown at the top of the view. The grid loads every operation for that job.

Each row shows the operation's **work center**, its planned start and end, its actual start and end when they exist, its utilization, and the routing step it came from. Read it top to bottom and you have the full path of the part through the plant.

## Step 3: Read the Columns

Work through the rows in sequence:

- The **work center** column tells you where each operation landed. If a step has an alternate or a pool behind it, this is where you learn which resource actually took the work.
- The **planned** dates are what the engine computed.
- The **actual** dates, if present, are what the shop floor reported. A blank actual means the step has not been confirmed yet.
- **Utilization** shows how much of the shift the step used. A material or component line reads zero because it consumes no machine time.

## What Changes When You Save

Nothing. This is a read-only view. Opening it, selecting a job, and reading the rows changes no data. It is a lens onto the committed plan, which is exactly why it is safe to hand to anyone who needs to answer a per-operation question.

## How to Check It Worked

1. Confirm the grid shows one row per operation in the routing, in sequence.
2. Confirm each work center operation names a real machine and carries planned dates.
3. If the job has been running, confirm the completed operations carry actual dates and the not-yet-started ones do not.
4. If a number looks wrong, cross-check the Gantt. If the two disagree, you are looking at unsaved Gantt edits, which do not reach this view until you save them.

## Common Mistakes

**Expecting unsaved Gantt drags to appear.** The step view reads the saved plan. Save the Gantt first, then reopen the view.

**Reading a material step's zero utilization as a fault.** Material and component steps consume no capacity and schedule instantly. Zero is correct for them.

**Confusing job-level dates with step-level dates.** The job's overall start and end come from the earliest and latest of its operations. They are not the same as any single step's dates. Use the step view when you need the machine-by-machine picture.
