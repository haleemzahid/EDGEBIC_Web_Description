**To find a job's scheduled dates in EDGEBIC, read the Start Date, Item Start and Job End columns on the Drive Schedule grid, or open the Job View tab for the date of every operation.** The grid gives you the job-level window at a glance. Job View gives you the per-work-center breakdown. Knowing which date is which, and which one to quote a customer, is the whole skill here, and EDGEBIC by User Solutions keeps the planner's inputs and the engine's outputs as separate columns on purpose.

This is a reading task. For the run that produces these dates, see [how to run the scheduler in EDGEBIC](/blog/how-to-run-the-scheduler-in-edgebic). The full library is at the [EDGEBIC how-to hub](/blog/edgebic-how-to-hub).

## Before You Start

- The job carries a **Scheduled** badge. An unscheduled job has no computed dates.
- You know the difference you are looking for: the job-level window, or the step-level detail.

## Read the Job-Level Dates on the Grid

1. **Open the Schedule area** and select the **Drive Schedule** tab.
2. **Read across the row:**

| Column | What it tells you |
|---|---|
| **Start Date** | The job's start anchor. For jobs without one, the earliest scheduled start is shown. |
| **Item Start** | When the last work center operation ends: the moment the item physically exists. |
| **Job End** | **Item Start** plus the product's lead time in calendar days: the delivery-ready date. |
| **Due Date** | The promise. Blank due dates are filled once from the first planned end and never moved. |
| **Days Late** | The gap between **Job End** and **Due Date**, shown as a projection for open jobs. |
| **Schedule Details** | A one-line "Start: …" summary of the plan. |

That is enough to answer most questions: when does it start, when is it built, when can it ship, and is it going to be late.

## Read the Step-Level Dates in Job View

1. **Open the Job View tab** and pick the job in the **Select Job** dropdown.
2. **Read the summary panel** for the whole-job window (**Original Start** and **Original End**) and the hours roll-up (**Total Hours**, **Actual Hours**, **Remaining**, **% Complete**).
3. **Read the per-operation grid** below it: **Sched Start**, **Sched End** and **Duration** per work center step, alongside **Actual Start** and **Actual End** once the floor logs them.
4. **Set the date window** with the **Start** and **End** pickers if operations fall outside the default view.

The Gantt beside the grid draws the same plan as bars, so you can read the dates as numbers or see them as a timeline.

## What the Dates Are (and Are Not)

The scheduled **Start Date** and **Item Start** are the engine's output. The order's **Start Time** field is your input: the earliest the job may begin. EDGEBIC does not overwrite your **Start Time** with the computed dates, because doing so would quietly raise the earliest-start floor on every run and drift the job later and later. The two are meant to differ, and the grid keeps them apart.

## How to Check It Worked

Confirm the row shows the **Scheduled** badge and the date columns are populated rather than blank. If **Item Start** and **Job End** are empty, the job is not scheduled yet, and there are no dates to find until you run it.

If the Job View step dates do not match what you see on the Gantt, check for unsaved drags. Job View reads the saved plan, so in-memory overrides you have not saved yet will not appear there until you save them.

## Common Mistakes

**Quoting Item Start when you mean the ship date.** Item Start is when the item is built; **Job End** is when it can ship. If the product has a lead time, the two differ by that tail, and the customer cares about **Job End**.

**Reading the order's Start Time as the scheduled start.** Start Time is the earliest-start input, not the computed result. The scheduled start lives in **Start Date** and **Item Start**.

**Expecting Job View to show unsaved drags.** The step grid reads from the database. Save your Gantt changes first, or the step dates will lag what you moved on screen.

**Treating a blank due date as an error.** On a forward job it is not. The scheduler fills it once from the first planned end, giving you an honest baseline instead of a promise nobody made.
