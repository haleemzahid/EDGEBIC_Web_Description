**To turn off a plant holiday in EDGEBIC by User Solutions without losing it, edit the holiday on the Plant Holidays page and untick Active. The record stays exactly as configured, and the scheduler stops honoring it from the next run onward.** The hours come back, the audit trail survives, and one checkbox puts the closure back when you want it.

This matters more than it sounds. Holidays are usually entered once with **Recurring each year** ticked so they fire every year automatically. That is the right way to maintain them, and it is precisely why you should never delete one to open a single day. Deleting a recurring closure costs you the rule; deactivating it costs you nothing.

## Before You Start

- **Know whether the change is permanent or temporary.** Temporary means deactivate. Permanent means delete, or edit the date if the closure simply moved.
- **Know whether the holiday recurs.** The **Recurring** column on the grid tells you. Deactivating a recurring holiday suspends it for every year, not just the one in front of you, so plan to reactivate it.
- **Expect to reschedule.** Nothing on the plan moves until you run Drive Schedule.

## Step 1: Open the Plant Holidays Page

Open the **Daily Hours** tab from the main navigation, then select the inner **Holiday** tab. The **Plant Holidays** page opens, described in the app as plant-wide closures affecting all work centers and shifts.

The grid lists every closure with these columns: **S.#**, **Name**, **Description**, **Date**, **Recurring**, **Scope**, **Window**, **Active**, and **Actions**. Read **Active** before you change anything, because a holiday that never seemed to reduce capacity may already be switched off.

## Step 2: Edit the Holiday and Untick Active

Click the edit control in the holiday's **Actions** column. The **Holiday** dialog opens with the record as you saved it.

Untick **Active**. Leave everything else alone: the **Name**, the **Date**, the **Recurring each year** checkbox, the **Closes the whole day** setting, and the **Start Time** and **End Time** of a partial window all stay put. That is the whole point of using the Active flag rather than editing the rule.

If it helps the next person, add a line to **Description** explaining the exception, for example `Suspended for 2026 only, reinstate in December`.

Click **Save**. The grid row now shows **Active** unticked.

## Step 3: Rerun the Scheduler

Open **Drive Schedule** and run **Schedule + Re-Schedule** for the jobs whose plans should now use the reopened day. Until you do, the plan on screen still flows around a closure the calendar no longer contains.

## What Changes When You Save

| Surface | Effect of unticking Active |
|---|---|
| **Plant Holidays** grid | Updates immediately, with the row and all its settings intact |
| Capacity for that date | The subtraction stops. A whole-day closure gives back the full day; a partial closure gives back only the overlap it was removing |
| Existing schedules and promised dates | Unchanged until the next scheduling run |
| Resource Calendar and capacity views | Show the restored hours when refreshed |
| Next **Drive Schedule** run | Plans jobs into the reopened day like any other working day |

The restored amount depends on the holiday's scope. A whole-day closure removed the date entirely for every work center, so reactivating the day returns each work center's full `shift hours x instances` figure. A partial closure removed only the overlap between its window and each shift, so a 10:00 to 10:45 meeting was costing the day shift 0.75 hours per instance and costing a night shift nothing. Turning it off returns those 0.75 hours and nothing more. [How to schedule a half-day holiday](/blog/how-to-schedule-a-half-day-holiday-in-edgebic) covers that arithmetic in full.

## Deactivate, Delete, or Edit the Date

Three different situations, three different answers.

| Situation | Do this |
|---|---|
| The closure is canceled this year but will return | Untick **Active**, reactivate later |
| The closure moved to a different day | Edit **Date** on the existing row |
| The holiday was entered by mistake, or the plant no longer closes for it at all | Delete the row |
| Only part of the day is now closed | Untick **Closes the whole day** and enter the window |

Deleting is done from the holiday's **Actions** column and is confirmed before it happens. It is the right call for a typo or a genuinely retired closure. It is the wrong call for a one-year exception, because a recurring rule has to be rebuilt by hand once it is gone.

## How to Check It Worked

1. On the **Plant Holidays** grid, confirm the row still exists and **Active** is unticked.
2. Run **Drive Schedule**.
3. Open the **Resource Calendar** and read the **Total Capacity** row for a work center on that date. It should show its normal shift hours instead of zero (or, for a partial closure, the full shift hours rather than the reduced figure).
4. Look at the Gantt. Jobs that used to jump the date should now flow through it.

## A Short Worked Sequence

Acme has a recurring whole-day closure on the Friday after Thanksgiving. This year the plant is working it to recover a late order.

1. **Daily Hours, then the Holiday tab.** The row shows **Recurring** ticked, **Scope** as Whole Day, **Active** ticked.
2. **Edit it and untick Active.** Leave **Recurring each year** alone. Add `Working 2026 only, reinstate December` to **Description**.
3. **Save.** The grid row stays, now marked inactive.
4. **Run Drive Schedule.** Every work center regains its normal `shift hours x instances` on that Friday. The late order picks up a full day of milling, and the jobs behind it move in with it.
5. **In December, tick Active again.** The recurring rule resumes untouched, and next year's Friday closes automatically like every year before it.

Total edits to the rule itself: none. That is what makes this the safe move rather than clearing the recurring flag or deleting the row.

## Common Mistakes

- **Deleting a recurring holiday to open one day.** You lose the rule and will rediscover the problem next year, usually a week too late.
- **Clearing the Recurring each year checkbox instead.** That also changes the rule permanently, with the added confusion that the row still looks normal.
- **Forgetting to reschedule.** The capacity is back; the plan is not. Nothing moves until the next run.
- **Forgetting to reactivate.** A suspended recurring closure stays suspended forever. Reactivate as soon as the exception is over.
- **Blaming the holiday when a capacity override is responsible.** A per-day capacity override of zero closes a day just as effectively and beats the calendar formula. Check the work center's per-day dialog before you keep editing holidays. [How to override capacity for one day](/blog/how-to-override-capacity-for-one-day-in-edgebic) shows where that lives.
