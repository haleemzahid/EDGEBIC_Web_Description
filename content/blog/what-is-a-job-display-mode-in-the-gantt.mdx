**A job display mode in the Gantt is a viewing setting that controls which jobs the chart shows and how they are drawn: All shows every job for a full plant view, Selected narrows to the job or jobs you have picked, and Compare overlays plan against actual.** It is a lens on the schedule, not a change to it, so switching modes only changes what you see on screen.

This entry is part of the [EDGEBIC by User Solutions](/edgebic) glossary series; for the wider vocabulary of scheduling, see the [manufacturing glossary](/blog/manufacturing-glossary). The job display mode decides which jobs appear, while the [timeline view mode](/blog/what-is-a-timeline-view-mode-in-the-gantt) decides how much time is on screen, so the two settings work together.

## How a Job Display Mode Works

The Gantt can hold a great deal at once: every operation of every job across many work centers. That is powerful for planning the whole plant but overwhelming when you want to concentrate on one order or answer one question. The job display mode is the control that trades breadth for focus.

The All mode is the default plant view. Every scheduled job is drawn, giving the planner the complete picture of load, sequence, and contention across the shop. It is the right mode when you are balancing work or looking for conflicts across many orders at once.

The Selected mode filters the chart down to only the job or jobs you have picked. Everything else disappears, so the operations of a single rush order stand alone and its sequence and timing are easy to read. The Compare mode does something different: it keeps the current bars but overlays them against the saved baseline plan, so plan and actual sit side by side and any drift is immediately visible. None of the three modes touches the schedule; each simply redraws it.

## A Concrete Example

Imagine a planner whose Gantt shows forty jobs across a dozen work centers, a dense wall of bars. A customer calls about one rush order and the planner needs to see just that order's route through the shop.

The planner selects the rush job and switches the display mode to Selected. Instantly the other thirty-nine jobs vanish and only the rush order's operations remain: its milling step, its drilling step, its assembly, laid out in sequence with their dependencies clear. The planner reads the order's timing in seconds, answers the customer, and switches back to All to restore the full plant view.

Later, in a review meeting, the same job is behind schedule. The planner switches to Compare mode, and now the job's actual bars are drawn against its original baseline. The gap between plan and reality is visible on the chart, so the team can see exactly where the drift began without cross-checking dates by hand.

## How EDGEBIC Uses It

The job display mode is a Gantt-level toggle that shapes what the chart presents.

- **All mode** renders every scheduled job, the plant-wide view for balancing load and spotting contention.
- **Selected mode** filters to the chosen job or jobs, giving a clean single-order view for focused work.
- **Compare mode** overlays current actuals on the saved baseline, turning the Gantt into a plan-versus-actual instrument for reviews.

The comparison that Compare mode draws is only as good as the reference it draws against, which is a saved [Gantt baseline](/blog/what-is-a-gantt-baseline). Because switching modes never alters the plan, it pairs safely with any other Gantt work you are doing.
