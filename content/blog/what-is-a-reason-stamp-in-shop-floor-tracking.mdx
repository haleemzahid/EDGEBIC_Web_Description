**A reason stamp is the event of attaching a reason code to a punch, and it comes in exactly two kinds: a pause stamp on a down, idle, or rework punch, and a scrap stamp on a run punch that is recording rejected pieces.** The stamp is the moment the why is captured. EDGEBIC by User Solutions treats it as its own event so the reason can be routed to whoever should respond, not just filed for a report.

## How it works

A punch records what an operator did: started, paused, resumed, completed. A reason code records why. Those are separate pieces of information, and they arrive at different moments, which is why the attachment of one to the other is worth naming.

The **pause** kind fires when a down, idle, or rework punch has its reason code set through the pause picker on the kiosk. The operator has already told the system that production stopped. The stamp is the follow-up answer to what caused it: broken tooling, no blanks, waiting on an approval.

The **scrap** kind fires on a punch that is still running. Production has not stopped, but parts have been rejected, so the punch records a scrap piece count and a scrap reason code together. Nothing pauses. The machine keeps cutting. What changed is that some of the output will not ship.

Keeping the two kinds distinct matters more than it first appears. Downtime analysis and quality analysis answer different questions and have different owners, and folding scrap into the same bucket as pauses makes both harder to read. An hour lost to a hydraulic fault and eleven parts lost to a bad lot are not the same kind of loss, even when they happen on the same machine in the same hour.

The second half of the mechanism is routing. A stamp can notify one of three roles: **maintenance** for machine fault escalation, the production **lead** for material or waiting issues, and the **quality inspector** for quality-related pauses. That mapping is what converts a captured reason into a response. Without routing, a reason code is a research tool. With it, the picker on the kiosk becomes the fastest way for an operator to get the right person to the station.

## A concrete example

Think of the difference between a fire alarm and the incident report someone writes afterward. Both record that there was a fire. Only one of them brings the fire brigade. A reason stamp is meant to be the alarm, and the report is a free byproduct of having sounded it.

Put that on a floor. At 10:05 the operator on Press-1 hits pause and picks broken tooling from the picker. That is a pause stamp with a machine-family reason, and it routes to maintenance, who can be walking before anyone tells them by radio. At 10:20, on the mill next door, a run punch records eleven scrap pieces against a material defect reason. That is a scrap stamp. The mill did not stop, so nobody would have raised it by voice, but the reason is now attached to that job at that time and the quality inspector has been told.

By the end of the week the same two events also serve the reporting side. Press-1 shows an hour of machine-family pause time, and the mill shows eleven rejected pieces tied to a lot rather than to the machine. Neither number needed anyone to reconstruct it from memory, because both were captured at the moment they were true.

Compare that with the plant where reasons are added the next morning. The pause is still there in the data, but the reason is a guess, no one was notified at the time, and the eleven scrap parts are a line on a tally sheet with no job, no hour, and no cause.
