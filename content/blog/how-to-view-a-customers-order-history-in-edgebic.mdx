**To view a customer's order history in EDGEBIC by User Solutions, open the Customers tab, find the customer, and click the view-history button on their row.** The Customer Order History dialog opens with two tabs, one for manufacturing orders and one for quotes, and lists every record for that account in one place. It is the fastest answer to "what are we building for this customer, and what have we promised them?" For the concepts behind customer records, read [customers and departments explained](/blog/edgebic-customers-and-departments-explained).

## Before you start

- The customer record exists. If not, see [how to add a customer](/blog/how-to-add-a-customer-in-edgebic).
- The customer has at least one order or quote, or the tabs will simply show zero counts.
- You know what you are looking for: an open job's status, a past quote, or a pre-deletion check of what is attached to the account.
- Remember the dialog is read-only. Edits happen on the order itself, from the schedule.

## Steps

1. Open the **Customers** tab from the main navigation.
2. Find the customer. Type a fragment of the name or Id into the grid's search panel.
3. Click the view-history button in the customer's row. The tooltip reads **View History**.
4. The **Customer Order History** dialog opens, titled with the customer's name. It has two tabs:
   - **Manufacturing Orders**, with columns for job number, product, quantity, due date, status, and estimated cost.
   - **Quotes**, with columns for quote number, product, quantity, quote date, expiry, status, and total.
5. The tab captions show the counts, so you can see at a glance how many orders and how many quotes exist without opening each tab.
6. Read what you need, then click **Close**.

## What the dialog shows and what it does not change

The dialog gathers, it does not act. Nothing you do inside it moves a job, changes a quote, or touches the schedule. It is a summary view assembled from records that live elsewhere.

| Tab | Columns | Answers |
|---|---|---|
| **Manufacturing Orders** | Job number, product, quantity, due date, status, estimated cost | What is this account building, and where does each job stand |
| **Quotes** | Quote number, product, quantity, quote date, expiry, status, total | What have we quoted this account, and what is still live |

To act on anything you find, open the order from the schedule, where it is editable, or open the quote from the quotes area. The history dialog points you there; it is not the place to make the change.

## How to check it worked

1. Confirm the dialog title carries the customer's name, so you are certain you opened the right account.
2. Compare the tab count on **Manufacturing Orders** against what you expect for the customer. A surprising count is often the sign of a duplicate customer record splitting the history.
3. Cross-check one job number from the dialog against the schedule. It should be the same job, with the same status.
4. If a job you expected is missing, check whether it was raised against a different customer record. Duplicate accounts are the usual reason history looks incomplete.

## Common mistakes

**Trying to edit in the dialog.** It is read-only by design. Open the order from the schedule to change it.

**Reading incomplete history as missing jobs.** If a customer was accidentally duplicated, their orders split across two records and each history dialog shows only part of the story. Consolidate duplicates by re-pointing orders to one record.

**Skipping the history check before a delete.** The delete confirmation does not list what is linked. The history dialog is the only pre-flight view of the orders and quotes that would lose their customer link, so open it first and prefer deactivation if anything is live. See [how to deactivate a product without losing history](/blog/how-to-deactivate-a-product-without-losing-history-in-edgebic) for the same principle applied to products.

**Expecting the history to reflect scheduling priority.** It does not. The customer is a traceability link, and every date and priority the scheduler acts on sits on the order itself.
