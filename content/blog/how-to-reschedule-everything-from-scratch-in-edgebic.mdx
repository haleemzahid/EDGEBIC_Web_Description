**To reschedule everything from scratch in EDGEBIC, select every job on the Drive Schedule tab and press Schedule + Re-Schedule for a full rebuild that still preserves completed work, or press Clear Schedule first for a true blank-sheet reset. EDGEBIC by User Solutions decides the scope from your selection, not from a mode picker.** Both paths are one button each, and knowing what each one keeps versus releases is the whole skill.

This is the plant-wide reset procedure. For the everyday pass, see [how to run the scheduler in EDGEBIC](/blog/how-to-run-the-scheduler-in-edgebic); for the mechanism underneath, [EDGEBIC scheduling modes explained](/blog/edgebic-scheduling-modes-explained) covers what a full pass does internally. It is one of the task guides in the [EDGEBIC how-to hub](/blog/edgebic-how-to-hub).

## Before You Start

| Prerequisite | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| At least one active shift exists | With no active shift the run stops and offers to create one. |
| Every product on the plan has a routing | An order with no routing is reported as a failure and skipped; it never aborts the rest. |
| Calendars and capacity are current | A full rebuild plans against calendars as they are right now. Fix master data first. |
| You know whether you want a rebuild or a wipe | A full reschedule keeps history; Clear Schedule deletes plan rows and reopens completed orders. |

## Path One: Rebuild the Whole Plan

This is the right choice most of the time. It replans every job while protecting recorded work.

1. Open the **Drive Schedule** tab.
2. Leave every **Select checkbox** unticked, or tick all rows explicitly.
3. Press **Schedule + Re-Schedule**.
4. If nothing was ticked, answer **Yes** to the **Schedule All Orders** prompt to include everything.
5. Read the **Confirm Scheduling** dialog. It reports the split, for example "You are about to schedule 40 job(s): 5 new, 35 existing." Answer **Yes**.
6. Watch the progress overlay to completion. Every scheduled job shows its **Scheduled** badge with dates filled in.

Each already-scheduled job releases its unfinished reservations so the engine can place them fresh, while completed and in-progress operations are kept exactly. The result is a rebuilt plan on the current calendar, not a wiped one.

## Path Two: A True Clean-Sheet Reset

Use this only when the plan itself is untrustworthy, for example after a bad import.

1. On the **Drive Schedule** tab, **deselect all** rows.
2. Press **Clear Schedule**.
3. Confirm the warning that all schedules will be cleared and the action cannot be undone.
4. With the plan now empty, tick every job and press **Schedule + Re-Schedule** to build a fresh plan.

Clear Schedule deletes every production schedule, schedule, and resource row, sets each order back to unscheduled, and reopens any completed order with an auditable inventory reversal. It deliberately leaves your planner inputs, including each job's start time, untouched.

## What Changes When You Run

| Surface | Full rebuild (Path One) | Clear Schedule (Path Two) |
|---|---|---|
| Not-started operations | Replanned against current capacity | Deleted, then replanned on the fresh pass |
| Completed operations | Preserved verbatim | Plan rows deleted; completed orders reopened |
| Logged actuals in the record | Preserved | Preserved in the underlying data, but orders reopened |
| Reserved capacity | Released and rebooked in one pass | Fully released |
| Inventory postings | Unchanged | Reversed auditably for reopened orders |
| Job start times | Kept (planner input) | Kept (planner input) |

## How to Check It Worked

1. After Path One, spot-check a job that was on time before: its **Days Late** should still read the value the current plan supports, not a surprise.
2. Confirm completed steps did not move by opening a finished job in **Job View** and reading its **Actual Start** and **Actual End** against what the floor reported.
3. After Path Two, verify the grid shows every job **Unscheduled** before you rebuild, then **Scheduled** after the fresh pass.
4. Check the **Scheduling Failure Dialog** if it appears. It lists any job that could not schedule, with a category and a fix hint, so a full pass never silently drops work.

## Common Mistakes and Gotchas

- **Reaching for Clear Schedule when a rebuild would do.** Clear Schedule reopens completed orders and reverses inventory. If you only want the plan to reflect new master data, Path One is safer and keeps history clean.
- **Forgetting to fix master data first.** A full reschedule plans against the calendar and routings as they are at run time. Edit shifts, holidays, capacity, and routings before you run, not after.
- **Assuming a rebuild wipes actuals.** It does not. Recorded work is preserved and remaining hours resume from reality. Only Clear Schedule tears down plan rows.
- **Ignoring the failure dialog.** On a plant-wide pass, a few jobs may fail pre-flight for a missing routing or an inactive work center. Read the fix hints and re-run the affected jobs rather than assuming everything scheduled.

A full rebuild is the right tool after a genuinely global change: a shift pattern edited across the plant, a wave of routing revisions, a capacity reset, or a fresh import that touched many products at once. It is the wrong tool for a single moved due date or one machine problem, where a rebuild churns dozens of jobs for a change that affected one. Match the size of the pass to the size of the change, and reach for a plant-wide rebuild only when the whole plan really does need to reflect new reality.
