**To see a machine's job history on the EDGEBIC by User Solutions kiosk, tap History on the header to open the history drawer, which lists every punch and every edit for the bound machine with who made it and when.** The drawer filters by Run, Setup, Down and Idle, and Edits, and it is where a supervisor reviews what actually happened at the machine and corrects a punch. Corrections never delete the original: they add an adjustment record with the old value, the new value, and a mandatory reason.

This is the history and correction task. The wider operator workflow lives in the [shop floor execution guide](/blog/edgebic-shop-floor-guide).

## Before You Start

| Prerequisite | Why |
|---|---|
| The kiosk is bound to the machine you want to review | The drawer shows the bound work center and instance's activity |
| The punches you want to review are closed | Open punches show as in progress; closed punches carry final hours |
| You have supervisor authority to correct a punch | Editing a closed punch is a supervisor action and requires a reason |

## Step 1: Open the History Drawer

Tap **History** on the kiosk header. The history drawer opens over the current screen and lists the punches and edits for the bound machine, most recent first. Opening the drawer changes nothing: it is a review surface. An operator can open it to see the shift so far, and a supervisor opens it to make a correction.

## Step 2: Filter to What You Are Looking For

The drawer offers filter tabs so you are not scrolling through everything at once.

| Filter | What it shows |
|---|---|
| **Run** | Production run punches with their hours and piece counts |
| **Setup** | Setup punches, including the phase-by-phase changeover records |
| **Down / Idle** | Pauses and downtime with the reason code each carried |
| **Edits** | Every correction made, including operator piece undos |

The Edits tab is the audit view. It shows every change anyone made, which is how a job's record stays honest: an operator's ten-second piece undo appears here just as a supervisor's hour correction does. How these punches roll up into the numbers a planner reads is covered in [actuals tracking explained](/blog/edgebic-actuals-tracking-explained).

## Step 3: Review a Punch

Select a punch to see its detail: the state, the start and end times, the hours computed on close, any piece counts, the reason code if it carried one, and the operator name stamped on it. Because timestamps come from the shared clock at the moment of each tap, the times are what actually happened, not what someone typed.

## Step 4: Correct a Punch (Supervisor)

To fix a wrong punch, a supervisor edits the field, for example the hours on a run punch that was completed early, and types a reason before saving. The reason is mandatory. Saving writes an adjustment record that carries the old value, the new value, the field changed, and the reason, while leaving the original punch row in place.

## Step 5: Rebuild the Daily Totals

After a correction, rebuild the daily breakdown so the corrected hours reach the planner. The rebuild recomputes the machine's daily hours from its current set of closed run punches, so the edited value flows into the totals that show up in Job View and the reports. The adjustment record and the rebuilt total are two separate things: the adjustment documents the change, the rebuild applies it.

## What Changes When You Correct a Punch

| Surface | Effect |
|---|---|
| The original punch | Unchanged; it stays in the record |
| The Edits filter | Gains an adjustment row with old value, new value, and reason |
| The daily hour breakdown | Recomputed on rebuild to reflect the corrected hours |
| Planner screens | Show the corrected hours once the breakdown is rebuilt |

## How to Check It Worked

The Edits tab shows your adjustment with the reason you typed, and the original punch still appears with its original value. After the rebuild, the affected day's hours match the correction. If the planner's Job View still shows the old number, the breakdown rebuild has not run yet for that schedule.

## Common Mistakes

- **Expecting the original punch to disappear after a correction.** It never does. The adjustment sits alongside it. That is the point of an append-only trail.
- **Editing a punch without rebuilding the daily totals.** The adjustment is recorded but planners keep seeing the old hours until the breakdown rebuilds.
- **Trying to delete a confusing zero-hour punch.** It cannot be deleted. Annotate it with a reason like duplicate tap instead, and coach against double taps.
- **Letting operators attempt corrections.** Beyond the ten-second piece undo, corrections are a supervisor action with a mandatory reason.
