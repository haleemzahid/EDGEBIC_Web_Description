**To block a time window across a whole shift in EDGEBIC by User Solutions, open the Shifts tab, select the shift, and add a downtime event on its Downtime Events tab.** Enter the date, a start and end time, a type, and a description. Because the downtime lives on the shift, every work center that runs that shift loses those hours on that date. It is the right tool for an event that stops the whole crew at once.

This is the shift-level counterpart to blocking a single machine. When the interruption is plant-wide, an all-hands meeting or a power cut, you want the block on the shift so it applies to every resource, not one at a time.

## Before You Start

- **You know the window:** the date and the exact start and end time.
- **You know it hits every machine on the shift.** That is the point.
- **You can rerun the scheduler** so jobs move out of the window.

## Step 1: Open the Shift

Open the **Shifts** tab and select the shift the interruption falls on.

## Step 2: Go to the Downtime Events Tab

Click **Edit** and open the shift's **Downtime Events** tab. Entering the downtime here, on the shift rather than on a machine, is what makes it apply to every work center on the shift.

## Step 3: Add the Downtime Event

Click **Add Downtime**. Enter the **Date** (or date range), the **Start** and **End** time of the window, a **Type**, and a **Description** such as `All-hands meeting` or `Utility power interruption`. Save.

Every work center running this shift now loses that window on that date.

## Step 4: Reschedule

Run **Schedule / Re-Schedule** so the jobs that would have run in the window slide out of it. Adding downtime changes the calendar; it does not move existing bars until you rerun.

## What Changes When You Save

| Surface | Effect |
|---|---|
| Every work center on the shift | Loses the window's hours on that date |
| Other shifts | Unaffected |
| Existing schedules | Not moved until you rerun |
| Capacity for the date | Reduced by the downtime hours per shift |

## How to Check It Worked

1. Reopen the shift's Downtime Events tab and confirm the event is listed for the right date and time.
2. Check capacity for that date across a couple of work centers on the shift and confirm it is reduced by the window.
3. Rerun the scheduler and confirm no work is planned inside the blocked window on that shift.

## Common Mistakes

**Blocking one machine when the whole crew stops.** Machine downtime pauses one work center. For a plant-wide event, enter the downtime on the shift instead.

**Using a downtime event for a full day.** Downtime carves hours out of a shift and is meant for partial-day windows. A whole day off is cleaner as a shift holiday or plant holiday.

**Forgetting to rerun.** The calendar shows the block, but existing bars stay until a reschedule moves them.
