**To block a time window across a whole shift in EDGEBIC by User Solutions, add a partial plant holiday for that window.** Open the **Daily Hours** tab, then the **Holiday** tab, click **Add Holiday**, untick **Closes the whole day**, and enter the start and end time. The scheduler charges each shift only its own overlap with the window, which is what makes one plant-wide record behave correctly shift by shift.

This is the right tool for an interruption that stops the whole crew at once: an all-hands meeting, a safety stand-down, a utility power cut. For one machine's maintenance you want a per-day capacity override instead, which is a different page.

## Why a Partial Holiday and Not a Downtime Record

Worth clearing up first, because the instinct is to look for a downtime tab on the shift.

EDGEBIC's scheduling engine understands shift-scoped closures and standalone downtime records, and it honors them if they exist. What the current release does not provide is a screen to create either one. There is no Downtime Events tab on a shift and no downtime editor anywhere else.

The partial plant holiday covers this case properly, and arguably better. Because the engine subtracts the **overlap** between the holiday window and each shift's own hours, a single record lands different amounts on different shifts without you doing anything:

```
blocked hours = max(0, min(shift end, holiday end) − max(shift start, holiday start))
```

A 09:00 to 11:00 meeting against an 08:00 to 16:00 day shift blocks two hours. Against a 16:00 to 00:00 night shift it blocks nothing, because the windows never touch. One entry, correct per shift.

## Before You Start

- **You know the window:** the date and the exact start and end time.
- **You know it hits every machine whose shift overlaps that window.** That is the point, and it is also the limitation covered at the end of this page.
- **You can rerun the scheduler** so jobs move out of the window.

## Step 1: Open the Plant Holidays Page

Open the **Daily Hours** tab, then the inner **Holiday** tab. The **Plant Holidays** page lists closures affecting all work centers and shifts.

## Step 2: Add the Holiday

Click **Add Holiday**. Enter a **Name** that explains the block for the audit trail, such as `All-hands meeting` or `Utility power interruption`, and set the **Date**.

## Step 3: Untick "Closes the Whole Day"

This is the step that turns a full closure into a window. Unticking it reveals the **Start Time** and **End Time** fields.

## Step 4: Enter the Window

Type the start and end time of the interruption, for example `09:00` to `11:00`. Save.

The grid shows **Scope** = **Partial** with the window in the **Window** column. Every shift that overlaps those hours now loses the overlap on that date.

## Step 5: Reschedule

Run **Schedule / Re-Schedule** so the jobs that would have run in the window slide out of it. Adding the holiday changes the calendar; it does not move existing bars until you rerun.

## What Changes When You Save

| Surface | Effect |
|---|---|
| Every shift overlapping the window | Loses its own overlap hours on that date |
| Shifts that do not overlap | Unaffected, automatically |
| Multi-instance work centers | Lose the overlap **per machine**, so the block scales with the size of the resource |
| Existing schedules | Not moved until you rerun |

That third row matters more than it looks. The subtraction happens before the instance multiplier, so a two-hour meeting on a three-machine work center removes six hours of schedulable capacity, exactly as it does in the building.

## How to Check It Worked

1. Reopen the **Plant Holidays** page and confirm the record shows **Scope** = **Partial** with the right date and window.
2. Check capacity for that date across a couple of work centers and confirm it is reduced by the overlap, multiplied by their instance counts.
3. Confirm a shift that should not be affected, such as nights for a morning meeting, still shows its normal hours.
4. Rerun the scheduler and confirm no work is planned inside the blocked window.

## Common Mistakes

**Looking for a downtime tab on the shift.** There isn't one in this release. The partial plant holiday is the mechanism for a crew-wide window.

**Using a partial holiday when only one of two overlapping shifts stops.** The record charges every overlapping shift. If the window straddles both shifts and only one crew is in the meeting, use per-day capacity overrides on the affected shift instead.

**Using a partial holiday for a full day.** Tick **Closes the whole day** instead. Carving 8 hours out of an 8-hour shift works, but the whole-day flag says what you mean.

**Forgetting to rerun.** The calendar shows the block, but existing bars stay until a reschedule moves them.
