**The standup dashboard tab in EDGEBIC by User Solutions is built for the morning production meeting: a Safety, Quality, Delivery, Cost band, an Andon strip of colored work-center tiles, a clickable late-jobs list, and a bottleneck panel, all in a dark floor-TV theme for a wall screen.** It is the tab you leave up on a monitor and read from top to bottom to run a ten-minute standup.

It is one of four cockpit tabs, and the wider tour is in [the EDGEBIC dashboard tabs](/blog/how-to-use-the-edgebic-dashboard-tabs), while the same self-declaring data-quality idea runs through [EDGEBIC dashboards explained](/blog/edgebic-dashboards-explained). For the strategic 90-day view, see [the executive dashboard](/blog/how-to-use-the-executive-dashboard-in-edgebic). Every task in this library is mapped on the [EDGEBIC how-to hub](/blog/edgebic-how-to-hub).

## Before You Start

- Today's jobs are scheduled. The standup tab reads today's schedules and orders.
- Operators are logging starts and finishes through the kiosk. The Andon colors are inferred from those actuals.
- You accept there is no auto-refresh. Click Refresh now at the start of the meeting.

## Step 1: Open the Standup Tab

Open the Dashboard section from the main navigation and click the **Standup** tab. It switches automatically to the dark floor-TV theme, which is tuned for a wall-mounted display. Connect it to your floor monitor and click **Refresh now** to load today's picture.

## Step 2: Read the SQDC Band

The top band follows the lean Safety, Quality, Delivery, Cost mnemonic:

- **Safety** and **Quality** currently show a dash with an Offline chip, because there is no incident or defect log to compute them from yet.
- **Delivery** shows on-time performance and carries a Plan chip.
- **Cost** shows a variance figure and carries a Live chip.

The dashes are deliberate honesty: the board states what it cannot yet compute rather than inventing a number.

## Step 3: Read the Andon Strip

Each active work center is one colored tile. Green means running on plan, yellow means the elapsed time has run past 125 percent of the planned duration, red means the station is past its planned start with no logged start, idle means nothing is active or imminent, blue means a material wait, and white means planned downtime. Because the state is inferred from schedule actuals rather than machine sensors, a tile is only as accurate as the promptness of kiosk logging on that station.

## Step 4: Work the Late Jobs and Bottleneck Panel

The late-jobs list ranks the jobs behind their due date. Click a row to jump straight to that job's Gantt for a closer look. The bottleneck panel shows the constraint station's load today and its seven-day average, which is the number to drive the standup off. A buffer figure of zero on that panel is a not-yet-populated placeholder; read the load percentages, not the buffer.

## How to Check It Worked

The tab is read-only, so nothing is saved. Confirm it worked by checking that a station you know is running shows green on the Andon strip after a refresh. If it shows red, check whether the operator logged the start through the kiosk; an unlogged start paints the tile red even while the machine runs.

## Common Mistakes

- **Distrusting a surprising red tile.** A red usually means the start was not logged, not that the job stopped. Check the kiosk before doubting the board.
- **Reading the Safety or Quality dash as broken.** They are honest Offline states until the underlying logs exist.
- **Over-reading the buffer figure.** It is a placeholder at zero. Drive the standup off the load percentages instead.
- **Forgetting to refresh.** There is no auto-refresh timer. A stale board shows the last activation, not the current shift.
