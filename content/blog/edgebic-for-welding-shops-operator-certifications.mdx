**Welding shop scheduling software that only checks whether a booth is open will happily assign Inconel TIG to a shift where no one is qualified to run it, and the shop finds out at delivery.** EDGEBIC by User Solutions gates each weld by the process and material certification it requires, so a job is scheduled only when both the equipment and a qualified, rostered welder are available. For a fabrication shop where the constraint is people, not machines, that is the difference between a plan the floor trusts and a plan it quietly ignores.

## The Constraint Is the Welder, Not the Booth

Most fabrication shops have more booths than certified welders for any given procedure. A shop might run three TIG stations but hold only one welder qualified on Inconel and one on aluminum. A scheduler that counts booths sees three units of capacity and books three jobs. The floor sees one qualified person and one of those jobs is late before it starts.

The fix is to schedule against the qualification, not the equipment. In EDGEBIC, operators carry skills and a weld step can require a certification. The scheduler places that step only on a shift where a qualified, rostered welder is present, so the plan reflects the real depth of your certified workforce. The mechanics of how a skill constrains placement are documented in [how operator skills gate work center assignment](/blog/how-operator-skills-gate-work-center-assignment).

## A Worked Thursday

Consider a queue for Thursday: three Inconel weldments and five mild-steel weldments. Joe is the only welder certified on Inconel, and Joe is on approved leave Thursday.

A booth-counting scheduler assigns all eight jobs to the TIG work center, sees three open stations, and prints a plan that runs the Inconel work Thursday. Nobody qualified is present. The three Inconel jobs slip silently and the customer hears about it at delivery.

With certifications modeled, the scheduler sees zero Inconel-qualified welders rostered Thursday. The three Inconel jobs slide to Friday when Joe returns, or to Wednesday if room exists, and the five mild-steel jobs run Thursday across the other welders. The plan the foreman prints is the plan the floor can actually run.

| Job type | Qualified welders | Thursday roster | Scheduler result |
|---|---|---|---|
| Inconel TIG (x3) | Joe only | Joe on leave | Slid to Friday |
| Mild steel TIG (x5) | Three welders | Two present | Run Thursday in parallel |

## Certifications That Expire

A weld procedure qualification is not permanent. It lapses, and a plan that assumes a credential nobody currently holds is a compliance problem waiting to surface. A certification in EDGEBIC can carry an expiry date, and once it lapses the operator drops out of eligibility for the steps that require it.

The schedule stops assigning that welder to the certified work on its own, so a requalification gap never becomes a plan built on an expired credential. When the welder recertifies, they re-enter eligibility and the schedule can use them again. The plan stays aligned with who is qualified this week, which is the only version that matters on the floor.

## Skill Depth Becomes Visible Capacity

Modeling certifications turns an invisible constraint into a planning number. If only one welder holds the aluminum qualification, aluminum capacity is one welder deep no matter how many booths exist. The scheduler will not stack three days of aluminum work into one shift the single qualified person cannot cover, and the plan shows aluminum as the genuine bottleneck it is.

That visibility is what makes a training decision concrete. When the plan repeatedly slides aluminum work because one person is the whole constraint, you have the evidence to cross-train a second welder, and you can see in a fresh schedule exactly how much the added depth relieves the queue. This is the same operator and skill model described in [operator and skill scheduling explained](/blog/edgebic-operator-and-skill-scheduling-explained).

## Keeping the Right Person on the Job

Skill gating decides who is allowed to run a step. Sometimes you also need a specific person to stay with a job. A multi-pass weldment where welder continuity is part of the quality record should not be handed to a different qualified welder mid-weld just because the schedule shifted.

An operator can be pinned to a job, and the pin holds through a reschedule. A delay that re-times the surrounding work does not quietly reassign the weldment to someone else. The qualified welder who started it stays with it, which is exactly what a traceable weld record expects.

## Feeding the Schedule From the Floor

Certified capacity is only trustworthy if the roster and the hours behind it are real. Welders log actual start and end on a shop-floor kiosk, so the schedule knows who ran what and for how long, and a job that took longer than the standard is captured as a measured actual rather than an assumption. On a reschedule, completed welds with recorded actual dates are never moved, so only the remaining work re-times. The workflow is covered in [shop-floor actuals tracking](/blog/edgebic-for-machine-shops-actuals-tracking).
