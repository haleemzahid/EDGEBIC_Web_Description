**A schedule change log is the audit trail that records every change to a job's production plan, from the first save through every reschedule, work-center swap, date override, completion, and reopening, each stamped with the reason it happened.** It is the schedule's history book, so a planner can always answer "why does this job look the way it does?"

## How it works

Every meaningful change to a plan produces a change log entry, and each entry carries a reason drawn from a fixed set. The reasons are deliberately specific so the history reads clearly rather than as a wall of "something changed."

The reasons are: Initial, for the first time a plan is persisted; Reschedule, for a change the engine produces during a scheduling run; a work-center swap, for a drag-drop move to a different machine; a date override, for a planner dragging a single bar to a new time; Closed, for a job being marked complete; and Reopened, for a completed job being put back into progress. A cancellation reason is reserved for order cancellations.

Because each entry is written after its change lands, the log is append-only history. It never alters the plan; it explains the plan. That separation is what makes it trustworthy. The scheduling engine, the Gantt drag, and the completion action each do the real work, and each leaves a labeled footprint behind.

## A concrete example

Think of the maintenance booklet in a car's glovebox. Every service writes one dated line: oil change, brake pads, timing belt. Nobody edits an old line; you just read down the list to understand the car's history. A schedule change log is that booklet for a job.

Take a job that starts life planned on Monday. The first save writes an Initial entry. On Tuesday a machine goes down and the engine replans the shop, producing a Reschedule entry for this job with a new end date. On Wednesday a planner decides the finishing step should run on a different machine and drags it there, writing a work-center swap entry. On Friday the operator finishes the job, writing a Closed entry.

Read those four lines in order and the job's whole week is legible: planned, replanned by a breakdown, hand-moved to another machine, then completed. No line contradicts another, and none was edited after the fact.

## How EDGEBIC uses it

In EDGEBIC, the schedule change log surfaces in the Job Audit Trail report, where entries are filtered and color-coded by reason. That lets a planner scan for one class of event, for example every manual date override in a period, without wading through routine engine reschedules.

The distinction between engine-driven and human-driven changes is the practical value. An Initial or Reschedule entry came from a scheduling run; a work-center swap or a date override came from a person acting on the Gantt; a Closed or Reopened entry came from the actuals workflow. When a schedule looks surprising, the log tells you which category of decision produced it, so you investigate the right thing.

Because completed work is never moved by a reschedule, the log also gives you a clean boundary: once a job is Closed, later runs will not silently disturb it, and if it ever does change state, a Reopened entry makes that explicit.
