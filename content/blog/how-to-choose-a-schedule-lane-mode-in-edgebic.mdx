**To choose a schedule lane mode in EDGEBIC by User Solutions, set the View by dropdown on the Schedule View Configuration screen.** It decides what the rows of the Schedule View Gantt represent, and there are five options: **WorkCenter**, **ResourceGroup**, **Job**, **Planner** and **Operator**.

Every mode reads the same scheduled operations. Nothing is recalculated, no operation moves, and no data differs between them. What changes is the axis, which is another way of saying what changes is which question the board answers in one glance.

## Before You Start

- At least one job scheduled, so there are operations to lane.
- For **ResourceGroup**, your work center groups set up. See [how to create a work center group](/blog/how-to-create-work-center-groups-in-edgebic).
- For **Operator**, operators and crew assignments in the window you are looking at.

## Step by Step

1. Open the **Schedule** tab, then the **Schedule View** tab.
2. Open the **Configuration** sub-tab, which sits beside **Scheduler**.
3. Stay on the **General** tab and find the **Detail Schedule Resources** panel.
4. Set the **View by:** dropdown to the mode you want.
5. Click **Apply & Save** at the bottom of the screen.
6. Return to the **Scheduler** sub-tab. The board rebuilds with the new lanes.

The choice is saved with the rest of your Schedule View configuration and stays put between sessions, so switching modes is a decision rather than a glance.

## The Five Modes

| Option | What the lanes become | Reach for it when |
|---|---|---|
| **WorkCenter** | One lane per work center. The historical default | You are asking what a machine is doing, or hunting for a machine with room |
| **ResourceGroup** | One lane per work center group; member operations roll up into the group's lane | The conversation is about a pool's total capacity rather than about one specific machine |
| **Job** | One lane per job; the whole job draws as a single bar you can drag to pin it | You want to see whole jobs against each other on one time axis |
| **Planner** | Job lanes on top, the full machine block underneath | You are asking where else an operation could run, and what that machine is already carrying |
| **Operator** | One lane per operator with crew assignments in the window, plus an **Unassigned** lane | You are planning people rather than machines |

## Choosing in Ten Seconds

**WorkCenter** is the right default and the right answer most of the time. It is the machine's-eye view: every lane is a resource, every bar is work booked to it. When somebody asks whether the mill is free on Thursday, this is the board. Reading it well is covered in [reading the work center lanes](/blog/reading-the-work-center-lanes-on-the-edgebic-schedule).

**ResourceGroup** trades machine detail for pool clarity. If four mills are genuinely interchangeable, seeing them as one lane with one combined load answers a capacity question faster than four lanes do. It is the wrong board when you need to pick a specific machine, because the members are behind the roll-up. Background in [EDGEBIC work center groups explained](/blog/edgebic-work-center-groups-explained).

**Job** draws each job as one consolidated bar, so twelve jobs are twelve bars on one axis and their relative spans are immediately readable. There are no machine lanes on this board at all, which is exactly why it is clean and exactly why you cannot reassign anything from it.

**Planner** is the one to reach for when the question spans both. It keeps the machine block intact and adds a block of job lanes above it, one lane per job, with **one bar per operation** rather than one bar per job. So you read the routing along the top, read every machine's load below, and drag between the two to assign work. Full tour in [the Planner view explained](/blog/the-planner-view-in-edgebic-explained).

**Operator** shifts the axis from machines to people, with an **Unassigned** lane for work that has no crew on it yet. Use it when the constraint you are planning around is who is available rather than what is available.

## What Changes When You Apply

The board rebuilds with a new set of lanes and every operation re-draws on whichever lane its new axis puts it on. That is the whole effect on the plan, which is to say none.

Two settings-level details are worth knowing. The **Detail Schedule Resources** grid follows the mode: it lists the rows relevant to the axis you chose, and in **Planner** mode it lists job rows first and then work center rows, with its key column reading **Job / WC Id**. In every mode the grid gives you **Show** to hide a lane, **Seq.No** to order lanes, **Value** to rename a lane's caption, and **Color Code** to tint it.

In **Planner** mode specifically, **Seq.No** orders rows **within their own block**. A job can never be numbered below the machines and a machine can never be lifted above the jobs, however you number them.

## How to Check It Worked

Go back to the **Scheduler** sub-tab and read the lane captions. On **WorkCenter** they are machine names; on **Job** they are job numbers; on **Planner** you should see job numbers on the upper rows and machine names below them; on **Operator** you should see people plus an **Unassigned** row.

If the board looks unchanged, the most likely cause is that **Apply & Save** was not clicked. The dropdown lives on the configuration screen and the change does not reach the board until it is applied.

## Common Mistakes

- **Setting the dropdown and navigating away.** Click **Apply & Save** before leaving the **Configuration** sub-tab.
- **Expecting the schedule to change.** It cannot. Lane modes organize, they do not plan.
- **Using ResourceGroup to pick a specific machine.** Members roll up behind the group's lane. Use **WorkCenter** or **Planner** when the machine identity matters.
- **Using Job mode to move one routing step.** It draws whole jobs as single bars. **Planner** is the mode that keeps every operation as its own bar.
- **Switching modes on a shared configuration without telling anyone.** The setting persists, so it is a shared decision when the configuration is shared.
