**Operator certification tracking prevents a lapse by treating each certification as scheduling data with an expiry date, so the plan refuses to assign a certified job to an operator whose credential has run out.** EDGEBIC by User Solutions lets a routing step require a skill, records each operator's certifications with their expiry dates, and only assigns operators who hold the required skill on the day the work runs. A lapse that used to surface as an audit finding after the fact now surfaces as a visible scheduling gap before the work is due.

This post is about the compliance-risk outcome. It sits under the [EDGEBIC results guide](/blog/edgebic-results-guide). For the underlying model, see [operator and skill scheduling explained](/blog/edgebic-operator-and-skill-scheduling-explained).

## The Lapse Nobody Catches Until It Is Too Late

Certifications expire quietly. A coded welder's qualification, an inspector's calibration authority, a machinist's process certification: each has a renewal date, and each keeps working normally right up to the day it lapses and beyond, because nothing on the floor changes at midnight when the credential expires.

The failure mode is that the schedule keeps assigning the person to the certified work. The binder in the quality office knows the cert expired last Tuesday. The plan does not, so it puts the lapsed welder on the coded weld Wednesday morning, and the non-compliant part is made, inspected, and shipped before anyone connects the expiry date to the assignment. The knowledge and the prevention live in two different places, and the gap between them is where the risk sits.

## Certification as Scheduling Data

The fix is to move the certification out of the binder and into the plan. When a certification is scheduling data, three things become true:

- **A routing step can require a skill.** The operation is gated: only an operator who holds that skill can run it. See [how to require a skill on a routing step](/blog/how-to-require-a-skill-on-a-routing-step-in-edgebic).
- **An operator carries certifications with expiry dates.** The credential is not just present or absent; it is valid until a date. See [how to certify an operator with an expiry date](/blog/how-to-certify-an-operator-with-an-expiry-date-in-edgebic).
- **The scheduler checks validity on the run day.** An operator only qualifies for the operation if their certification is current on the day the work actually runs, not merely on the day the plan was built.

That third property is what closes the loop. A certification expiring next month is fine for work that runs this week and disqualifying for work that runs after the expiry, and the scheduler knows the difference because it checks against the run date. For the concept, see [what is an operator certification in scheduling](/blog/what-is-an-operator-certification-in-scheduling).

## What Happens at the Boundary

The interesting behavior is at the expiry boundary, where the value shows up as a gap rather than a violation:

| Situation | Old outcome | With skill-gated scheduling |
|---|---|---|
| Cert current, work this week | Runs fine | Runs fine |
| Cert expires next week, work after | Lapsed person runs it anyway | Operation stops resolving to that person |
| No qualified operator at all | Job runs uncertified, unnoticed | Operation will not schedule, gap is visible |

The plant does not get a silent non-compliant run. It gets a visible signal: this operation cannot be staffed by a qualified operator on this date. That signal arrives before the work is due, which is exactly when a renewal or a reassignment is still possible. A gap you can see is a decision you can make; a lapse you cannot see is a finding you will explain later.

## Why This Is a Scheduling Job, Not an HR Job

It is tempting to treat certification tracking as a human-resources matter, and the renewal paperwork is. But the moment a lapse causes harm is the moment a person is assigned to work they are no longer qualified for, and assignment is a scheduling act. HR can know the expiry date perfectly and still not prevent the assignment, because HR does not build tomorrow's plan.

When the certification lives in the scheduling data, the plan itself enforces the rule. It is the difference between knowing a cert expired and preventing it from being used, and only the second one keeps a non-compliant part off the truck. This is the compliance-risk counterpart to [how skills data targets cross-training spend](/blog/how-skills-data-targets-cross-training-spend), which uses the same skill model for a different outcome.

## The Evidence Trail

The control is also provable. Because a skill-gated step only accepts a qualified operator, the plan could not assign an uncertified person, and because the floor's actuals record who ran the operation, you can show who did the work. An auditor asking how you guarantee only certified people ran regulated operations gets two pieces of evidence: the gate that prevented the wrong assignment and the actuals that recorded the right one.

That structural control is worth more than a procedural one. A supervisor catching a lapse is a person who might miss it; a schedule that refuses the assignment does not have an off day. The heritage lineage includes exactly the regulated work where this matters, from US Navy overhaul programs to medical device and aerospace manufacturing across the User Solutions install base. For expiry and renewal handling, see [skill expiry and recertification](/blog/skill-expiry-and-recertification-in-edgebic).

## What Skill Visibility Cannot Do

**It cannot renew the certification for you.** The plan refuses to schedule lapsed work and surfaces the gap early, but somebody still has to arrange the recertification. What the software buys is the warning, in time to act on it.

**It cannot verify the certification is real.** The system trusts the data you enter. If a credential is recorded as valid when it is not, the gate passes work it should stop. The control is only as honest as the certification records behind it.

**It cannot certify skill on the day it matters.** A certification is a claim about qualification, not proof of competence on this specific part. The gate ensures a qualified person is assigned; it does not guarantee a good weld. Process control is a separate discipline.

**It cannot staff a plant that has no qualified operators.** If nobody holds the skill, the gate correctly refuses to schedule the work, which surfaces a real capacity and training problem rather than papering over it. That is the honest outcome, but it is still a problem you have to solve with people.
