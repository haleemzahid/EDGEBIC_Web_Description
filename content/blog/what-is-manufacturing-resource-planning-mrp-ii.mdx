**Manufacturing resource planning, or MRP-II, extends basic material requirements planning with capacity checks, financial rollup, and closed-loop feedback from the shop floor.** Where MRP-I only calculates what materials are needed and when, MRP-II also asks whether the machine hours exist, whether the cash exists to pay suppliers, and whether the sales forecast agrees, then feeds shop floor actuals back into the plan. It is the step from a materials calculation to a coordinated, capacity-aware production plan.

## How it works

Start with MRP-I, the material half. Given a demand like "100 bicycles by Friday," it works backward through the bill of materials, nets against what is already in stock and on order, and time-offsets the result to suggest that you make or buy 200 tyres by Wednesday. It is pure material arithmetic, and it assumes infinite capacity: it never asks whether the wheel-building line has the hours to do the work.

MRP-II adds the questions MRP-I skips. Do we actually have the machine hours to build those wheels in that window? Do we have the cash to pay the tyre supplier? Does the sales forecast that drove the demand still hold? And critically, it closes the loop: as the shop floor reports what really happened, that feedback flows back into the plan, which is recomputed so the remaining work reflects reality instead of the original assumptions.

The single most important addition is finite capacity. MRP-I hands you order due dates that ignore the machines; MRP-II, through capacity requirements planning and finite scheduling, places the work into real available hours and tells you whether the dates are achievable. That is the difference between a plan that looks feasible on paper and one that the floor can actually run.

## A concrete example

An MRP-I run for a bicycle order calculates that 200 tyres are needed by Wednesday and 100 frames by Thursday, and it schedules all the wheel-building for Wednesday because the material math allows it. On paper the plan is complete and the due dates line up.

The floor tells a different story. The wheel-building line has one shift, and Wednesday's suggested load is two shifts of work. MRP-II catches this at the capacity check: it places the wheel-building into the line's real hours, sees the overload, and pushes part of the work to Thursday with an honest revised date. When the line then runs an hour slow on the day, the closed loop feeds that actual back, and the plan updates the downstream steps rather than pretending the original Wednesday finish still holds. The plan stays connected to reality from calculation through execution.

## How EDGEBIC uses it
