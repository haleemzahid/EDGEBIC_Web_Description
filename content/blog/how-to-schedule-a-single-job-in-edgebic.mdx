**To schedule a single job in EDGEBIC, tick only that row on the Drive Schedule tab and press the schedule button, or open the Job View tab and click Re-Schedule.** Either path re-plans that one job while every other job keeps its dates and its reserved capacity. This is the most useful discipline in the whole product: narrow scope means fewer bars move, and a plan that does not churn is a plan the shop floor believes. EDGEBIC by User Solutions makes the scope explicit at every step.

## Before You Start

- The job exists on the **Drive Schedule** grid, scheduled or not.
- At least one active shift exists, otherwise the run stops and offers to create one.
- The job's product has a routing. An order with no routing is reported as a failure and dropped from the run.
- Work center calendars and capacity are current, because the pass plans against them as they are right now.

## Path A: From the Drive Schedule Grid

1. **Open the Schedule area** and select the **Drive Schedule** tab.
2. **Tick the Select checkbox on that one row.** Untick everything else. If a filter is active, make sure the row you ticked is the row you think it is.
3. **Press the schedule button.**
4. **Read the Confirm Scheduling dialog.** It states how many jobs are new (first-time scheduling) and how many existing jobs will be rescheduled. For a single job it should read 1. If it reads more, answer no and check your selection.
5. **Confirm** and watch the progress overlay. The grid is locked while the pass runs, and the **Cancel** button is always safe: nothing partial is kept.

## Path B: From the Job View Tab

1. **Open the Job View tab** and pick the job in the **Select Job** dropdown.
2. **Click Re-Schedule** on the control bar.
3. **Confirm the prompt**, which asks whether to re-schedule that job and states that it will clear and regenerate the schedule for it.
4. **Read the completion message** confirming the job was re-scheduled.

Path B is the better habit when you are already looking at the job's operations, because there is no selection to get wrong.

## What Changes When You Save

| Surface | Effect of a single-job run |
|---|---|
| That job's operations | Cleared and re-planned into real shift hours on real work centers. |
| Its recorded work | Completed and in-progress steps are preserved exactly. Only remaining work is re-planned, resuming from the latest actual. |
| Every other job | Untouched. Bars stay, capacity stays reserved, and your job is planned around them. |
| A blank due date | Filled once from the job's first planned end, then never moved. |
| The job's routing snapshot | Frozen with the job on its first schedule, so later edits to the master routing do not silently rewrite a running job. |
| Gantt, dashboards, reports | All refresh to the new plan when the pass completes. |

That fourth row is worth dwelling on. Because unselected jobs keep their reservations, a single-job run does not get to use hours that other jobs already claimed. Your job fits into the genuinely free space, which is exactly the answer you want when you are asking "can this one job move without breaking anything else".

## How to Check It Worked

On the **Drive Schedule** row, read **Start Date**, **Item Start**, **Job End** and **Days Late**. Then spot-check a neighbor: pick a job you did not touch and confirm its dates are identical to what they were before. If a job you did not select moved, your scope was wider than you intended.

On the **Job View** tab, the per-operation grid shows **Sched Start**, **Sched End**, **Actual Start** and **Actual End** per step. Recorded actuals should be unchanged; only the remaining steps should carry new planned times.

## Common Mistakes

**Pressing the button with nothing ticked.** The **Schedule All Orders** prompt then offers to include every order, and answering yes turns a one-job task into a plant-wide reshuffle. Tick first, or answer no.

**Ticking while a filter is active.** Selection follows the rows on screen. If you filter, tick, then change the filter, verify what the confirm dialog says before you commit.

**Blaming the run for moving completed work.** It cannot. Recorded work is never moved. What moved is the remaining work, resuming from the actual finish rather than from the old plan. Compare **Actual Start** and **Actual End** on the operation rows and the picture becomes clear.

**Re-running one job repeatedly to chase a date.** If it lands later than you want, the constraint is upstream of the run. Work the checklist in [how to see why a job is late](/blog/how-to-see-why-a-job-is-late-in-edgebic) instead of running again and hoping.
