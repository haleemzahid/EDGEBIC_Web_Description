**To deactivate a product category in EDGEBIC by User Solutions, open the manage categories window from the product toolbar, edit the category, untick its active flag, and save.** It disappears from the category picker used on the product form. Nothing else changes: products that already carry the category keep it, and the grouping still shows in grids and reports.

Categories go stale faster than most master data. A plant reorganizes its product families, a sales-driven grouping is replaced by a manufacturing one, or somebody creates a category during a slow afternoon that nobody else ever uses. Deactivating is how you clean the picker without editing history.

## Before You Start

- **Know why the category is going.** Replaced by another, no longer meaningful, or created by mistake. If it is being replaced, know the replacement before you start.
- **Know how many products use it.** The product grid can be grouped or filtered by category, which gives you the count in one click.
- **Decide whether the products move.** Deactivating and reassigning are separate jobs and you can do either first.
- **Remember reports.** Historic grouping in a report reflects the assignments on the products, not the active flag on the category.

## Step 1: Open the Category Manager

Open the **Product** tab and click **Manage Categories** on the toolbar. The **Manage Product Categories** window opens, listing the categories you already have with **Edit** and **Delete** controls on each row.

If you are creating rather than retiring, [how to create a product category in EDGEBIC](/blog/how-to-create-a-product-category-in-edgebic) covers the other direction.

## Step 2: Count the Products First

Before you touch anything, close the window and use the toolbar's **Category** filter on the product grid to show only the products in the category you are retiring.

You are answering one question: is this an empty grouping or a live one? An empty category is a clean five-second deactivation. A category with 40 products behind it is a small project, and knowing that before you start is the difference between a tidy retirement and a half-finished one.

## Step 3: Reassign the Products, If They Are Moving

With the grid filtered to the category, open each product, set the **Category** dropdown to the replacement, and save. The picker also carries a control to create a category on the spot if the replacement does not exist yet.

Products with no natural home can be left with no category. That state is legitimate and reads as no category in the grid.

Work the filtered list down until it is empty. That is the honest definition of finished.

## Step 4: Deactivate the Category

Reopen **Manage Categories**, use **Edit** on the category's row, untick the **Active** flag, and save.

The category stops appearing in the picker on new and edited products. Every assignment already made stays exactly where it is.

Close the window.

## Step 5: Reinstate If You Change Your Mind

Deactivation is a single tick, and so is undoing it. Edit the category again, tick **Active**, and save. It returns to the picker with its name, code, and description intact, and every product that still carries it lines up under it again.

That reversibility is the argument for deactivating rather than deleting. The deleting option is there on the row, but it is blocked while active products reference the category, and there is no equivalent one-tick undo.

## How to Check It Worked

1. **Open a product and check the picker.** The retired category should not be selectable.
2. **Check the toolbar filter.** Whether a deactivated category still appears as a filter value depends on the products that carry it, so use this to confirm your reassignment work actually finished.
3. **Group the product grid by category.** A retired grouping with rows still under it tells you the reassignment pass is incomplete.
4. **Open a report that groups by category** and confirm the numbers still make sense to the people who read it.

## Common Mistakes

**Deactivating and calling the reorganization done.** The picker is clean, the catalog is not. Products keep the old grouping until somebody changes them one at a time.

**Reassigning without checking the whole list.** Filter, work the list to empty, then deactivate. Doing it by memory leaves two or three parts behind every time.

**Creating a near-duplicate instead of reactivating.** If a retired category is needed again, tick its active flag rather than typing a similar name. Two categories that mean the same thing make every grouped report misleading, and category names must be unique anyway.

**Reaching for delete because the list is long.** A long category list is a naming problem, not a deletion problem. Retire what is genuinely dead and leave the rest.

**Assuming reports change.** They report what the products say. Retiring the category changes nothing until the products move.
