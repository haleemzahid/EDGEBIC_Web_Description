**To pause a job with a reason code in EDGEBIC by User Solutions, tap PAUSE on the shop floor terminal, pick one of the four reason categories, then pick the specific reason.** The run punch closes and a paused punch opens carrying that reason. The point of the extra two taps is that paused time never becomes production hours on the operation, so downtime is recorded without polluting anyone's efficiency numbers.

The rest of the operator terminal workflow is in the [shop floor execution guide](/blog/edgebic-shop-floor-guide). This is the pause.

## Before You Start

| Prerequisite | Why |
|---|---|
| The operation is in a run punch | Pause closes an open run; there is nothing to pause from an idle screen |
| Reason codes exist for the category you need | The picker offers active codes; if the right words are missing, the reason recorded will be a compromise |
| You know which of the four categories fits | The category choice is what makes the downtime data sortable later |

## Step 1: Tap PAUSE

Tap **PAUSE** on the running screen. The run punch closes at that moment, timestamped from the shared clock. Nobody types a time.

## Step 2: Pick the Category

The **WHY ARE YOU PAUSING?** picker opens with four category tiles:

| Category | What belongs here |
|---|---|
| **MACHINE** | Breakdown, tooling failure, alarm, coolant, unplanned maintenance |
| **MATERIAL** | Waiting on stock, wrong material delivered, incoming defect |
| **QUALITY** | Inspection hold, process problem, rework decision pending |
| **WAITING** | Operator, crane, fixture, instruction: anything the machine is idle for that is not the three above |

This is the same four-way taxonomy the scrap reason picker uses, which is why one glance at a work center's history tells you whether it is an equipment problem or a supply problem.

## Step 3: Pick the Specific Reason

Choose the reason underneath the category. The paused punch carries it. The screen now shows the paused state and confirms the reason was recorded.

## Step 4: Tap RESUME When Work Restarts

Tap **RESUME**. The paused punch closes, a fresh run punch opens, and the elapsed clock restarts. From the operation's point of view nothing was lost: production hours resume accumulating, and the gap between them is on the record with its reason attached.

If the operation is actually finished rather than paused, **COMPLETE** is available directly from the paused screen. Completing from a pause runs the same checks as completing from a run, described in [how to complete an operation](/blog/how-to-complete-an-operation-in-edgebic).

## What Changes When You Pause

| Surface | Effect |
|---|---|
| The operation's production hours | Unchanged: paused time is excluded from run hours |
| Downtime reporting | The paused stretch is recorded with its category and specific reason |
| Shift handoff summary | The pause appears in the recent pauses list with its reason label |
| The history drawer | The punch is visible with its open and close timestamps and the operator's name |
| The schedule | Nothing moves until a reschedule runs; actuals record reality, re-planning is a separate decision |

## How to Check It Worked

The running screen shows the paused state rather than a counting clock, and the piece counters have stopped accepting taps (they only increment during a run, which is often how an operator notices they never resumed). Open the **Handoff** summary: the shift's hours split into setup, run, and down buckets, and your pause shows up under recent pauses with the reason you picked. On the planner side, the operation's daily actual hours reflect run time only.

## Common Mistakes

- **Forgetting to resume.** A punch left paused stops accumulating production hours, so the day's numbers come out short and nobody notices until the operation looks stalled. If the counters will not increment, check whether you are still paused.
- **Forgetting to pause.** The opposite failure and the more expensive one: an open run punch through a four-hour breakdown books four hours of production time that never happened. Pause first, diagnose second.
- **Using WAITING as a catch-all.** It is the least actionable category. A broken spindle tapped as waiting sends maintenance nowhere and shows up in the Pareto as a supply-chain problem.
- **Correcting a closed punch on the floor.** Operators cannot edit closed punches by design. Corrections are a supervisor action in the history drawer, and every one requires a reason and is kept alongside the original.
- **Expecting the pause to re-plan the job.** It does not. Log through the shift, then reschedule.
