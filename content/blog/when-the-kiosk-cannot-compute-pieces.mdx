**When a routing step has no usable cycle time, the EDGEBIC by User Solutions kiosk computes an effective rate of zero and records piece fields as zero rather than inventing a number.** The hours keep working perfectly, because they come from a clock rather than a calculation. The pieces stay honestly blank. That choice, refusing to fabricate, is worth understanding because the alternative would quietly corrupt every downstream number that touches output.

Here is where the rate comes from, what a zero costs you, and how to fix the routing that caused it.

## Where the Rate Comes From

Hours and pieces are two views of the same work, and EDGEBIC converts between them with an effective rate expressed in pieces per hour:

```
effective rate = (pieces per unit / hours per unit) x work center efficiency factor
```

Both inputs on the left come from the routing step, and the efficiency factor comes from the work center. The rate is calculated and snapshotted onto each punch at the moment the punch opens. The conversion itself is covered in [how hours and pieces convert on the shop floor](/blog/how-hours-and-pieces-convert-on-the-floor).

If hours per unit is zero, the division has no answer. If pieces per unit is zero, the numerator is zero. Either way the rate is zero, and there is no rate to convert with.

## What Zero Actually Costs

The honest answer is: less than people fear on most work centers, and quite a lot on some.

| Entry mode | Primary measurement | Effect of a zero rate |
|---|---|---|
| Hours-primary | Elapsed clock | Derived pieces show zero, hours unaffected |
| Pieces-primary | Tapped or entered counts | Counts recorded, hours not derived from them |
| Both | Each captured independently | Variance between them cannot be computed |

On an hours-primary work center, the loss is cosmetic. Punch hours are measured, not calculated, so daily actual hours, the reschedule, plan versus actual variance, and job percent complete all behave exactly as they should. See [how punches roll up into daily actual hours](/blog/how-punches-roll-up-into-daily-actual-hours).

On a pieces-primary work center the loss is real. That entry mode exists because output is the meaningful unit there, and without a rate the counts stand alone rather than feeding an hours figure. Operators can still record piece counts and those counts are kept, but you have a work center configured for one model and a routing that cannot support it. That is a routing to fix rather than a behavior to work around.

## Why Not Just Estimate

It would be easy to fall back to some plausible number: the work center's nominal rate, an average from similar parts, last quarter's observed rate for this product. Every one of those options is worse than zero, and for the same reason.

A fabricated piece count is indistinguishable from a measured one once it is in the record. It flows into the day's output, into the observed rate for that date, into the quality factor of OEE, and into any quoting analysis built on historical output. Nobody looking at the number later can tell that it was a guess. A zero, by contrast, is obviously an absence, and an absence prompts somebody to go find out why.

This is the same principle behind [the auto-filled actuals badge](/blog/the-auto-filled-actuals-badge-explained): EDGEBIC would rather show you that data is missing or assumed than smooth it over. A plan is only as trustworthy as the truth underneath it.

## The Snapshot Rule

The rate is frozen onto each punch when the punch opens, and later routing edits do not reach back.

That matters for two reasons. First, historical reporting stays stable: last quarter's rates are whatever was actually in force last quarter, and correcting a standard today does not silently rewrite them. Second, a fix works immediately in the direction you want. Set the missing cycle time this morning and punches opened this afternoon carry a real rate, while nothing about yesterday's record changes.

The daily rollup follows the same spirit. For a date with both hours and pieces it uses the observed rate, pieces divided by hours, which is the truth about that day. Only when one of them is missing does it fall back to the snapshotted rate from the last closed punch.

## Diagnosing and Fixing It

The symptom is easy to spot: an operation running normally, hours accumulating correctly, and piece fields stubbornly at zero.

Work through the inputs in this order.

**Check hours per unit on the routing step.** This is the usual culprit. A step created quickly, imported from a spreadsheet with a blank column, or copied from a template that never had a cycle time will carry zero here. See [logging actuals from the planner](/blog/logging-actuals-from-the-planner-in-edgebic) if you need to correct the record for work already run.

**Check pieces per unit on the routing step.** Less common, but a zero here produces the same result.

**Check the work center efficiency factor.** A factor of zero would zero out an otherwise valid rate. This is rare and usually a data entry slip.

**Decide whether the step should be pieces-primary at all.** Some operations genuinely have no meaningful piece count: a heat treat soak, a paint cure, an inspection hold. Those belong on hours-primary work centers, and a zero derived piece count there is not a problem to solve.

## A Worked Example

Two operations, same job, same shift.

**Operation 30, CNC-1, hours-primary.** Routing carries 0.14 hours per piece and 1 piece per unit, so the rate is about 7.14 pieces per hour. The operator runs 7.53 hours and taps 42 good pieces. The daily row shows 7.53 hours, 42 pieces, and an observed rate of 5.58 pieces per hour, which is below standard and points at the quality hold recorded on its own punch.

**Operation 40, Cure Oven, hours-primary, no cycle time on the routing.** The rate is zero. The operator punches 4.0 hours and the piece field stays at zero. The daily row shows 4.0 hours and no pieces.

The job's percent complete is computed from hours, so operation 40's blank piece count does not drag it down. The reschedule reads hours and plans the remainder correctly. Nothing about the plan is impaired, and the only thing you cannot say is how many pieces came out of a cure oven, which is not a question worth asking.

## The Bottom Line

A missing cycle time produces a zero rate, and EDGEBIC records a zero rather than a guess. Hours keep working because they are measured; pieces stay blank because they would have to be invented. Fix the routing where output genuinely matters, especially on pieces-primary work centers, and accept the blank where it does not. Rates are snapshotted per punch, so your fix applies forward without disturbing history.
