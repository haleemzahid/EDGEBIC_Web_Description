**A work center group's pooled load lives on the Resource Calendar in EDGEBIC by User Solutions, where each machine pool appears as a summary row you expand into its members, with per-day cells for capacity against booked load.** The group row is a live sum of its member machines, so it reads the pool at a glance while the machines underneath hold the real hours. It is the view you open to answer whether a pool can absorb more work.

If you have not built the pool yet, start with [how to add a machine to a pool](/blog/how-to-add-a-machine-to-a-pool-in-edgebic). This page is the load view for an existing pool, one of many tasks mapped on the [EDGEBIC how-to hub](/blog/edgebic-how-to-hub).

## Before You Start

- The work center group exists and has at least one active member. A group with no members has nothing to sum.
- The member machines have shifts assigned. Capacity on the calendar comes from those shift calendars, and a machine with no shift reads as having no capacity.
- Jobs have been scheduled. Load is the hours the plan booked, so an unscheduled plant shows capacity and no load.

## Step 1: Open the Resource Calendar

Open the **Workcenter** tab from the main navigation. The area has three inner tabs: Workcenters, Workcenter Groups, and **Resource Calendar**. Select Resource Calendar.

The screen lists your groups, each as a summary row with a group badge. This is a reading surface. Nothing you do here edits capacity or moves a job; it only shows what the schedule has already booked against what the calendars make available.

## Step 2: Set the Date Window

Set the window with the **From** and **To** date pickers, or use the **This Week** shortcut for the current week. Choose the span that matches your question: one week to check whether a pool can take a rush order, the next month for a capacity conversation.

The cells refresh to the window, so a narrow window gives you day-by-day detail while a wider one gives you the shape of the load over time.

## Step 3: Expand the Group Row

Click a group to expand it into its member machines. Now you see two levels: the group summary row, which is the live sum, and one row per member with its own per-day capacity and load cells.

Read the summary row first for the pool total, then drop to the members to see where the slack actually sits. A pool that looks comfortable at the group level can hide one member running hot and another idle, which is exactly the imbalance a machine pool is meant to smooth on the next run.

## Step 4: Read Capacity Against Load

Each per-day cell pairs the machine's available capacity with the hours the plan booked on it that day. A member near or above its capacity every day has no room; a member with capacity and little load is where a new job or a re-shopped step can go.

Remember the pool has no capacity of its own. Every hour on a group row belongs to a member machine, so a machine shared between two groups shows the same hours under both group rows. Read group rows as views, not as additional capacity, or you will double-count a shared machine.

## What Changes When You Open It

Nothing. The Resource Calendar is a read-only view of the current schedule and the shift calendars. It never edits a machine, a group, or a booking.

What changes is what you decide next: a per-day capacity override, a job moved on the Gantt, or a scheduler run that re-shops not-yet-started group steps across the pool. None of those happen from this view.

## How to Check It Worked

After a change, such as adding a member or moving a job, re-open the Resource Calendar with the same window. The group summary row should reflect the new pooled total, and a re-shopped step should show its hours land on a different member. If you added a machine to the pool and then ran Drive Schedule, the not-yet-started steps that moved to the new member show up as load on its row where before there was none.

## Common Mistakes

- **Double-counting a shared machine.** A machine in two groups shows its hours under both; the hours exist once.
- **Expecting the group to add capacity.** The pool is a routing choice, not a machine. Capacity is always on the members.
- **Reading pooled slack as schedulability.** If the group or all members are inactive, a bound step still fails despite comfortable load.
- **Using too wide a window for a rush question.** A comfortable month can hide two oversold days. Narrow the window for day-level truth.
