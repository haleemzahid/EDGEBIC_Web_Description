**To run a work center at reduced capacity across a period in EDGEBIC by User Solutions, enter a smaller-than-usual figure in the per-day capacity override for each day in the range.** The scheduler honors the reduced number ahead of the shift formula, so a retooling week, a partial-crew stretch, or a machine limping on temporary parts is planned against what it can really deliver.

Reducing is not blocking. A block sets a machine to zero for a full day, covered in [how to block a machine for a maintenance day](/blog/how-to-block-a-machine-for-a-maintenance-day-in-edgebic). Reducing keeps the machine running at a lower rate. And it is not a single-day tweak either, which is [how to override capacity for one day](/blog/how-to-override-capacity-for-one-day-in-edgebic). This page is the sustained, multi-day, non-zero case. For the resolution order behind every capacity number, read [how EDGEBIC calculates work center capacity](/blog/how-edgebic-calculates-work-center-capacity).

## Before You Start

- **The work center already exists** and normally schedules at full capacity.
- **You know the period and the reduced level:** the start and end dates, and roughly how many hours the machine can offer each day during it.
- **You know why.** Common cases are a retooling or partial-maintenance week, a stretch with one crew instead of two, or a machine running below spec while it waits for parts.

## Step 1: Open the Per-Day Capacity Dialog

On the work center's row, click the **📊** per-day capacity button. The **Per-Day Capacity** dialog opens, showing a stacked bar per day: **Loaded**, **Free**, and **Over capacity**, with a summary card above.

## Step 2: Set the Date Range

Adjust the **From:** and **To:** range to cover the reduced period. The grid below lists every day in the range with its **Shift default (h)** and an editable **Capacity (h)** column.

## Step 3: Enter the Reduced Hours per Day

For each affected day, type the reduced figure in the **Capacity (h)** column. Enter the total hours for the machine that day, not per-instance hours: on a three-instance work center, "each machine gets four hours" is a Capacity of 12. Add a short **Reason**, such as "Retooling" or "One crew," so the audit trail explains the light load. The change saves as you type, and the **Overridden** column ticks for each day you set.

If you want an even spread of a total budget, divide it across the working days. Sixty hours across five days is 12 per day. This mirrors EDGEBIC's internal date-range override, which spreads a total across the available days in a window; the per-day dialog is the reliable way to enter that spread.

## Step 4: Confirm and Close

Check the summary card: **Overridden days** should show the count you set. Click **Close**.

## What Changes When You Save

| Surface | Effect |
|---|---|
| Capacity on the covered days | Drops to your entered figure, ahead of the shift formula |
| Resource Calendar | Shows the reduced bars on refresh |
| Existing schedules | Unchanged until you rerun |
| Next **Drive Schedule** run | Fills only the reduced hours; work that no longer fits slides past the period |

A worked case: a welding shop that normally offers 48 hours a day is set to 12 across a five-day retooling week. The next run places far less work in that week and pushes the rest forward, and every downstream step of the affected jobs moves accordingly.

## How to Check It Worked

1. In the per-day dialog, confirm the **Overridden** tick and the reduced **Capacity (h)** on each day of the range.
2. Open the Resource Calendar and read the machine's capacity across the period. It should show your reduced figures, not the shift default.
3. Run **Drive Schedule** and confirm the machine carries less work during the period and normal load before and after it.

## Common Mistakes

**Entering per-machine hours on a multi-instance work center.** The override is used as-is and is not multiplied by the instance count. Enter machines times hours, or you will get a fraction of the capacity you intended.

**Reducing on the wrong shift.** Overrides are shift-specific. A machine that runs day and night needs the reduced figure entered on both shifts, or the untouched shift keeps promising full hours.

**Forgetting to reset.** A reduced override persists until you clear it. When the crew or the tooling returns, reset the days so the machine goes back to its shift default; otherwise it runs light indefinitely.

**Reaching for a shift edit instead.** For a temporary reduction, an override carries a reason and reverts cleanly. Save shift edits for a permanent change in the machine's pattern.
