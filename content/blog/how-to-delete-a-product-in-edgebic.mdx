**To delete a product in EDGEBIC by User Solutions, open the Product tab, click the trash Remove button in the product's Actions column, and read the Confirm Delete prompt before you accept it. The delete is not blocked by references. It cascades, taking the product's entire Bill of Routing with it and flushing any production schedule rows tied to it.**

That behavior is what makes this task worth thinking about for a minute. Deleting a work center is refused while anything still points at it, so the system stops you. Deleting a product does not stop you. It tells you what it is about to remove and then removes it, and there is no undo.

## Before You Start

- **Decide whether delete is really what you want.** Delete is for records created in error. A product that has been built, quoted, or shipped has history hanging off it.
- **Read the BOR column on the row first.** It tells you whether a routing is part of the cascade.
- **Check whether the product is used as a component elsewhere.** A product can appear as a material step inside another product's routing. The confirmation names this product's own routing and schedule rows, not other routings that consume it.
- **Have a database backup if the product has any history at all.** Restoring from backup is the only route back.

## Step 1: Open the Product Management Grid

Open the **Product** tab from the main navigation. The **Product Management** page lists your catalog with **S.#**, **Product Id**, **BOR**, **Product Name**, **Unit**, **Lead Time**, **Size**, **Sale Price**, **Cost**, **Procured**, **Category**, and an **Actions** column holding **Edit** and **Remove**.

The toolbar above carries the **Category:** filter, **Manage Categories**, **Import**, **Delete Selected**, and **Add Product**. The grid is grouped by category by default, and the search panel filters every column at once, which is the fastest way to find a part number you only half remember.

## Step 2: Read the BOR Column Before You Click Anything

The **BOR** cell tells you what the cascade will take:

| Cell text | What it means for the delete |
|---|---|
| **Active BOR** | A routing exists and goes with the product |
| **No BOR** | Nothing routing-related to lose |
| **⚠️ 2 BORs (Legacy)** in red | Legacy data left more than one routing on this product, and all of them go |

A red legacy row is usually a cleanup task rather than a delete candidate. Consolidating to a single routing is covered in [how to find which products have no routing](/blog/how-to-find-which-products-have-no-routing-in-edgebic), which also explains how to filter the column to find every offender at once.

## Step 3: Delete One Product

Click the trash **Remove** button in the product's row. The **Confirm Delete** prompt appears and spells out the consequences: if the product has a routing, deleting it also deletes its entire Bill of Routing configuration, and if it has been scheduled, the prompt names how many production schedule rows will be flushed.

Read those numbers. They are the clearest signal you will get about whether you are removing a stray test record or a working part of the plant.

## Step 4: Delete Several at Once

Tick the checkbox on each row you want gone, then click **Delete Selected** in the toolbar. Confirm when prompted. This is the right path for a batch of test records created during setup, and the wrong path for anything you have not individually looked at, because a multi-row confirmation is easy to accept without reading.

## Step 5: Or Leave the Record Alone

If the confirmation names a routing you use or schedule rows you recognize, stop. EDGEBIC's own guidance is that old products left in the list are harmless and cost nothing, so the ordinary answer for a discontinued item is to leave it and move it into an obsolete category so it drops out of your working view. For the full retire-without-deleting route, see [how to deactivate a product without losing history](/blog/how-to-deactivate-a-product-without-losing-history-in-edgebic).

## What Changes When You Save

| Situation | Immediate effect | What goes with it |
|---|---|---|
| Product with **No BOR**, never scheduled | Row disappears | Nothing else |
| Product with **Active BOR** | Row disappears | Its entire routing configuration |
| Product that has been scheduled | Row disappears | Its routing plus the production schedule rows tied to it |
| You leave the record in place | Nothing | Nothing. Every reference stays readable |

The product also vanishes from every product dropdown across the application at once, including the order dialog and the routing pickers. This is the mirror image of how a customer delete behaves, where the orders survive and are simply unlinked, as [how to delete a customer](/blog/how-to-delete-a-customer-in-edgebic) covers. Products do not work that way. Their dependents go too.

## How to Check It Worked

1. The product is gone from the **Product Management** grid. Clear the **Category:** filter with the **✕** button and search the Product Id to be certain you are not just filtered away from it.
2. Open the **BOR** tab and confirm the product no longer appears in the routing selector.
3. Check any routing that used the product as a material step. A component row pointing at a product that no longer exists is the one thing worth verifying by eye.
4. Open the order dialog on **Drive Schedule** and confirm the product is absent from the **Product (BOR)** list.

## Common Mistakes

- **Accepting the confirmation without reading it.** The prompt is the only place the cascade is spelled out, and it names real counts rather than generic warnings.
- **Deleting to tidy the list.** Category filtering does the same job with none of the loss.
- **Deleting a product that is a component of something else.** Check the routings that consume it before you remove it, not after.
- **Assuming you can rebuild it.** Recreating the product record is easy. Recreating its routing, and the schedule and cost history that referenced it, is not.
- **Using Delete Selected on rows you have not individually inspected.** Read each row's **BOR** cell first.
