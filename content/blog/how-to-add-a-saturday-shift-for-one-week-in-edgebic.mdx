**To add a Saturday shift for one week in EDGEBIC, put a per-day capacity override on the work centers that will run, not a shift edit.** Open the per-day capacity dialog, select the shift, find the Saturday row, and type the approved hours in the **Capacity (h)** column with a reason. It switches on that one Saturday, for those work centers only, and reverses when you click **Reset**.

Weekend production is off by default. The temptation is to tick Saturday on the shift, but that turns Saturday on forever, for every machine using that shift. The override is the surgical alternative. For the dialog in full generality, read [how to override capacity for one day](/blog/how-to-override-capacity-for-one-day-in-edgebic) from EDGEBIC by User Solutions.

## Before You Start

- **You know which work centers will actually work.** Overtime is rarely the whole plant.
- **You know the approved hours per work center**, expressed as the day total across instances.
- **You know the shift.** Overrides are shift-specific.
- **You can rerun the schedule.** The Saturday hours do nothing until Drive Schedule runs.

## Why an Override, Not a Shift Edit

| | Shift edit | Per-day override |
|---|---|---|
| Scope | Every week the shift runs | The one Saturday you choose |
| Work centers affected | Every one using the shift | Only the ones you override |
| Audit trail | None | Reason field plus an Overridden flag |
| Undo | Untick Saturday again, plantwide | Click Reset on the row |

The override wins on every axis for a one-off. A shift edit is correct only when Saturday becomes a permanent part of the pattern.

## Step 1: Open the Per-Day Capacity Dialog

Open the **Workcenter** tab. On the first work center that will work overtime, click **📊** (tooltip **Per-day capacity**). The **Per-Day Capacity** dialog opens.

Alternate route: in **Schedule → Resource Calendar**, double-click the Saturday cell on the Total Capacity row for the work center.

## Step 2: Select the Shift and Find the Saturday

Pick the shift in **Shift:**. Set the **From:** and **To:** range so the target Saturday appears in the grid.

On that Saturday's row, **Shift default (h)** reads 0, because the weekend is off. That zero is exactly what your override replaces.

## Step 3: Type the Approved Hours

Type the approved hours in **Capacity (h)**. Remember the rule: this is the total for the day across every instance, used verbatim.

| Situation | Enter |
|---|---|
| One machine, 8-hour Saturday | `8` |
| Two machines, both 8 hours | `16` |
| Two machines, one staffed for 8 hours | `8` |
| One machine, half day | `4` |

The change saves as you type. The **Overridden** column ticks.

## Step 4: Add a Reason

Enter a **Reason** so the plan explains itself: `Overtime approved, rush order`, `Saturday catch-up, week of the 12th`. Six weeks from now, a Saturday of production with no reason attached is a mystery. With one, it is a record.

## Step 5: Repeat for Each Work Center

Capacity belongs to a work center and shift pairing, not to the plant. If four work centers are working Saturday, that is four overrides. Move to each one's **📊** dialog and repeat. Use the same reason text so the audit reads cleanly.

## Step 6: Close and Rerun

Click **Close**, then run **Drive Schedule**.

## What Changes When You Save

| Surface | Effect |
|---|---|
| The override | Saves instantly, Overridden column ticks |
| Resource Calendar | Shows the Saturday hours on refresh |
| Existing schedules | **Unchanged** until Drive Schedule runs |
| Next scheduling run | Places work on the overridden Saturday, ahead of the shift formula |
| Every other week | Untouched. Only this Saturday gained hours |

## How to Check It Worked

1. Confirm the **Overridden** tick on each Saturday row, across every work center you meant to authorize.
2. Confirm you entered the day total, not per-machine hours, on each.
3. Open the Resource Calendar and read the Saturday capacity. It should match your numbers, and the following Saturdays should still read zero.
4. Run **Drive Schedule**. The rush job's finish date should pull ahead, and Saturday bars should appear only on the work centers you overrode.

## Step 7: Let It Expire

The override applies to one date. Next Saturday is untouched automatically, so there is nothing to undo for the following week. If plans change and the overtime is cancelled, reopen the dialog and click **Reset** on the row to return that Saturday to zero.

## Common Mistakes

**Editing the shift instead.** Ticking Saturday on the shift turns on every Saturday of the year, for every work center on that shift. This is the exact mistake that sits in our [shift and calendar mistakes](/blog/shift-and-calendar-mistakes-edgebic) list under "expecting weekend hours to produce weekend work" and its inverse.

**Per-machine hours on a multi-instance work center.** The override is the day total. Two machines at 8 hours is 16, not 8.

**Overriding the wrong shift.** If the work center runs days and nights, the Saturday hours land on whichever shift you selected. Pick the one the crew is actually working.

**Skipping the rerun.** The Saturday hours become available capacity instantly on the dashboards, but no job moves onto them until Drive Schedule runs.
