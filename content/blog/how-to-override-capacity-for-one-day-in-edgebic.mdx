**To override capacity for one day in EDGEBIC, open the per-day capacity dialog on the work center, select the shift, find the date, and type the new hours in the Capacity column.** The override saves as you type, takes priority over the shift formula, and applies only to that one date and shift. Reset the row to undo it.

This is the tool for one-off realities the calendar should not absorb: approved Saturday overtime, a half-day preventive maintenance window, a temporarily de-rated machine. It carries a reason and an audit flag, which is exactly why it beats editing a shift for anything temporary. For the full capacity chain it sits at the top of, read [how EDGEBIC calculates work center capacity](/blog/how-edgebic-calculates-work-center-capacity) from EDGEBIC by User Solutions.

## Before You Start

- **You know the date and the shift**, and the exact hours you want that day to have.
- **You know the work center's instance count.** The override is the day total, not per machine.
- **You are handling a temporary exception**, not a permanent pattern change. Permanent changes belong in the shift or the calendar.
- **You can rerun the schedule.** The override is honored at the next run, not retroactively.

## Step 1: Open the Per-Day Capacity Dialog

Open the **Workcenter** tab. On the work center's row, click **📊** (tooltip **Per-day capacity**). The **Per-Day Capacity** dialog opens.

Alternate route: in **Schedule → Resource Calendar**, double-click a date cell on the Total Capacity row for the work center.

## Step 2: Select the Shift

Pick the shift in **Shift:**. Overrides are shift-specific. If a work center runs both a day and a night shift and you want to change the total hours for the day, you override each shift separately. Get this wrong and half the day's capacity behaves as you expect while the other half follows the formula.

## Step 3: Set the Date Range

Adjust **From:** and **To:** so the target date appears in the grid. The ↺ button resets the range to today minus seven through today plus seven.

## Step 4: Read the Day, Then Type the New Value

The grid gives you, per day:

| Column | Editable | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| **Day** | No | The calendar date |
| **Shift default (h)** | No | What the formula (shift hours × instances × utilization) would give |
| **Capacity (h)** | **Yes** | The effective hours for that day. Typing here creates the override, used as-is |
| **Loaded (h)** | No | Hours already committed by the current schedule |
| **Overridden** | No | Ticks when the day carries an override |
| **Reason** | Yes | Audit note stored with the override |

Compare **Shift default (h)** with what you want. Then type the new figure in **Capacity (h)**. You can also drag the top edge of the day's bar in the chart, but typing is more precise.

The change saves as you edit. The **Overridden** column ticks and the summary card's **Overridden days** count rises.

## Step 5: Get the Number Right

**Capacity (h)** is the total for the whole day across every instance. The scheduler uses it verbatim and does not multiply by the instance count.

| You want | Instances | Enter |
|---|---|---|
| 4 extra hours on a single machine | 1 | `4` |
| Each of two machines to work 8 hours | 2 | `16` |
| Each of three machines to work 4 hours | 3 | `12` |

## Step 6: Add a Reason and Close

Type a **Reason**: `Overtime approved, rush order`, `Half-day PM`, `De-rated, trainee operator`. The footer reminds you the overrides are honored by the scheduler and persist for audit. Click **Close**, then run **Drive Schedule**.

## What Changes When You Save

| Surface | Effect |
|---|---|
| The override | Saves instantly as you type |
| Dialog chart and summary card | Update immediately |
| Resource Calendar | Shows the override on refresh |
| Existing schedules | **Unchanged** until Drive Schedule runs |
| Next scheduling run | Uses the override first, ahead of any date-range override and ahead of the shift formula |

## How to Check It Worked

1. Confirm the **Overridden** tick on the target row and the reason you entered.
2. If the work center runs more than one shift, change **Shift:** and confirm the override is where it needs to be on each.
3. Open the Resource Calendar and read the work center's total capacity on that date. It should match the number you typed, not the formula default.
4. Run **Drive Schedule**. For added hours (overtime), the affected job should finish sooner; for reduced hours, downstream steps should push out by the difference.

## Step 7: Undo It

When the exception is over, reopen the dialog and click **Reset** on the row ("Remove the override and revert to the shift default"). The day returns to the formula at the next run. Overrides persist until you do this, so a forgotten override quietly distorts the plan for as long as it sits there.

## Common Mistakes

**Per-machine hours instead of the day total.** The headline error. Three instances at 4 hours each is 12, not 4, and the resulting plan is wrong by exactly the instance count.

**Overriding one shift and expecting the whole day.** A two-shift work center needs the override on both shifts to change its total daily capacity. This is a recurring entry in our [shift and calendar mistakes](/blog/shift-and-calendar-mistakes-edgebic) list.

**Using an override for a permanent change.** If a machine now always works fewer hours, change its shift or calendar. Overriding the same day every week is a maintenance burden and hides the real pattern.

**Never resetting.** An override left in place after the reason has passed is invisible in normal use and shows up only as a plan that will not balance.
