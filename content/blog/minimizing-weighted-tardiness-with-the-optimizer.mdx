**Weighted tardiness is the optimizer's core delivery measure: the total lateness across all jobs, with each job's lateness scaled by its priority so a high-priority late job counts more than a low-priority one.** For a single job it is the hours past its due date, floored at zero, times a weight from its priority. Summed across every job, it captures in one number how many jobs are late, how badly, and how much each matters. That is why most goals rank it first: driving it toward zero is the most direct way to protect delivery promises. The optimizer will accept a slower or busier plan to shrink it.

## The calculation, step by step

Weighted tardiness is built from two pieces per job, then summed.

First, tardiness. For a job, take the hours it finishes past its due date. If it finishes early or on time, its tardiness is zero. There is no credit for finishing early: a job one week ahead scores the same zero as one that lands exactly on its date. This floor keeps the measure focused on jobs at risk.

Second, the weight. Each job's tardiness is scaled by a weight derived from its priority. A top-priority job counts at full weight, a second-priority job at half, a third at a third, and so on, following one over the priority number, with a small floor so no job ever counts as literally nothing. This means a late important job hurts the score far more than a late minor one.

Multiply each job's tardiness by its weight, sum across all jobs, and you have the plan's weighted tardiness. A perfect plan on delivery has zero.

## A worked example

Three jobs on a shared shift, hours including setup, with priorities:

| Job | Finishes | Due | Hours late | Priority | Weight | Weighted lateness |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0121 | Tue 10:00 | Fri | 0 | 1 | 1.0 | 0.0 |
| 0122 | Wed 14:00 | Wed 12:00 | 2 | 2 | 0.5 | 1.0 |
| 0123 | Thu 09:00 | Wed 16:00 | 8 | 3 | 0.33 | 2.6 |

The plan's weighted tardiness is 3.6 hours. Notice how the weighting shapes the picture: job 0123 is eight raw hours late but its low priority pulls its contribution down to 2.6, while job 0122's two raw hours at higher priority still register a full hour. If you could rescue only one job, the measure points you at the trade with the biggest score impact, guided by your priorities rather than raw hours alone.

## Why it leads most goals

The default On-time first goal ranks weighted tardiness first, ahead of late job count, stability, and makespan. The reason is that weighted tardiness is the most complete single view of delivery health. Late job count tells you how many jobs miss, but not by how much or how important they are. Raw total tardiness tells you the hours, but treats a minor job's slip the same as a key customer's. Weighted tardiness folds all three concerns into one number, so ranking it first is the most direct way to protect the dates that matter.

Because it ranks first, the optimizer will accept costs on the lower measures to reduce it. A plan that finishes the plant a little later but gets one more important job in on time wins, because the makespan increase sits below weighted tardiness in the ranking and never enters the decision once the top measure separates the plans. This is the goal doing exactly what a delivery-driven planner would do by hand, only faster and across more orderings than a person could test. ## Set priorities honestly, then trust the measure

The whole value of weighting depends on your priorities being real. The measure only knows what you tell it. If every job is priority 1, weighting collapses and weighted tardiness behaves like raw total tardiness, treating all lateness equally. If you set priorities to reflect genuine customer and contract importance, the optimizer will protect the right jobs first, letting minor jobs absorb small slips to keep major jobs on time. The discipline of honest priorities is what turns the measure from a formula into a tool that ranks plans the way your business actually weighs them. ## When to rank something else first

Weighted tardiness leads by default, but it is a trade-off like every measure. When the week's priority is different, another goal fits better: [Fastest overall finish](/blog/the-minmakespan-optimizer-preset-explained) ranks makespan first to clear a backlog, [Least setup time](/blog/the-minsetup-optimizer-preset-explained) ranks setup first in a changeover-heavy shop, and [Fewest changes](/blog/the-moststable-optimizer-preset-explained) ranks stability first on a committed mid-week floor.
