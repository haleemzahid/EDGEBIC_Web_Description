**Holiday scope is the level a non-working day applies to, plant-wide, one shift, or one work center, so the scheduler removes capacity from exactly the right resources and no others.** Choosing the correct scope is what keeps a closure from either idling the whole shop by accident or ignoring downtime that is really there.

## How it works

A non-working day can be declared at different levels, and the level is its scope. A plant holiday is company-wide and removes capacity from all work centers. A shift holiday removes capacity from a specific shift. A work-center holiday removes capacity from a single work center only.

The scheduler reads each holiday at its scope and zeroes the matching capacity for that day. So a plant holiday empties the calendar everywhere, a shift holiday empties just that shift's hours while other shifts run, and a work-center holiday empties just that machine while the rest of the shop continues.

The reason scope exists is precision. Real downtime is rarely uniform: a site closes for Christmas, but a single shift might take an eve off, and a single machine might go down for calibration while everything else runs. One flat holiday type could not express those differences, so the scope tells the engine exactly which resources to remove and leaves the rest untouched.

## A concrete example

Think of a school calendar. A national holiday closes the whole school (plant scope). A single class canceled for a teacher's training day affects only that class (shift scope). A gym closed for floor refinishing affects only that room while every other class meets (work-center scope). Same idea of a non-working day, three different reaches.

Take a week where the plant closes Friday for a long weekend, the night shift is off Wednesday for an event, and Furnace 2 is down Tuesday for calibration. You model three holidays at three scopes: a plant holiday Friday that idles everything, a shift holiday Wednesday that zeroes only the night shift, and a work-center holiday Tuesday that zeroes only Furnace 2. When the engine schedules, Friday is closed shop-wide, Wednesday still runs the day shift, and Tuesday runs every machine except the furnace.

## How EDGEBIC uses it

In EDGEBIC, holidays are declared at plant, shift, or work-center scope, and the scheduler removes capacity accordingly when it builds the plan. Plant holidays cover site-wide closures in one entry, shift holidays handle a single shift being off, and work-center holidays confine downtime to one machine.

Matching scope to reality keeps the plan honest in both directions. Too broad a scope over-blocks, idling resources that are actually available; too narrow a scope leaves phantom capacity the shop cannot really use. Because the engine respects each scope precisely, a reschedule flows work around exactly the resources that are down, and steps needing an unavailable machine slide past its holiday while everything else holds.
