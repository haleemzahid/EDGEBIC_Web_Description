**To change a job quantity after it is scheduled in EDGEBIC, edit the order, save it, then run the scheduler again for that job.** The saved edit changes order master data on its own. The bars on the plan keep showing the old quantity's hours until you re-plan, and that is deliberate: EDGEBIC by User Solutions never lets a data edit silently rewrite a plan the shop floor is already working to.

Quantity is one of the five fields that genuinely require a re-run. For the run itself, see [how to run the scheduler in EDGEBIC](/blog/how-to-run-the-scheduler-in-edgebic). The rest of the library lives at the [EDGEBIC how-to hub](/blog/edgebic-how-to-hub).

## Before You Start

- The job exists on the Drive Schedule grid and already carries a **Scheduled** badge.
- You know whether the job has actuals logged. Check the **Actuals Logged?** column: a **Y** changes what the re-run will and will not touch.
- Work center calendars and capacity are current, because the re-run plans against them as they are right now.

## The Steps

1. **Find the job** on the **Drive Schedule** tab.
2. **Click the edit button** in the row's **Actions** column. The **Edit Manufacturing Order** dialog opens with the same fields as order entry, pre-filled.
3. **Change the Quantity field.** It must stay greater than 0 and no more than 1,000,000.
4. **Click Save Order.** The dialog closes and the grid row updates immediately.
5. **Decide your scope.** Tick only the rows you intend to re-plan. Unticked jobs keep their existing schedules and their reserved capacity.
6. **Run the scheduler.** Press the schedule button and read the **Confirm Scheduling** dialog, which reports how many jobs are new and how many existing jobs will be rescheduled. For one job on its own, the **Job View** tab's **Re-Schedule** button is cleaner.
7. **Confirm** and let the progress overlay finish.

## What Changes When You Save

| Stage | What actually changes |
|---|---|
| Save the edit | The order's quantity. Nothing else. No capacity is claimed or released. |
| Before the re-run | Every bar, every date, and every hours figure still reflects the old quantity. |
| After the re-run | The job's operations are rebuilt with run hours computed from the new quantity, and capacity is re-claimed on every work center the job touches. |
| Recorded work | Completed and in-progress operations are preserved exactly. Only remaining steps are re-planned. |
| Other jobs | Jobs you did not tick keep their slots. New work is planned around them. |

The cost roll-up follows the quantity too, so the estimated hours and cost figures in the order's Cost Analysis panel reflect the new number after the save.

## How to Check It Worked

Open the **Job View** tab and select the job. The summary panel's hours header shows **Total Hours**, **Actual Hours**, **Remaining** and **% Complete** for the whole job. Total Hours should have moved in proportion to the quantity change on the run-time portion of the work. Then read the per-operation grid: **Sched Hours** on each work center step is where the new quantity actually lands.

Back on Drive Schedule, check **Item Start**, **Job End** and **Days Late**. A quantity increase that pushes **Job End** past the **Due Date** turns **Days Late** red before the date passes, which is the warning working as intended.

## Common Mistakes

**Editing the quantity and walking away.** The plan does not react to master data edits. Until you re-run, the Gantt, the dashboards and anything you print for the floor all describe the old quantity. Make the change, then run.

**Re-running the whole plant to fix one job.** Ticking every row moves bars that did not need to move, and shop floors distrust plans that churn. Tick the one job, or use the single-job **Re-Schedule** button on **Job View**. [How to schedule a single job](/blog/how-to-schedule-a-single-job-in-edgebic) covers the narrow path.

**Assuming a bigger quantity only stretches that one job.** It does not. The extra hours are claimed from the same finite work center capacity everything else competes for, so the jobs queued behind it on that machine move too. Check the work center's load after the run, not just the job you edited.

**Cutting a quantity on a part-built job and expecting the hours to tidy themselves.** Recorded work is never moved or deleted by a reschedule. If operators have already logged more hours than the reduced plan calls for, the variance stays visible in the roll-up. That is the honest answer, and it is usually the one you want.
