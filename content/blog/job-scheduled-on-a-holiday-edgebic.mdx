**When a job lands on a holiday, the scheduling engine almost never chose that day: in nearly every real case, the holiday was entered after the schedule was generated, leaving a booking that was legal when it was made stranded on a day that is now closed.** The engine checks every holiday layer at scheduling time, so the diagnosis is really about which calendar record changed and when, and the fix is nearly always a single reschedule.

[EDGEBIC by User Solutions](/edgebic) models plant closures in four distinct layers, and its built-in anomaly report checks bookings against every layer after each run. Knowing the layers turns "why is there work on Labor Day?" from a mystery into a two-minute check. This post is part of the [EDGEBIC troubleshooting guide](/blog/edgebic-troubleshooting-guide).

## The Four Calendar Layers

A booking can conflict with four different kinds of closed time, and each has its own record and its own typical failure story:

| Layer | Scope | Typical use | Typical failure story |
|---|---|---|---|
| Plant holiday | Whole facility, whole day | Statutory holidays, plant shutdown | Holiday entered after the annual schedule was built |
| Work center holiday | One machine or cell | Planned maintenance day | Maintenance date agreed verbally, entered late |
| Shift holiday | One shift, a date range | Day crew off, night crew working | Range entered for the wrong shift |
| Downtime event | One-off or recurring window | Weekly PM window, a known repair | Recorded against the wrong work center |

The distinction matters because a plant holiday silently also blocks every work center, while a work center holiday blocks exactly one. If you only ever check the plant calendar, machine-level and shift-level conflicts stay invisible until the floor hits them.

## Cause 1: The Holiday Was Added After Scheduling Ran

This is the cause in the overwhelming majority of cases. The engine's holiday check runs when the schedule is generated: closed days contribute zero capacity, and the allocator flows work around them. A booking sitting on a holiday therefore means the sequence went the wrong way round: schedule first, holiday second.

**How to confirm:** compare the holiday record's creation date against the last scheduling run. EDGEBIC's anomaly report lists every booking on a plant holiday, work center holiday, shift holiday, or downtime window, with the holiday's name and the affected job and date, so you do not have to hunt.

**The fix:** re-run scheduling. Remaining work re-plans around the now-closed day. Completed work stays put, as it should: if people genuinely worked that day before it was declared closed, that history is preserved.

**Prevention:** make "calendar first, then schedule" a standing rule. Load next year's holidays before the annual planning pass, and treat any mid-year closure as a two-step action: enter it, then reschedule.

## Cause 2: The Holiday Exists but on the Wrong Layer

The plant is open, one machine is down for maintenance, and someone entered a plant holiday instead of a work center holiday, or the reverse. Now either the whole plant lost a day it should have worked, or one machine kept taking bookings it cannot honor.

**How to confirm:** open the calendar records for the date and check the scope of each entry. A shift holiday needs the correct shift attached; a work center holiday needs the correct machine.

**The fix:** move the record to the right layer, then reschedule. Capacity comes back where it should exist and disappears where it should not.

## Cause 3: A Downtime Window Pointing at the Wrong Target

Recurring downtime (a Friday PM window, for example) is matched by work center and, where relevant, by shift. If the record points at a different machine than the one physically down, the engine happily books the machine you meant to protect.

**How to confirm:** the anomaly report's downtime findings name both the event and the booked machine. If the names disagree with your intent, the record is misaimed.

**The fix:** correct the downtime record's work center or shift, then reschedule the affected jobs.

## Cause 4: It Only Looks Like a Holiday Booking

Two look-alikes are worth ruling out before touching anything:

- **Overnight shifts.** A shift running 22:00 to 06:00 legitimately produces bookings whose clock time crosses into the next calendar day. Work starting the evening before a holiday can display against the holiday's date without violating it.
- **Completed work.** Actual dates never move. If a completed operation shows a holiday date, that is a record of what really happened, not a scheduling decision to fix.

If neither applies and the bookings are on genuinely closed days, you are back to causes 1 through 3.

## Why This Matters More Than It Looks

A single stranded booking is a nuisance. A calendar that drifts from reality is a systemic problem: every promise date computed against phantom capacity is optimistic by exactly the closed time the system does not know about. Shops that keep calendars honest get the compounding benefit that [finite capacity scheduling](/blog/finite-vs-infinite-capacity-scheduling) exists to deliver: dates you can commit to. The discipline is identical to keeping actuals fresh before a reschedule; both are cases of telling the system the truth before asking it for a plan. If a calendar change also made a job's dates move unexpectedly, see [why a job jumps after a reschedule](/blog/why-did-my-job-jump-after-a-reschedule-edgebic), and for the full recovery drill after an unplanned outage, the [machine breakdown reschedule walkthrough](/blog/machine-breakdown-reschedule-walkthrough-edgebic) shows every step with real numbers.
