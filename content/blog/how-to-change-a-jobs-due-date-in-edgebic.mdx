**To change a job's due date in EDGEBIC, type the new date into the Due Date cell on the Drive Schedule grid, click Save Changes, then re-run the scheduler for that job.** Due Date is one of three fields you can edit inline without opening a dialog, alongside **Priority** and **Direction**. The edit changes the promise. It does not move a single bar until you plan again, which is how EDGEBIC by User Solutions keeps a live plan from rewriting itself under the floor's feet.

This post covers the one task. For the run that follows it, read [how to run the scheduler in EDGEBIC](/blog/how-to-run-the-scheduler-in-edgebic); the full task library is at the [EDGEBIC how-to hub](/blog/edgebic-how-to-hub).

## Before You Start

- The job exists on the **Drive Schedule** grid.
- You know the job's scheduling direction. Backward jobs require a due date and cannot be saved without one.
- You know whether the job is closed. The **Days Late** column always reads 0 for completed jobs, so a due date change on a closed job has no visible effect there.

## The Steps

1. **Open the Schedule area** and select the **Drive Schedule** tab.
2. **Click into the Due Date cell** on the job's row and type or pick the new date. The **Save Changes** button appears on the filter bar as soon as there is an unsaved edit.
3. **Repeat for any other rows** whose promises are moving. Inline edits stage together.
4. **Click Save Changes.** The dates are written to the orders.
5. **Tick the affected rows** and run the scheduler. Read the **Confirm Scheduling** dialog: it reports how many jobs are new and how many existing jobs will be rescheduled.
6. **For a single job**, use the **Re-Schedule** button on the **Job View** tab instead. It re-plans that job alone and leaves every other job's reservations alone.

If you prefer a dialog, click the edit button in the row's **Actions** column and change **Due Date** in the **Edit Manufacturing Order** window, then click **Save Order**.

## What Changes When You Save

| Surface | Effect |
|---|---|
| The order | The due date is written. Nothing else moves. |
| The plan | Unchanged until the next scheduling run for that job. |
| **Days Late** | Recomputes as soon as the job has both a due date and a planned **Job End**. |
| Backward jobs | The due date is the anchor the whole plan works back from, so a change here changes where every operation lands on the next run. |
| Audit | The change is recorded in the job's history, readable from the row's audit trail button. |

The direction of the job decides how much the new date actually matters to the engine. A forward job starts as early as it can and compares its end to the promise. A backward job works back from the promise itself, so moving the date moves the whole plan. That difference is covered in [how to schedule a job backward from its due date](/blog/how-to-schedule-a-job-backward-from-its-due-date-in-edgebic).

## How to Check It Worked

Read three columns on the row after the run: **Due Date** shows the new promise, **Job End** shows the delivery-ready date the plan produces, and **Days Late** shows the gap. Days Late of 0 means the plan makes the date. A positive number, shown before the date has passed, means it does not.

If **Days Late** did not move at all, check that the job carries a **Scheduled** badge. An unscheduled job has no **Job End** to compare against.

## Common Mistakes

**Filling in a due date you have not actually promised.** A blank due date on a forward job is not a gap in the data. The scheduler fills it once from the job's own first planned end, which gives you a baseline that reflects reality. Type in a hopeful date instead and **Days Late** starts warning about a commitment nobody made.

**Expecting the scheduler to move the date back for you.** It fills a blank field once and never touches a populated one again. If the plan cannot make the date, EDGEBIC tells you with Days Late rather than quietly relaxing the promise. That is the behavior you want when a customer asks whether the date is still good.

**Changing the date and not re-running.** The plan does not react to master data edits by itself. Until you run, the bars, the dashboards and anything you printed still describe the old world.

**Reading Days Late against Item Start.** It compares against **Job End**, which is **Item Start** plus the product's lead time in calendar days. A job whose last operation finishes on the due date but whose product carries a two day lead time reads as two days late, correctly. The distinction is worth understanding before you argue with the number.
