**Pharmaceutical production scheduling breaks when cleaning time is treated as a single fixed number, because the real cost of a changeover depends entirely on what the line ran last and what it runs next.** EDGEBIC by User Solutions prices every product-to-product transition from a per-line lookup, gates each operation behind the certifications the work legally requires, and keeps a per-job record of the routing that produced each batch. For a plant losing validated line time to a changeover order nobody planned, those three behaviors are the difference between a plan the floor trusts and a plan the floor ignores.

## Why a Flat Cleaning Time Lies

A tablet, capsule, or liquids line does not clean the same way for every changeover. Going from a standard product to another standard product may need a minor clean of thirty minutes. Going from an allergen or high-potency product to a sensitive one may require a full validated clean of two to four hours, and the reverse direction may be cheaper or free. A single cleaning value stored against the routing step cannot tell those cases apart.

Consider one coating line running three batches today, sequenced by due date, each carrying a flat 30-minute changeover in the routing.

| Batch | Product class | Run hours | Cleaning claimed |
|---|---|---|---|
| First | Standard | 3.0 h | 0.5 h |
| Second | Allergen | 4.0 h | 0.5 h |
| Third | Standard | 2.5 h | 0.5 h |

The plan reads eleven hours across the batches and looks comfortable inside two shifts. On the floor the standard-to-allergen transition needs a two-hour clean and the allergen-to-standard return needs a four-hour validated clean, so the day actually consumes closer to nineteen hours. The plan did not lie on purpose. It used the only number it had, and every promise built on it was wrong by most of a shift.

## The Cleaning Changeover Matrix

Configure the same line with three cleaning classes and a class-to-class matrix. This is the [sequence-dependent setup](/blog/edgebic-setup-matrix-explained) mechanism applied to cleaning validation, and the classes are ordinary [setup families](/blog/what-is-a-setup-family-sequence-dependent-changeover) named for how the line actually cleans.

| From class | To class | Clean minutes |
|---|---|---|
| Standard | Standard | 30 |
| Standard | Allergen | 120 |
| Allergen | Standard | **240** |
| Allergen | Allergen | 30 |

Run the identical due-date sequence and the plan now shows the standard-to-allergen clean at 120 minutes and the allergen-to-standard clean at 240. The plan overflows two shifts, and it is the first honest plan you have seen for that line. The planner can now decide before the fact: overtime, a third shift, or a different order.

Keep everything the same and group the two standard batches first, then the allergen batch. The second standard batch is a same-class run at 30 minutes, and only one class transition remains. Total cleaning falls from 390 minutes to roughly 180, and the day fits. Nothing about the equipment changed. Only the order did, and the order became a decision the moment the cost of it was visible.

## Certification Gates, Not Just Capacity

A validated line is not schedulable just because a machine is free. It is schedulable when a qualified operator is present. In EDGEBIC, operators carry skills and a routing step can require a certification, so the scheduler places aseptic, weigh-and-dispense, or line-clearance work only on shifts where a rostered, qualified operator is available. If the one certified operator is on leave, the step slides to a shift where the certification exists rather than printing a plan the floor cannot legally execute.

Certifications can carry expiry dates. A qualification that lapses drops the operator from eligibility on its own, so a training gap never becomes a schedule that silently assumes a certification nobody currently holds. This is the same operator and skill model documented for [regulated device work](/blog/edgebic-for-medical-devices-operator-certifications), and it works identically for GMP pharmaceutical lines. The mechanics of how a skill constrains placement are covered in [how operator skills gate work center assignment](/blog/how-operator-skills-gate-work-center-assignment).

## A Per-Batch Record That Survives Rescheduling

Regulated manufacturing needs to know not just what happened but what was planned, and to reconcile the two. Each job in EDGEBIC keeps a snapshot of the routing that scheduled it, held separately from the master routing that engineering may revise next month. When you reschedule around a delay, the engine reads that snapshot, so the batch is not quietly re-planned against a routing it never used.

Completed operations that carry recorded actual dates are never moved by a reschedule. The finished steps stay exactly as they ran, and only the remaining work is re-timed from where the batch actually stands. That gives you a per-job trail of planned versus actual that a batch record can reconcile against, described further in [the medical device audit-trail application](/blog/edgebic-for-medical-devices-audit-trail).

## Where the Numbers Come From

Honest cleaning times come from the floor, not from a guess. Operators log actual start and end on a shop-floor kiosk, and a changeover captured as a real teardown, clean, and line-clearance sequence gives you a measured duration to load back into the class-to-class grid rather than a standard nobody has checked in years. The kiosk workflow is covered in [shop-floor actuals tracking](/blog/edgebic-for-machine-shops-actuals-tracking), and the resulting actual times feed the same schedule that priced them.

The matrix imports and exports as a spreadsheet, so a quality-owned cleaning-validation table and the scheduling grid stay a single workflow instead of two copies drifting apart. You can also test a proposed sequence without committing anything by running a [quote simulation](/blog/edgebic-quote-simulation-explained) against a hypothetical batch order, because the simulation uses the same cleaning lookup and the same certification gates as a live schedule.

## Fitting It to Your Plant

Start on the one line where changeover pain is loudest. Define three or four cleaning classes that describe how that line actually cleans between products, assign the products that run there, and fill the class grid from your validated clean times. Reschedule, and read the changeover column: you will usually find one transition costs far more than the flat number claimed, and one pair you assumed was expensive is nearly free. Both change how you sequence, and neither was visible before.
