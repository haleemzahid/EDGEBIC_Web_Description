**Adding a product to the MPS grid in EDGEBIC** is really about making it a make-to-stock product. In EDGEBIC by User Solutions the master production schedule grid plans items that are built to stock, so a product joins the grid once its build method is make-to-stock and it is flagged as stocked. After that, opening the MPS tab and selecting the product loads its time-phased build plan. This guide covers the product configuration that makes a product eligible and how to confirm it appears.

For working the grid once the product is on it, read [how to work the MPS grid](/blog/how-to-work-the-mps-grid-in-edgebic). For what the grid is and why it exists, see [the master production schedule explained](/blog/edgebic-master-production-schedule-explained). This is documented behavior of [EDGEBIC](/edgebic); the full task set is in the [how-to hub](/blog/edgebic-how-to-hub).

## Before You Start

- You need the product to exist.
- You need the master production schedule tab to be available in the inventory area of your installation.
- Decide the product is genuinely planned to stock, not built only against customer orders.

## Step 1: Open the Product's Planning Attributes

Open the product and go to its inventory planning section. This is where the fields that govern stock planning live.

## Step 2: Set Build Method to Make-to-Stock

Set **Build Method** to **make-to-stock**. This activates the make-to-stock path in the projection, which is what lets the product receive replenishment suggestions and carry master schedule entries. A make-to-order product, by contrast, gets no automatic suggestions and will not plan a build to stock.

## Step 3: Flag the Product as Stocked

Tick **Is Stocked**. Both this flag and the make-to-stock build method are needed for full stock behavior. The projection's make-to-stock path keys on the build method, and a separate data check flags any make-to-stock product left unstocked, because that pairing almost always means somebody set one field and forgot the other. Setting both avoids that anomaly.

## Step 4: Save the Product

Save. The product is now a make-to-stock, stocked item and is eligible for the grid.

## Make-to-Stock Versus Make-to-Order

The build method is the fork that decides whether the grid plans for a product at all.

| Build method | Automatic suggestions | Carries MPS entries | Firm demand from orders |
|---|---|---|---|
| Make-to-stock | Yes, when triggered | Yes | Yes |
| Make-to-order | No, ever | No stock plan | Yes, from confirmed lines |

A make-to-order product is only ever built against a specific customer order, so its suggested build quantity is always zero and stock netting does not apply. It can still show firm demand from a confirmed sales order line, but the grid will never propose a build to stock for it. If you want the grid to plan replenishment, the product has to be make-to-stock. If an item genuinely needs both a stock buffer and customer-order builds, treat it as make-to-stock for planning and let confirmed order lines add firm demand on top.

## Step 5: Open the MPS Tab and Select the Product

Go to the inventory area and select the **MPS** tab. Choose the product from the product dropdown. The grid loads automatically, showing the time-phased build plan: buckets down the side, and columns for forecast, firm, demand, projected on hand, available-to-promise, suggested, MPS build, status, and the frozen flag.

## What Changes When You Save

The product now participates in stock planning. It receives replenishment suggestions when its projected balance dips below its trigger, it can carry committed build quantities on the grid, and consume-from-stock netting applies to it. Nothing is scheduled yet: eligibility is not a build. The grid gives you the picture and the controls; committing and firming are separate steps covered in [how to firm an MPS quantity into a job](/blog/how-to-firm-an-mps-quantity-into-a-job-in-edgebic).

## Getting Suggestions to Appear

Eligibility puts a product on the grid; it does not guarantee the suggested column shows anything. For a suggestion to fire, three more settings have to be right on top of make-to-stock and stocked:

1. **A reorder method other than none.** With no method, the grid has no rule to trigger on. Set reorder point or min-max.
2. **A non-zero trigger level.** The reorder level (reorder-point mode) or minimum level (min-max mode), or the safety stock, must be above zero, or the projected balance never crosses a threshold.
3. **A horizon that reaches the shortfall.** If every visible bucket stays above the trigger, no suggestion appears; extend the horizon until it reaches a projected dip.

Miss any one and the suggested column reads zero even though the product is correctly on the grid. Work through them in order when a suggestion you expect is missing.

## How to Check It Worked

Open the MPS tab, select the product, and confirm the grid loads with real buckets rather than staying empty. If the product has demand (forecast, confirmed sales order lines, or both), the demand and firm columns should show it. If the projected balance dips and the policy settings above are in place, the suggested column should propose a quantity. An empty grid usually means the product has no demand yet, not that the configuration failed.

## Common Mistakes

- **Setting build method but not the stocked flag.** Both are required for full make-to-stock behavior. A make-to-stock product left unstocked trips a data check and may not net from stock correctly.
- **Expecting a make-to-order product to plan replenishment.** It never will. Its suggested quantity stays zero by design. Make it make-to-stock if you want the grid to plan it.
- **Confusing the module with the product.** The MPS tab being present is an installation matter; the product being make-to-stock is a product matter. Both have to be true.
- **Reading an empty grid as a failure.** No demand means an empty grid. Add a forecast or confirm a sales order line and the grid fills.

## Where This Fits in Onboarding a Product

Making a product eligible is the second step in bringing a new item into planning, not the whole job. The usual sequence is: create the product, set it make-to-stock and stocked (this task), then set the planning attributes that govern how it replenishes and how far ahead it plans (safety stock, reorder method and levels, lead time, and the time fences). Only then do you enter the first forecasts and confirm a sales order line or two so the grid has demand to react to. Eligibility without those follow-on settings gives you a grid that loads but never suggests anything, which is why the empty-grid outcome is so common right after this step. Treat it as the start of a checklist, not the end.
