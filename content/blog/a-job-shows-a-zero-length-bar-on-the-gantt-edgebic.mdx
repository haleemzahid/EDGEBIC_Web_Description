**A job that shows a zero-length or backwards bar on the Gantt has an operation with no duration: its scheduled end equals or precedes its start.** The overwhelming cause is a routing step whose per-unit hours and setup time are both zero, which produces a zero-duration operation. If the step is genuinely meant to be instant, it belongs as a material step rather than a timed work-center step. The fix is quick once you see which case you have.

[EDGEBIC by User Solutions](/edgebic) flags a schedule row whose end date is at or before its start date as a critical plan inversion, because such a row cannot draw a real bar and breaks the date math for every step that depends on it. This post, part of the [EDGEBIC troubleshooting guide](/blog/edgebic-troubleshooting-guide), walks the causes and the correct model for instant steps.

## What a Zero-Length Bar Means

A bar's width is the elapsed time from an operation's start to its end. When the end equals the start, there is nothing to draw, so the bar collapses to a sliver or vanishes. When the end precedes the start, the bar would run backwards. Either way the schedule row is invalid, and the plan-inversion checks flag it:

- **Schedule end at or before start** flags an operation whose window has zero or negative length.
- **Resource end at or before start** flags the underlying allocation with the same problem.

This is a single-operation problem, distinct from [the whole Gantt showing nothing after a run](/blog/the-gantt-shows-nothing-after-a-run-edgebic), where no bars appear at all. Here the job is scheduled; one of its operations just has no length.

## Cause 1: A Routing Step With No Hours and No Setup

The most common cause is a work-center step whose per-unit hours and setup time are both zero. The engine multiplies per-unit hours by quantity and adds setup to get the operation's duration; if both are zero, the duration is zero, and the operation ends the instant it starts.

**How to tell:** open the routing and read the flagged step. Per-unit hours zero and setup zero together produce this exactly.

**Fix:** decide what the step really is. If it takes real machine time, even a few minutes, give it a non-zero per-unit hours value so it has a duration to schedule. If it takes no time at all, it should not be a work-center step; convert it to a material step (next section).

## Cause 2: An Instant Step Modeled as a Work-Center Step

Some routing events are genuinely instant: a stock issue, a paperwork gate, a logical marker. Modeled as a work-center step with zero hours, they trip the plan-inversion check every time. The correct shape for an instant event is a material step, which has no work center and no hours and is handled as an instant assignment rather than a timed operation.

**How to tell:** the step represents something that consumes no machine capacity and no clock time, yet it is set up as a work-center step.

**Fix:** model it as a material step. See [how to add a material step to a routing](/blog/how-to-add-a-material-step-to-a-routing-in-edgebic). A material step is placed instantly at the right point in the sequence without trying to draw a timed bar, so the plan-inversion check never fires on it.

## Cause 3: An Allocation That Computed Zero Effective Hours

Occasionally a fresh routing produces a zero-length operation after a reschedule even though the step looks fine, because the allocation computed zero or negative effective hours and stamped the end equal to the start. A minimum-allocation threshold that nothing met can leave the operation empty.

**How to tell:** the routing hours look correct, but the flag appears after a reschedule.

**Fix:** re-run scheduling for the job. A clean pass rebuilds the allocation from the routing. If the operation still shows zero length, fall back to cause 1 and confirm the step's hours are genuinely non-zero.

## How to Confirm the Fix

After correcting the step and re-running scheduling, re-open the anomaly report and confirm the plan-inversion chips return zero rows for the job. On the Gantt, the operation should now draw a visible bar with a real width (or, if you converted it to a material step, appear as an instant marker in sequence rather than a timed bar). The end date must sit after the start date on every operation.

## Prevention

- **Never leave a work-center step at zero hours.** A timed step is meant to consume time; give it real per-unit hours or setup, or it will collapse.
- **Use material steps for instant events.** Stock issues, gates, and markers belong as material steps, which are built to be instant and never trip the plan-inversion check.
- **Audit routings after import.** If routings [arrive from your ERP](/edgebic-erp-integration) with blank hour columns, a scheduling pass and an anomaly-report read surface any zero-duration steps before they reach a plan.
- **Treat any plan inversion as critical.** These checks are critical for a reason: a zero or backwards operation corrupts the date chain for everything downstream of it, so clear them before trusting the plan.
