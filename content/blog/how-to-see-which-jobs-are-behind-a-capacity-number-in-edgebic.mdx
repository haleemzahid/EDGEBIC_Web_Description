**Every capacity number in EDGEBIC by User Solutions is clickable: click an assigned hours or backlog cell in the Resource Calendar and a Job Detail dialog lists the exact jobs, shifts, dates, and hours that add up to it.** A red day tells you there is a problem. This tells you whose problem it is, which is the part you can act on.

For the wider capacity picture, see [how to see a work center's load for next month](/blog/how-to-see-a-work-centers-load-for-next-month-in-edgebic), and for the utilization report's ranking of the heaviest jobs in the plant, [how to read the Top Jobs by Load tab](/blog/how-to-read-the-top-jobs-by-load-tab-in-edgebic). Every task in this library is mapped on the [EDGEBIC how-to hub](/blog/edgebic-how-to-hub).

## Before You Start

- Jobs are scheduled. Every figure in the Resource Calendar is derived from the plan, so an unscheduled shop shows empty cells.
- Work centers carry shifts and calendars, because capacity comes from the real shift calendars and any per-day overrides.
- You know which question you are asking. Assigned hours and backlog are different numbers and drill into different job lists.

## Step 1: Open the Resource Calendar

Open the **Schedule** area and select the **Resource Calendar** tab. It is a grid of work centers against time periods, and it is read-only: nothing you click here changes a plan.

## Step 2: Set the Lens

The controls across the top decide what each cell means.

| Control | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Mode** | All, Assigned, Backlog, Total, Available Capacity, or Utilization. In All mode each work center block shows five stacked rows |
| **View** | Day, Week, Month, or Quarter column buckets |
| **Rounding** | Decimal places on the numbers |
| **Shift Filter** | Restricts the figures to one shift |
| **Job** | Restricts assigned and backlog figures to one job |
| **Start Date** and **End Date** | The reporting range |
| **Include Weekends** | Shows weekend columns |
| **Show as Hours** | In Utilization mode, shows hours instead of percentages |

Start with Mode set to **All** and View set to **Week**. That gives you total capacity, hours assigned, available capacity, backlog, and utilization for every station in one screen.

## Step 3: Click the Number

Click a **Hours Assigned** or **Backlog** cell that holds a non-zero value. The **Job Detail** dialog opens, headed with the work center and the period you clicked, and lists one row per allocation:

| Column | What it holds |
|---|---|
| **Job Number** | The job claiming the hours |
| **Product Name** and **Product Number** | What that job builds |
| **Work Center** | The station, repeated per row |
| **Shift** | Which shift the hours land in |
| **Date** | The day inside the period |
| **Instance** | Which machine instance in a multi-machine work center |
| **Transaction Hours** | The hours this row contributes |
| **Transaction Pieces** | The pieces this row contributes |

The transaction hours add up to the cell you clicked, under the same Shift Filter and Job settings that were active on the grid.

## Step 4: Read Assigned Against Backlog

The two cells answer different questions and the difference decides your response.

**Hours Assigned** is what lands in this period. It is the number to click when someone asks what the station is doing on Tuesday.

**Backlog** is how much work is still ahead of the station as of this period. It is a forward sum of remaining allocation hours, so a step with 24 hours spread over three days reads 24, then 16, then 8, then 0 rather than repeating 24 until it is finished. A backlog that grows week over week means work is arriving faster than the station clears it, which is the earliest warning you get.

## Step 5: Turn the List Into a Decision

Read the job list and pick the cheapest fix:

1. **The capacity is wrong.** Maintenance finished early or overtime was agreed. Double-click the **Total Capacity** cell for that day to open the per-day capacity dialog and set the day's hours with a reason. See [how to override capacity for one day](/blog/how-to-override-capacity-for-one-day-in-edgebic).
2. **The load is wrong.** One job on the list has slack. Move it on the Gantt, or let the optimizer propose a better ordering.
3. **Neither.** The station is genuinely short of hours, and that is a capacity conversation rather than a scheduling one.

## What Changes When You Click

Nothing. Opening the calendar, switching modes, and drilling into a cell are all read-only. Only the per-day capacity dialog reached by double-clicking a **Total Capacity** cell writes anything, and even then already-scheduled jobs stay where they are until the next scheduling run re-plans against the new hours.

## How to Check It Worked

After you act and re-run the scheduler, click the same cell again. The Job Detail list should have changed composition: the job you moved is gone from that period, or the hours are spread differently across the instances. If the list is identical, the change you made did not reach the plan, which usually means the scheduler was not re-run.

## Common Mistakes

- **Clicking a zero cell and concluding the drill-down is broken.** There is nothing behind an empty number.
- **Comparing a filtered cell against an unfiltered expectation.** The dialog honors the calendar's Shift Filter and Job filter, so clear them before you audit a total.
- **Reading backlog as a workload for the week.** It is the queue ahead of the station, not the hours planned inside the period.
- **Using the calendar as the authority on utilization percentages.** For the full capacity report with plant-wide ratings and the daily heatmap, use [the work center utilization report](/blog/how-to-run-the-work-center-utilization-report-in-edgebic).
