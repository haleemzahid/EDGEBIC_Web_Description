**To change a step's time in a scenario in EDGEBIC, open the quote's Scenarios view, add a step override, set the Override Type to ModifyTime, and enter a new Setup Time and/or Process Time in hours, then click Run Simulation.** The step uses the new times inside that scenario only. The real routing keeps its original values.

A scenario in EDGEBIC by User Solutions is a named what-if variant of a quote's simulation. A ModifyTime step override is the lever for testing "what if this operation were faster" (or slower): a proposed fixture that cuts setup, a better tool that speeds the run, or an honest allowance for a slower vendor. The change is confined to the scenario, so you can test a claim before spending money on it.

## Before You Start

- **A quote exists and has been simulated once.** Scenarios are children of a quote, and the base simulation is your comparison baseline. See [how to create a what-if scenario](/blog/how-to-create-a-what-if-scenario-in-edgebic).
- **You know the step and the new times.** ModifyTime needs the routing step and the new setup and/or process hours.
- **You know which step is the constraint.** Cutting time on a non-bottleneck step rarely moves the finish date.
- **You keep a baseline scenario.** A no-override Standard scenario is what you compare the change against.

## Step 1: Open the Scenario View

On the **Quote** tab, select the quote and click **Scenarios**. The What-If Scenario Analysis view opens. Select an existing scenario or click **Create New Scenario** and name it after the lever, for example `New mill fixture`.

## Step 2: Add a Step Override

In the **Production Step Overrides** group, click **Add Step Override**. A new override row appears.

## Step 3: Pick the Step and Set ModifyTime

Choose the **Step**, then set **Override Type** to **ModifyTime**. The time fields become relevant.

| Override Type | Effect in this scenario | You also fill in |
|---|---|---|
| **ModifyTime** | The step's times are changed | **Setup Time** and/or **Process Time** (hours) |

## Step 4: Enter the New Times

Type the new **Setup Time** and/or **Process Time**, in hours. Change one or both:

- Lower **Setup Time** to model a quicker changeover or a new fixture.
- Lower **Process Time** to model a faster method or tool.
- Raise either to model an honest allowance, for example a slower outside vendor.

## Step 5: Run the Simulation

Click **Run Simulation**. Read the **Simulation Results**: **Estimated Start Date**, **Estimated End Date**, **Lead Time**, **Total Hours**, and **Estimated Cost**. Each run overwrites this scenario's stored results, and nothing touches the shop plan.

## What Changes When You Save

| Action | What changes | What does NOT change |
|---|---|---|
| **Run Simulation** | The scenario's stored dates, hours, and cost | The production schedule, the quote, the real routing |
| **Apply to Quote** | The parent quote's estimated numbers | The product's real routing times |

## How to Check It Worked

1. Confirm the override row shows **ModifyTime** and the new times you entered.
2. Click **Run Simulation** and note the new **Total Hours** and **Estimated End Date**.
3. Open the **Comparison Analysis** tab, tick this scenario and a Standard baseline, and read the Lead Time and Cost charts. The change should show a different bar.
4. Confirm the real routing is unchanged: open the product's routing in the BOR editor and verify the step still carries its original setup and process times.

## Common Mistakes

**Aiming at the wrong step.** Reducing time on a non-bottleneck step adds slack there but rarely pulls the finish in. Target the constraint, which the Work Center Utilization report identifies.

**Entering minutes instead of hours.** Both fields are in hours. A 30-minute setup is 0.5, not 30.

**Forgetting to re-simulate.** The override does nothing until you click Run Simulation.

**Treating the scenario as a real change.** Applying a winning scenario copies its numbers onto the quote only. Update the real routing in the BOR editor before converting the quote so the schedule can reproduce the gain.
