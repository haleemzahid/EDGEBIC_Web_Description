**To see a work center's load for next month in EDGEBIC by User Solutions, open the Resource Calendar or the machine's per-day capacity view and set the date range to the coming month.** Each day shows a stacked bar of Loaded, Free, and Over capacity hours, so you can read at a glance which days are full, which have room, and which are already over-promised.

Monthly totals lie by averaging. The value of a day-by-day load view is that it shows the peaks and gaps a monthly number hides. This page is how to read that forward load for one machine. For the numbers behind each bar, read [how EDGEBIC calculates work center capacity](/blog/how-edgebic-calculates-work-center-capacity).

## Before You Start

- **The plant has a current schedule.** Load only means something against a plan, so run **Drive Schedule** first if the plan is stale.
- **You know which machine you are checking** and the window you care about, such as the next four weeks.

## Step 1: Open the Load View

You have two routes to the same forward-load picture:

- On the work center's row, click the **📊** per-day capacity button to open the **Per-Day Capacity** dialog for that machine.
- Or open the **Resource Calendar** under **Schedule**, which shows load and capacity across machines.

## Step 2: Set the Range to Next Month

Set the **From:** and **To:** dates to cover the coming month. The view lists every day in the range with its capacity and its committed load.

## Step 3: Read the Daily Bars

Each day shows a stacked bar and a set of figures:

| Reading | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Loaded** (blue) | Hours already committed to the machine that day |
| **Free** (green) | Capacity still available |
| **Over capacity** (red) | Committed hours beyond what the machine can offer |

The summary card totals the range: total days, total capacity, loaded, free, and over capacity. Read down the month for the shape of the load, not just the total.

## Step 4: Read Backlog for Forward Remaining Work

The Resource Calendar also offers a **Backlog** reading: the forward sum of remaining hours per machine per day. A long job burning down over several days reads as a decreasing remaining total rather than repeating its full size on every day it touches. Backlog answers how much work is still waiting from here forward, which complements the loaded-versus-capacity picture.

## What This Tells You (and What It Does Not Change)

The load view is read-only. It shows where a machine is tight, has room, or is over-promised, so you can decide whether to act. It does not move any work. To change the plan you would adjust capacity, reroute steps, or rerun **Drive Schedule**; the load view is where you decide whether you need to.

## How to Read It Well

1. Scan for red days. Over-capacity days are where the plant will miss, and they hide inside a comfortable monthly total.
2. Compare weeks. A machine that is jammed for three weeks and idle for one has a leveling problem, not a capacity problem.
3. Before promising new work, check the specific week it would land in, not the monthly average.
4. Drill into a heavy day if you need the job list behind the number; the load cell opens the detail of what makes up the hours.

## Common Mistakes

**Trusting the monthly total.** A balanced month can hide weekly overloads. Read the load day by day and week by week.

**Reading load without a fresh plan.** Load is computed against the current schedule. If the plan is stale, rerun **Drive Schedule** before you trust the bars.

**Confusing loaded with backlog.** Loaded is committed hours on a day; backlog is remaining work summed forward. Use loaded to see how full a day is, backlog to see how much is still queued.

**Treating the view as an action.** Looking ahead changes nothing. Adjust capacity or rerun the schedule to actually move work.
