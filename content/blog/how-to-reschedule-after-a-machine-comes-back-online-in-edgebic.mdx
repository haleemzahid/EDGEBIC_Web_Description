**To reschedule after a machine comes back online in EDGEBIC, first restore the work center's capacity by resetting its per-day override or re-checking Is Available, then tick the affected jobs and press Schedule + Re-Schedule. EDGEBIC by User Solutions reflows the remaining work onto the recovered machine while completed and in-progress work stays put.** Restoring capacity and rescheduling are two separate steps; the plan does not update itself.

This is the recovery procedure. For the wider downtime story, see the [machine breakdown reschedule walkthrough](/blog/machine-breakdown-reschedule-walkthrough-edgebic); for the reverse action, [how to block a machine for a maintenance day](/blog/how-to-block-a-machine-for-a-maintenance-day-in-edgebic) shows how the machine came out of the plan. It is one of the task guides in the [EDGEBIC how-to hub](/blog/edgebic-how-to-hub).

## Before You Start

| Prerequisite | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| You know how the machine was taken out | You reset the same lever you used: a per-day override, or the Is Available flag. |
| Actuals on running jobs are logged | Started and completed work is locked by actuals, so the reflow only touches remaining work. |
| You know which jobs were displaced | Their rows are the scope of the recovery reschedule. |

## Step One: Restore the Machine's Capacity

Match how the machine was removed.

### If you used a per-day capacity override

1. On the work center's row, click the per-day capacity button (tooltip **Per-day capacity**), or in the Resource Calendar double-click the machine's Total Capacity cell for that date.
2. Find the day you had set to zero or a reduced value.
3. Click **Reset** on that row. The tooltip reads "Remove the override and revert to the shift default." The day returns to its normal capacity.
4. Click **Close**.

### If you marked the work center unavailable

1. Open the **Work Center** editor for the machine.
2. Re-check **Is Available**. An unavailable work center is skipped by the scheduler entirely.
3. Confirm **Effective Date** is not set to a future date, which would keep the machine out of the plan.
4. Save.

## Step Two: Reschedule the Affected Jobs

1. On the **Drive Schedule** tab, tick only the jobs that were pushed off the machine.
2. Press **Schedule + Re-Schedule**.
3. Read the **Confirm Scheduling** dialog, answer **Yes**, and let the pass complete.

The not-started operations reflow onto the recovered capacity. If the machine came back early, those operations can pull earlier, improving finishes. Started and completed operations do not move.

## What Changes When You Run

| Surface | Effect |
|---|---|
| The recovered machine | Its restored hours become available and the scheduler places work there again. |
| Not-started operations on affected jobs | Reflow onto the recovered capacity, possibly earlier. |
| Started and completed operations | Untouched; recorded work is never moved. |
| Unselected jobs | Keep their plans and reserved capacity. |
| Days Late on recovered jobs | Should improve if the machine returned earlier than planned. |

## How to Check It Worked

1. Open the machine's capacity view and confirm the recovered day now shows normal capacity, not zero.
2. On the Gantt, confirm the affected jobs now have operations back on the recovered machine.
3. Read **Days Late** on the recovered jobs and confirm it moved the way you expected.
4. Confirm no untouched job changed by comparing one unselected job's dates before and after.

## A Worked Recovery

A mill was booked out of the plan on Tuesday for a spindle repair. You had blocked it with a per-day capacity override of zero on that date, and every job that needed the mill Tuesday was pushed to Wednesday and later. The repair then finishes Monday evening, a full day early.

Open the mill's per-day capacity dialog, find Tuesday, and click Reset. The day reverts to its shift default, so the mill offers its normal hours again. Nothing has moved yet, because restoring capacity and rescheduling are two separate steps.

Now tick the jobs that were displaced off the mill and press Schedule + Re-Schedule. Their not-started mill operations reflow onto Tuesday's recovered hours, and because the capacity returned a day sooner than planned, several finishes pull back in. A job that read three days late may now read one, and one that was projected late may recover entirely.

Check the result three ways: the mill's capacity view shows Tuesday full again, the affected jobs have operations back on the mill, and their Days Late improved. Jobs you did not select stayed exactly where they were, so the recovery cost the rest of the plant nothing.

## Common Mistakes and Gotchas

- **Restoring capacity but not rescheduling.** The old plan that routed around the machine stands until you run. Reset the capacity, then reschedule the affected jobs.
- **Resetting the wrong lever.** If you blocked the machine with an override, re-checking Is Available will not help, and vice versa. Undo the same lever you used.
- **A future Effective Date.** If the work center's Effective Date is ahead of the recovered day, the scheduler still treats it as not yet commissioned. Confirm the date.
- **Leaving the override at zero.** Typing a new number is not the same as Reset. Use Reset to return the day cleanly to the shift default.
- **Rescheduling everything.** You only need the displaced jobs. A targeted pass keeps the rest of the plant stable.
