**A material step is meant to be instant: it represents consuming a component, not running a machine, so it should carry no work center and no hours. When one shows labor hours or tracked time, a code path stamped hours onto a schedule that should have stayed material-shaped.** [EDGEBIC by User Solutions](/edgebic) flags this with a specific consistency check, and the fix is nearly always a fresh scheduling run.

This post is part of the [EDGEBIC troubleshooting guide](/blog/edgebic-troubleshooting-guide). It explains what a correct material step looks like, why hours on one are a defect, and how to clear it.

## What a Material Step Represents

Not every routing step runs on a machine. A material step marks a point where a component is consumed into the job. It draws stock from the [inventory ledger](/blog/what-is-an-inventory-ledger) rather than occupying capacity, which is why it is modeled as instant: consuming a part takes no shift hours. In the scheduling model, a material step has no machine, no capacity allocation, and no tracked hours. It schedules with zero duration.

That is the contract the anomaly check enforces. A material step with labor hours, machine allocations, or a non-trivial tracked-hours value has broken it.

## Correct Versus Broken

| Attribute | Correct material step | Broken material step |
|---|---|---|
| Work center | None | A machine assigned |
| Capacity allocation | None | Hours booked on an instance |
| Tracked hours | Empty or zero | A non-trivial value |
| Duration | Instant | A window with real length |

An empty or zero tracked-hours value is harmless and does not trigger the check. The finding fires only on a genuinely non-trivial value or real machine allocations attached to the step. So a material step showing an empty tracked field is correct, not a defect, and worth confirming before you treat it as a problem.

## Why Hours on a Material Step Matter

A material step that carries hours is not just cosmetic. It implies capacity was consumed where none should have been, which inflates the machine's apparent load and can distort the job's promised finish. Because [scheduling and inventory stay in step](/blog/how-a-scheduler-nets-demand-against-inventory) through these material markers, a material step that behaves like a machine step muddies both the capacity picture and the consumption picture. That is why the check treats it as a consistency problem worth surfacing rather than ignoring.

## The Fix

1. **Run the anomaly report for the job** and confirm the material-step finding. Its detail names the step and shows the hours or tracked value that should not be there.
2. **Re-run scheduling for the job.** The engine emits material steps through a separate path that never creates machine allocations, so a fresh run regenerates the step in its correct instant shape.
3. **Re-run the anomaly report.** A clean result confirms the material step is back to zero duration and no hours.

If the finding survives a clean reschedule, that points at a write path stamping hours onto a material schedule, which is worth a support ticket with the anomaly detail attached. A surviving value is not something to patch by hand, because the next scheduling run would only reintroduce it if the underlying path is wrong.
