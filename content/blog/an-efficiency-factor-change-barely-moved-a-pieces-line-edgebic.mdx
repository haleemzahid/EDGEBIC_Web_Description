**The efficiency factor on a pieces-based work center is a multiplier clamped to a fixed range, so a value set past the ceiling or below the floor is capped before the scheduler uses it, which is why an aggressive factor change barely moves the plan.** [EDGEBIC by User Solutions](/edgebic) computes the effective pieces per hour as the base rate times this bounded factor, and the bound is deliberate.

This post lives in the [EDGEBIC troubleshooting guide](/blog/edgebic-troubleshooting-guide) and pairs with [pieces-based versus hours-based capacity](/blog/pieces-based-vs-hours-based-capacity-edgebic) and the wider question of [what is production scheduling](/blog/what-is-production-scheduling).

## What You Are Seeing

You have a pieces-based work center, and you changed the efficiency factor to reflect a line running much faster (or much slower) than its rated speed. You expected the schedule to react in proportion: a factor of three should roughly third the run time, a factor of a twentieth should stretch it out. Instead the durations moved only a little, as if the scheduler ignored most of your change. The factor field accepted the number, but the plan did not follow it.

## Why It Happens

The efficiency factor is a bounded multiplier, and the bound is the whole story.

- **Effective rate is base rate times factor.** The scheduler sizes a pieces operation from the effective pieces per hour, which is the work center's base pieces-per-hour rate multiplied by the efficiency factor.
- **The factor is clamped to a range.** That factor is held between a floor near one tenth and a ceiling near two. Values outside the band are pulled back to the nearest edge before the multiplication happens.
- **An out-of-range value is silently capped.** Set the factor to three and the scheduler uses the ceiling, so you get roughly double the base rate, not triple. Set it to a twentieth and the scheduler uses the floor, so the slowdown stops well short of what you asked. The field kept your number, but the calculation used the capped one.

The clamp exists on purpose: it keeps a single mistyped factor from driving a line to an impossible output rate that would corrupt the whole plan. The trade-off is that the factor is for trimming, not for large speed corrections.

## How to Fix It

Decide whether you are trimming or correcting, and use the right lever.

1. **Keep the factor inside its band for fine adjustments.** A worn machine at ninety percent, a fast cell at one hundred and thirty percent: these sit comfortably inside the range and behave exactly as entered.
2. **Move large speed differences to the base rate.** If the line genuinely runs at triple or a fraction of its rated speed, that belongs in the base pieces-per-hour rate, which has no clamp. Set the base rate to the real observed output.
3. **Re-run scheduling and check one operation.** The duration should be pieces required divided by (base rate times the factor actually used). If it still looks capped, your factor is outside the band and the base rate is the lever to move.
4. **Reserve the factor for what it is for.** Per-instance and machine-to-machine speed differences within a normal band are the factor's job. Wholesale rate corrections are the base rate's job.

## How to Prevent It

- **Set the base rate to reality first.** Get the base pieces-per-hour rate matching the line's real output before you touch the factor, so the factor only ever carries a small, in-band adjustment.
- **Treat a factor above two or below a tenth as a signal.** If you find yourself reaching past the band, that is the base rate telling you it is wrong. Fix the base rate instead.
- **Do not confuse this with utilization.** The efficiency factor scales output rate; utilization scales how many shift hours a work center offers. They are different dials, described in [pieces-based versus hours-based capacity](/blog/pieces-based-vs-hours-based-capacity-edgebic).
- **Spot-check after any factor edit.** Confirm the effective rate on one operation matches base rate times the factor you expect. A capped value shows up immediately as an effective rate that stopped at an edge.

If your pieces work center is scheduling in run hours rather than by output at all, that is a different symptom covered in [a pieces work center scheduled in hours](/blog/a-pieces-work-center-scheduled-in-hours-edgebic).
