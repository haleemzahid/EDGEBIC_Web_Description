**To put a job on hold in EDGEBIC, keep it out of your scheduling runs and clear its schedule so the capacity it was holding goes back to the plant.** There is no button that pauses a job while leaving it on the plan, because a paused job that keeps its work center reservations is worse than useless: it blocks machine hours nobody is going to use. EDGEBIC by User Solutions handles a hold as two plain acts, releasing the plan and controlling the run scope.

This is a task post. For the broader run mechanics it depends on, read [how to run the scheduler in EDGEBIC](/blog/how-to-run-the-scheduler-in-edgebic), and browse the rest at the [EDGEBIC how-to hub](/blog/edgebic-how-to-hub).

## Before You Start

- The job exists on the **Drive Schedule** grid, whether it is scheduled or not.
- You know whether operators have logged work on it. Check the **Actuals Logged?** column.
- You understand the scope rule: ticked rows define what a run touches, and unticked jobs keep their plans.

## The Steps

1. **Open the Schedule area** and select the **Drive Schedule** tab.
2. **Tick the Select checkbox on the held job only.** Untick everything else. This scope matters for the next step.
3. **Click Clear Schedule** on the filter bar.
4. **Read the scoped confirmation.** With rows ticked, only those jobs' schedules are cleared. With nothing ticked, the prompt asks whether to clear all schedules, which is not what you want here.
5. **Confirm.** The job returns to the **Unscheduled** badge, its **Schedule Details** goes back to "Not scheduled yet", and its **Completed?** flag is reset if it had been ticked.
6. **Leave it unticked from now on.** Every future run you make with specific rows ticked will pass it by.

If you want the hold visible to colleagues rather than implied, note it in the **Assigned To** field or record the reason in the job's audit history so the next planner does not schedule it back in by accident.

## What Changes When You Save

| Surface | Effect of clearing a job's schedule |
|---|---|
| The order | Untouched. Product, quantity, due date, priority and direction all survive. |
| The plan | The job's operations are removed. Its bars disappear from the Gantt. |
| Work center capacity | The hours the job was holding are released and available to other jobs on the next run. |
| Recorded work | Actual start and end dates that operators logged remain on the job. |
| **Completed?** | Reset for cleared jobs. |
| Other jobs | Untouched. Only the ticked rows are cleared. |

The released capacity is the reason to clear rather than simply avoiding the job in your runs. Leave the plan in place and the machine hours stay reserved: the plan says the mill is busy Tuesday, so nothing else gets planned there, and Tuesday is spent on nothing at all.

## How to Check It Worked

Look for three things on the row: the **Unscheduled** badge in the **Status** column, "Not scheduled yet" under **Schedule Details**, and empty **Item Start** and **Job End** columns. Then open the **Job View** tab or the Gantt and confirm the job's bars are gone.

To confirm the capacity actually came back, run the scheduler for the jobs that were queued behind it on the constrained work center. If they now start earlier, the hold released real hours.

## Common Mistakes

**Deleting the order instead of clearing its schedule.** Deleting is permanent and takes the job's production schedules with it, and the confirmation says so. A hold is temporary by definition. Always clear.

**Clearing with nothing ticked.** With no rows selected, **Clear Schedule** asks whether to clear all schedules and wipes the entire plan if you say yes. Tick the one row first and read the prompt before confirming.

**Answering yes to Schedule All Orders afterwards.** That prompt only appears when no rows are ticked, and it sweeps every order into the run including the one you held. Tick specific rows before you press the schedule button, or answer no.

**Assuming a hold protects the recorded work from a later reschedule.** It does not need to. Completed and in-progress operations are never moved by a reschedule in the first place, and clearing a schedule does not delete the actual dates. When the job comes back, the engine resumes from where the floor got to.

## Bringing the Job Back

Tick the row, run the scheduler, and confirm the job counts in the dialog. The job is planned against the plant as it stands on the day you release it, not as it stood when you paused it, which is the right answer: two weeks of other work has been booked in the meantime. Check **Days Late** afterwards, because a paused job usually comes back with less runway than it left with.
