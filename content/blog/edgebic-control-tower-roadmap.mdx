**The EDGEBIC Control Tower is a documented roadmap vision, not a shipped feature: there is no control tower screen in EDGEBIC today, and this article describes a future direction rather than a capability you can buy or turn on now.** [EDGEBIC by User Solutions](/edgebic) is deliberate about the line between what exists and what is planned. The Control Tower is squarely on the planned side. What makes it worth describing honestly is that the data it would draw on is already real, captured, and usable through the surfaces that ship today.

We are labeling this clearly on purpose. It is easy for roadmap ideas to blur into product claims, and that blur is exactly what a careful buyer should distrust. So here is the vision, what is real underneath it, and what you actually use in the meantime.

## What the Control Tower Vision Is

The idea is a single screen built around three verbs: **See, Log, Run.** See the live state of the floor at a glance. Log what is happening on it. Run the next corrective action without hunting through menus. It is an overview that would link out to the focused views that already exist, not a replacement for them.

In the vision, the top of the screen is a pulse strip: the percentage of jobs on schedule, at risk, and late, plus the current constraint work center. A center panel shows the floor and its load. A side rail lists the problems worth acting on. And a time slider lets you drag backward to see how the plan drifted, or forward into the current plan, with a translucent what-if overlay for testing a change.

That is the picture. Now the honest part: none of it is a screen you can open in [EDGEBIC](/blog/edgebic-complete-guide) today.

## What Is Real Underneath It

The reason the vision is credible, rather than a mockup with nothing behind it, is that EDGEBIC already captures the data each piece would need. These capabilities ship today and are usable now through existing surfaces.

**An append-only change history.** Every reschedule, work-center swap, date override, completion, and reopen is recorded as an event, with a timestamp, the person who made it, and a reason where one is required. This history is never pruned by the application. It is the raw material a future replay would read, and today it is your audit trail of how a plan evolved.

**Original dates kept separate from actual dates.** Every scheduled operation carries its original planned start and end, its current planned start and end, and its actual start and end once work is logged. The original dates are set once and never overwritten. That is what makes it possible, in principle, to show planned versus actual drift, and today it is what lets reports compare the plan to reality.

**Integrity checks.** EDGEBIC runs a full set of schedule and data checks (inverted dates, orphaned rows, unexplained gaps, on-hand drift, and more) that the vision's exception rail would stream. Today you reach the same checks through the anomaly report and the [data snapshot](/blog/edgebic-data-snapshot-support-tool).

**What-if simulation.** EDGEBIC can produce a candidate schedule from a proposed change without committing it, then let you accept or discard the result. The tower vision would render that as a ghost overlay. Today you use it through quote simulation and scenario what-if. To be precise about scope: this is a planning what-if, a simulation you review and choose to keep or throw away. It is not an automated promise-to-customer engine.

## The Deferred-Apply Habit Is Already Here

One design principle from the vision is already the way EDGEBIC works: propose a change, review its effect, then commit. On the interactive schedule, dragging a job to a new time or work center marks it as a pending override in memory and shows a pending-changes indicator. Nothing is written until you apply. If you close without applying, the proposal is dropped. That "the planner is always in control, nothing commits by surprise" habit is not a tower feature. It is how the schedule already behaves, and any future tower would inherit it.

## A Worked Example of the Vision, Clearly Hypothetical

To make the idea concrete, here is how a morning would read on the envisioned screen. Treat this as an illustration of a future direction, not a description of a screen you can open.

At 08:00, the pulse strip would summarize four active jobs: one on schedule, two at risk, one late, with the heat-treat station shown as the constraint. A supervisor would know instantly to chase the late job and the two at-risk ones.

Later, a planner curious about a change would toggle what-if and add a night shift to the heat-treat station for the week. The screen would render translucent candidate bars over the committed plan: a job that finished Thursday afternoon now finishing Wednesday morning instead. The planner would compare solid bars against translucent ones and either commit or discard.

Every number in that scene comes from data EDGEBIC already keeps: the pulse from schedule and order status, the constraint from the bottleneck flag, the what-if from simulation. The screen that merges them is the part that does not exist yet.

## Honest Roadmap Boundaries

Some elements of the vision depend on capabilities that are themselves still ahead on the roadmap, and it would be wrong to imply otherwise:

- Coloring the floor by operator or skill would depend on deeper operator scheduling work.
- Live buffer-health zones would depend on a formal critical-chain buffer model.
- Marking a machine down and having only the affected jobs reschedule automatically would depend on a targeted-replan trigger.
- A live sensor or machine-signal overlay would depend on shop-floor equipment integration.

These are named here so the vision is not oversold. The tower is a direction, and parts of that direction are further out than others.

## What You Use Today Instead

Until a merged screen exists, you run the floor across a small set of focused, shipping surfaces:

- The interactive schedule and job views show the live plan and let you drag to reschedule with a review-and-commit step.
- The reports and dashboards summarize status, on-time delivery, utilization, and performance.
- The anomaly report surfaces the problems the tower's rail would list.
- Quote simulation and scenarios let you test a change before you keep it.

These are not placeholders waiting for the tower. They are the working tools of a real plant, and they carry the substance the tower would one day gather into one glance. If you are comparing scheduling tools, the right way to weigh EDGEBIC is on what it does now (finite capacity scheduling, [forward and backward scheduling](/blog/forward-vs-backward-scheduling), [bottleneck-aware planning](/blog/production-bottleneck-identification), actuals tracking, and reporting) with the Control Tower understood for exactly what it is: a documented direction, grounded in real data, not yet a screen you can open.
