**The work center utilization percentage in scheduling is a per-work-center setting that controls how much of a shift's raw hours the engine offers as schedulable capacity. It is multiplied into the capacity formula, so a work center at 80 percent with an eight-hour shift offers 6.4 schedulable hours.** It is the deliberate throttle that reserves part of every shift for breaks, minor delays, and urgent work, and it defaults to a conservative 50 percent.

This entry is part of the [EDGEBIC by User Solutions](/edgebic) glossary series; for the wider vocabulary of scheduling, see the [manufacturing glossary](/blog/manufacturing-glossary). This is a work-center-level throttle, distinct from the per-order [schedule-at-utilization setting](/blog/what-is-the-schedule-at-utilization-setting), which limits how much of a machine a single order may consume.

## How the Utilization Percentage Works

Effective capacity is not the raw shift length. The engine computes it as shift hours multiplied by the number of machine instances multiplied by the utilization percentage. The utilization figure is the factor that scales raw availability down to the hours you actually expect a work center to deliver.

The reason to scale it down is that no shift is 100 percent productive. Operators take breaks, small delays accumulate, and a plant usually wants a margin for the urgent order that always seems to arrive. Setting the utilization percentage to a realistic figure bakes that reality into every schedule, so the engine never books a work center to hours it cannot honestly deliver.

Two points are worth stressing. First, the default is 50 percent, which is deliberately conservative and halves effective capacity until a planner raises it. Leaving it untouched is one of the most common reasons a schedule looks half-empty. Second, this is the operative field: a separate efficiency value is stored and shown, but it does not feed the capacity formula. If you want to change schedulable hours, change utilization.

## A Concrete Example

Take a press on a single eight-hour day shift. At the default utilization of 50 percent, the engine offers four schedulable hours a day, because eight times one instance times one-half is four. If the shop actually runs that press for closer to six of its eight hours, the schedule will look artificially starved, and jobs will queue behind capacity that exists on the floor but was never offered to the engine.

Raise the utilization to 75 percent and the same press now offers six schedulable hours: eight times 0.75. The extra two hours a day are real capacity that was being withheld by the conservative default. Conversely, a plant that wants to protect a fifth of the press for rush jobs sets the figure to 80 percent, and the engine permanently leaves 20 percent of every shift unbooked and available.

The utilization percentage did all of this at the work-center level, once, without touching a single order.

## How EDGEBIC Uses It

The utilization percentage is a work-center field that flows straight into the capacity calculation the engine and the reports share.

- **The capacity formula** multiplies shift hours by instances by the utilization percentage, so the figure directly determines how many hours the scheduler may place on the work center.
- **The conservative default** of 50 percent means a new work center offers only half its raw hours until reviewed, which is a setting to raise deliberately.
- **It is the operative throttle**, not efficiency. Efficiency is stored and displayed but does not enter the capacity calculation, so utilization is where you tune schedulable time.

The hours the utilization percentage leaves available become the pool the engine draws from, described in [available capacity in scheduling](/blog/what-is-available-capacity-in-scheduling). To see how the resulting booked hours are reported against that available pool, read [capacity utilization in scheduling](/blog/what-is-capacity-utilization-in-scheduling).
