**To add an alternate work center to a step in EDGEBIC by User Solutions you open the step's True Alternatives tab, add the fallback machine, and enter its own setup and hours.** The scheduler then treats that machine as a substitute it may pick instead of the primary, which is how a routing dodges a bottleneck without you rerouting jobs by hand.

An alternate replaces the primary. A parallel machine runs alongside it. Getting that distinction right is the whole game, and it is covered from the concept side in [parallel and alternate work centers explained](/blog/edgebic-parallel-work-centers-explained). This post is the setup task for the alternate.

## Before You Start

- The alternate machine exists as a work center, and it has shifts assigned. A machine with no shift returns no capacity and the scheduler simply skips it.
- You have the alternate's own numbers. An older, slower mill has larger setup and per-unit hours, and you enter its figures, not the primary's.
- You know when substitution is appropriate. Alternates suit a specific operation with a specific fallback. For "any of these machines, plant-wide," a Work Center Group is the better model.

## Adding the Alternate

1. Open the **BOR** tab, select the routing in **Data Grid** mode, and select the step. Expand the step detail panel.
2. In the **Parallel Processing** section, open the **True Alternatives** tab.
3. Click **+ Add True Alternative**. In the **Select Work Center** popup, pick the fallback machine and click **OK**.
4. In the alternative's row, enter the machine's **own** numbers: **Setup Time** and **Hours Req'd** as they apply on that machine. The scheduler uses these directly and does not scale the primary's hours to fit.
5. Click **Accept** in the detail panel, then **Update Standard BOR**.

In the grid, the alternate appears as a child row under the step, marked with **⟳** and the tooltip *True Alternative Work Center*. In **Designer** mode the same thing is a node joined with a **True Alternative** connector (right-click a connector, **Connector Type**, **True Alternative**), and selecting it shows a *True Alternative Work Center* banner in **Properties**.

## Choosing How the Scheduler Picks

When you run a schedule that touches a step with true alternatives, the **Alternative Work Center Selection** dialog appears: *"One or more BOR steps have True Alternative work centers."* You have two top-level choices, and three strategies under the automatic one.

| Choice | Behavior |
|---|---|
| **Auto – system picks** | The scheduler decides per step, using the strategy you select |
| **Manual – I'll choose per step** | You set the machine for each listed step in the **Selected Work Center** column |

| Strategy (Auto) | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Earliest Completion** | Picks the machine that finishes soonest, weighing speed against existing queue. The fastest outcome wins |
| **Primary First** | Stays on the primary while it has same-day capacity; falls back only when the primary is fully booked today |
| **Earliest Start** | Prefers whichever machine can begin soonest, so same-day availability beats a faster machine that can only start tomorrow |

Which strategy fits depends on why you added the alternate. **Earliest Completion** suits a shop where hitting dates is everything and you do not care which mill runs the job, because it always takes the fastest finish. **Primary First** suits a shop with a preferred machine, perhaps the one with the best fixturing, that falls back only under real pressure. **Earliest Start** suits work where getting parts moving matters more than the exact finish, for instance to free a queue or start a downstream crew. You can also drop to **Manual** and set the machine per step yourself when the call is judgment rather than arithmetic.

## What Changes When You Save

| Surface | Effect |
|---|---|
| New manufacturing orders | The selection dialog appears; steps may book onto the alternate per your strategy |
| Steps with recorded actuals | Locked to the machine that ran them; never swapped |
| Jobs already scheduled | Unchanged until rescheduled, and each keeps its own routing copy |
| The Gantt | The operation lands on one machine, chosen at schedule time |

Worked through: the primary mill is booked for three days, and a 20% slower second mill is free tomorrow. Waiting for the primary starts the operation Thursday at 51.0 hours. Under **Earliest Completion**, the alternate starts Wednesday at 1.0 setup plus 0.60 times 100, which is 61.0 hours, and finishes about a day and a half earlier despite being slower. Availability beat raw speed, which is exactly the trade the alternate exists to make.

## How to Check It Worked

In the grid, the alternate should show as a **⟳** child row under its step with its own setup and hours. If those cells are empty, the scheduler has nothing to plan the alternate with.

Run a test schedule and watch for the **Alternative Work Center Selection** dialog. It appearing at all confirms the alternate registered. Choose **Auto** with **Earliest Completion**, then read the Job View Gantt: the operation should sit on whichever machine the strategy chose, as a single bar rather than two.

If the alternate should have been used but was not, the usual causes are the **Primary First** strategy while the primary still had same-day room, or a step with actuals that is locked to its machine.

## Common Mistakes

**Entering the primary's hours on the alternate.** The alternate uses its own **Setup Time** and **Hours Req'd**. A slower machine with the fast machine's numbers plans as if it were fast, and the plan lies.

**Setting a factor and expecting it to matter.** A true alternative is driven by its own hours, not by a parallel factor. Factors apply to parallel machines, which are a different mechanism: see [how to make two operations run in parallel](/blog/how-to-make-two-operations-run-in-parallel-in-edgebic).

**Adding an alternate with no shift.** A machine with no shift assigned returns no capacity and is silently skipped. Confirm the alternate has a working calendar first.

**Confusing alternates with pools.** Hand-configuring the same alternate on every step across a shop is the wrong tool for "any mill will do." A Work Center Group models a pool once, and per-step alternates are for a specific fallback on a specific operation.

**Expecting a mid-run job to move.** Steps with actuals are locked to their machine. The alternate is a candidate only for work that has not started.
