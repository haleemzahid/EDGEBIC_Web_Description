**To set partial-confirm behavior in EDGEBIC, open Settings and then the Schedule tab, find the Partial-Confirm Behavior group, and choose how a short confirm is handled: trust the operator's Actual End, forward-shift the remaining hours, or trust the stamp except on flagged steps; then click Save.** In EDGEBIC by User Solutions a short confirm is when an operator marks an operation finished but logged fewer hours than planned, and this policy decides whether the next reschedule writes off the gap or re-plans it, all without ever moving completed work.

This post covers one policy setting. For the whole Settings and Schedule screen, see [how to configure scheduling policy in EDGEBIC](/blog/how-to-configure-scheduling-policy-in-edgebic); the full task library is at the [EDGEBIC how-to hub](/blog/edgebic-how-to-hub).

## Before You Start

- You can reach the **Settings** tab; it is permission-controlled.
- You know how your shop treats a close-out: is the operator's finish the ground truth, or should unfinished hours stay in the plan?
- You understand this affects future work only. Completed operations never move on a reschedule.

## The Steps

1. **Open the Schedule policy tab.** From the main navigation open **Settings**, then the **Schedule** sub-tab.
2. **Find the Partial-Confirm Behavior group.** Scroll to the group of that name.
3. **Choose the option.** Pick one:
   - **Trust the operator's Actual End** writes off the missing hours; the finish stamp is final.
   - **Forward-shift the remaining hours** (default) re-plans the gap on a future slot; downstream steps queue behind it.
   - **Trust Actual End unless the step is flagged short** trusts the stamp everywhere except on routing steps flagged for strict tracking.
4. **Save.** Click **Save**. The next scheduler run uses the new policy; there is no restart.
5. **Run the scheduler** and watch how a short-confirmed operation's gap is handled downstream.

## What Changes When You Save

| Surface | Effect |
|---|---|
| Trust the operator's Actual End | The finish stamp is final; missing hours are written off, pulling later work earlier. |
| Forward-shift the remaining hours | The planned-minus-logged gap is re-planned on a future slot; downstream steps queue behind it. |
| Flagged variant | Strict forward-shift only on steps you flag; everything else trusts the stamp. |
| Completed operations | Never move, under any option. |
| Stored data | None. Only the next reschedule's placement of future work changes. |

## How to Check It Worked

Log a short confirm on a test operation, recording fewer hours than planned, then run the scheduler. With **Forward-shift the remaining hours**, the missing hours should reappear as future work and downstream steps should queue behind them. Switch to **Trust the operator's Actual End**, save, and re-run; the gap should now be written off and later work pulled earlier. If the same short confirm produces the two different downstream outcomes, the policy is taking effect.

## Common Mistakes

**Expecting completed work to move.** No option moves a completed operation. Partial-confirm behavior only decides how the gap from a short confirm is treated in future planning.

**Choosing trust when hours must stay visible.** If you trust the Actual End, the missing hours are written off and disappear from the plan. In a shop where unfinished work must stay tracked, forward-shift is the safer default.

**Forgetting to Save and run.** Policy is read at the next scheduler run. Change the option, click Save, then run; nothing changes until you do.

**Flagging everything instead of a few steps.** The flagged variant is meant for a handful of critical operations. Flag broadly and you have effectively chosen forward-shift for everything, just less obviously.
